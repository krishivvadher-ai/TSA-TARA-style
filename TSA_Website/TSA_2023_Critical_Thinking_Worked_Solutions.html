1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer A
It may seem like we’re living in the age of the conspiracy theory, but ‘alternative facts’ have been around since long before conspiracy theorists claimed the 1969 moon landing was faked by a movie production crew.Context The truth is that society could be much smarter in how it addresses the popularity of such theories.Main conclusion While it may sound counter-intuitive, it’s actually not all that useful to directly challenge someone’s belief in a conspiracy theory.Intermediate conclusion That sort of intervention just tends to reinforce existing beliefs. More effective strategies focus on how information is delivered by scientists and other authorities and on developing open communication between the public and those providing ‘official’ or ‘expert’ information.Reason Finally, it’s critical not to simply dismiss conspiracy theories as trivial nonsense,Intermediate conclusion as they can reveal much about levels of trust, equality, and other key components of a functioning society.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ASociety could be much smarter in how it addresses the popularity of conspiracy theories.✓ “The truth is…” is the big claim. Everything after it lists ways society could be smarter: don’t challenge directly, deliver information better, don’t dismiss.
  2. BIt’s not all that useful to directly challenge someone’s belief in a conspiracy theory.✗ One of the ways society could be smarter, backed by its own reason. An intermediate conclusion.
  3. CChallenging someone’s belief in a conspiracy theory just tends to reinforce it.✗ The reason for B, so it sits even further down the argument.
  4. DBetter strategies for dealing with conspiracy theories focus on authorities’ information delivery and communication with the public.✗ Another of the supporting points, not the overall claim.
  5. EIt’s critical not to simply dismiss conspiracy theories as trivial nonsense.✗ The last supporting point (“Finally”), with its own “as…” reason.
Tip “The truth is that…” flags the claim. “Finally” tells you the rest is a list of points backing it up.

2(Q3 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer E
Religious believers often try to convince others to act morally by offering the reward of some kind of ‘heaven’.Context But this approach misunderstands the nature of morality.Main conclusion We should do the right thing because it is the right thing, not because of selfish desires about the consequences. Ethics is a matter of virtuous character, not seeking rewards and avoiding punishments.Reason For instance, if you believe it is right to pray daily then you should do so even if it will not get you into heaven.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AYou should not jump queues even if you are in a rush to get somewhere.✗ About resisting a temptation when you are in a hurry. No reward for doing right is involved.
  2. BYou should be honest about your friend’s new outfit even if you might offend them.✗ A clash between honesty and hurting a friend. The worry is a cost to them, not a reward for you.
  3. CYou should not steal from an organisation even if you believe that it is corrupt.✗ About whether the victim’s wrongdoing excuses you. Rewards play no part.
  4. DYou should not give to charity just to avoid feeling guilty.✗ Tells you not to do a good act. The principle says do the right thing anyway, whatever the payoff.
  5. EYou should return lost property even if the owner is not offering any compensation.✓ Returning lost property is the right thing, so do it even with no reward on offer. Just like praying even if it won’t get you into heaven.

Working: write the principle first

Heaven as a reward wrong reason to be moral pray if it is right, even if it won’t get you into heaven.
So the principle: do the right thing because it is right, even when there is no reward for you.

EReturning lost property is right → do it even with no compensation (no reward). ✓ Same principle, new setting.
ADon’t jump the queue even in a rush: about inconvenience, not reward. ✗
BBe honest even if it offends: a cost to someone else. ✗
CDon’t steal even from a corrupt firm: about excuses, not reward. ✗
DDon’t give to charity to dodge guilt: stops a good act instead of doing it for no reward. ✗
Tip The example in the passage is the template: “do the right thing even if it will not get you [a reward]”. E copies it.

3(Q4 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer E
When we are walking our centre of mass is constantly moving up and down. If we are wearing a backpack then this also moves up and down as we walk. A new prototype has been developed in which the backpack is suspended on sliding rails that allow it to move more smoothly.Context It is attached to a pair of rubber ropes on a pulley systemReason which acts like a car’s suspensionComparison to reduce the impact of the pack as you walk, making it easier to carry. Additionally, the relative movement between the pack and the body is used to power a small generator that converts the mechanical power into electricity.Reason Although these features will increase the price of the product,Opposing view this backpack is likely to become one of the most popular on the market.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. ACars that have better suspension systems tend to be more popular than other models.✗ The car is just a comparison to explain the suspension. Car sales are irrelevant.
  2. BPeople carrying this new type of backpack will be able to use the electricity as it is generated.✗ Not needed. The power could be stored for later, and comfort alone could sell it.
  3. CThe most important feature of a backpack is the ease with which it can be carried.✗ Too strong. Ease of carrying need not be the most important feature for it to sell well.
  4. DCurrent devices that can generate electricity as you walk do not create as much electricity as the new backpack will.✗ Not needed. No comparison with other devices is made.
  5. EConsumers will consider the benefits of the new backpack design to be worth the increase in price.✓ The price goes up, yet it will be popular. That only works if buyers think the comfort and the electricity are worth paying extra for.
Tip Negation test: if buyers think the benefits are NOT worth the higher price, it won’t be popular. The argument falls.

Unfortunately, in recent years, teachers in the UK have ‘gone soft’ and no longer have the right priorities.Intermediate conclusion Most schools these days are concerned with children’s happiness, confidence and self-expression rather than maximising academic achievement.Reason We should reverse this trend and re-introduce more traditional teaching methods (such as those focused on memorisation) in all schools.Main conclusion After all, the purpose of teaching is to develop children’s learning, and discipline and good exam results can get you into a top university and ensure a high-paying career later in life. Traditional methods are tried-and-tested,Reason so they are the best way of ensuring good academic outcomes for children.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ASome private schools have maintained a focus on traditional teaching methods despite changes in attitudes among education professionals.✗ Some schools keeping traditional methods says nothing about whether those methods work better.
  2. BTraditional teaching methods involve the use of strict punishment in schools to improve discipline, which some parents dislike.✗ Some parents disliking punishment is a side issue. It doesn’t show the methods fail to raise results.
  3. CModern technology has increased the range of teaching methods available.✗ More methods being available doesn’t show traditional ones are worse.
  4. DCareers have changed significantly over time and now require a wider range of skills than those represented by academic achievement.✓ The argument says exam results “ensure a high-paying career”. If careers now need wider skills, results alone won’t do that, and confidence and self-expression may matter after all.
  5. EAs part of a pilot programme, some schools have started allowing students to opt out of homework in order to provide a better ‘work–life balance’.✗ If anything, another example of the ‘soft’ trend the author dislikes. No results are given.
Tip Attack the link the argument leans on: good exam results → top university → high-paying career.

5(Q9 in the original paper) FlawAnswer E
Researchers believe that shortened sleep, and shortened rapid eye movement (REM) sleep in particular, contributes to a shorter lifespan. For every 5% reduction in REM sleep, mortality rates increase by 13% to 17% among older and middle-aged adults, they report.Opposing view But is there really a connection?Context A study comparing the sleeping patterns of men and women from 28 different countries using fitness trackers has shown that Japan is the country that sleeps the least. Japanese men and women sleep only an average of 6 hours and 30 minutes, and 6 hours and 40 minutes, respectively. Yet Japanese women have a life expectancy of 87.3 years – the second highest in the world after Hong Kong – while male life expectancy in Japan is the third highest internationally.Evidence / example What this shows is that shortened sleep does not, in fact, contribute to a shorter lifespan.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AHealth problems that shorten people’s lifespans might also interfere with their sleep in old age.✗ This would weaken the researchers’ claim, so it helps the author. It isn’t a flaw in the argument.
  2. BThe proportion of sleep that is REM sleep might decrease with age.✗ REM changing with age doesn’t touch the Japan evidence.
  3. CThe length of time that the Japanese sleep might be gradually increasing.✗ Even if sleep is creeping up, the Japanese sleep least now and still live long.
  4. DJapanese life expectancy might now be falling to a level closer to the global average.✗ Questions whether the data is up to date. The flaw is in the reasoning, not the figures.
  5. EThe life expectancy of Japanese people might be even longer if they slept for longer.✓ Other things (diet, healthcare) may keep the Japanese long-lived. Short sleep could still be cutting their lives, just from a very high starting point.
Tip One group doing well despite a risk factor doesn’t show the factor is harmless. Ask: would they do even better without it?

6(Q10 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
Every participant in the cross-country race received either a trophy for finishing in the top ten, or a medal for participating.Reason Amy finished in twelfth place,Evidence / example which means she will have been awarded a medal for participating.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AThe school can take in one hundred more pupils next year if they build more classrooms. They have not received approval for the building work, so they will not be able to increase their pupil intake.✗ An “if” rule with the condition missing. No either/or.
  2. BThe shopkeeper can keep her store open on the weekends if she hires people who can work weekend shifts. It is Sunday and the store is open, so she must have hired new employees.✗ An “if” rule run backwards: the store is open, so she must have hired. No either/or.
  3. CWhenever Cara and her family visit the zoo they have an ice cream at the outdoor café or – if it’s raining – visit the butterfly house. It wasn’t raining when they went yesterday, so they must have had an ice cream at the café.✓ Same shape: every time it is one of two things, the condition for one fails (not raining / not top ten), so it must be the other.
  4. DIf fireworks continue to be sold illegally, hospitals can expect a spike in fire-related injuries during the holiday season. There was a decrease in hospital admissions due to such injuries this year, so the efforts to crack down on illegal fireworks must be succeeding.✗ An “if” rule, then a jump to a cause (the crackdown). No either/or.
  5. EThere is only enough space left in the moving van for an armchair or a side table. The armchair has some sentimental value to Peter, so the side table will be left behind.✗ Two options, but one is picked for a reason (sentimental value). Nothing is ruled out by a rule.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: every time, either X (trophy, if top ten) or Y (medal) the condition for X fails (twelfth) so Y.

CEvery visit: either X (butterfly house, if raining) or Y (ice cream) → condition for X fails (not raining) → so Y. ✓ Same shape.
AIf build → more pupils. Can’t build → no more pupils. A single “if”, no either/or. ✗
BIf hires → can open. Open → must have hired. Backwards “if”. ✗
DIf illegal sales → spike. No spike → crackdown working. ✗
EArmchair or side table → armchair is precious → side table goes. A preference, not a failed condition. ✗
Tip Two parts to match: a rule that gives one of two outcomes, then “not this one, so that one”.

7(Q11 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
It is widely claimed that ancient stone circles were designed as astronomical observatories – that the stones were laid out to create sightlines to various celestial objects.Opposing view While it seems clear that solar and lunar alignments were important in the layout of many circles,Context there are problems when it comes to the stars.Intermediate conclusion Many stones are missing or have toppled over and it is extremely difficult to fix their original position. Further, the position of the stars in relation to each other is constantly changing.Reason Using the laws of physics, we can recreate a map of the heavens for any period in the past.Context But to draw conclusions about the orientation of any sightline, we have to know exactly when the stones were erected. Unfortunately, none of the dating methods used, including radiocarbon dating, is accurate enough for that. Finally, there are hundreds of combinations of stones at any site that produce sightlines.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AThe builders of the stone circles knew less about the stars than modern humans.✗ The builders’ knowledge is never compared with ours.
  2. BBetter dating methods would let us determine the importance of sightlines.✗ Better dating fixes only one problem. Missing stones and hundreds of sightlines remain.
  3. CAncient astronomical observations made using stone circles were inaccurate.✗ The passage says we can’t judge the sightlines, not that the builders got them wrong.
  4. DIt is unclear whether the stars were important in the layout of stone circles.✓ Three problems (stones moved, dates too vague, too many possible sightlines) mean we can’t tell if star sightlines were planned. So it is unclear.
  5. EThe sun and moon were more important to ancient humans than the stars.✗ Sun and moon mattered, but the passage doesn’t say stars mattered less. It says we can’t tell.
Tip Match the strength of the passage: “there are problems” leads to “unclear”, not to “no”.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
A cataract is a clouding of the lens inside the eye, which results in decreased vision and eventually blindness.Context Approximately half of all cases of blindness globally are cataract-related. Cataracts often lead to social exclusion, unemployment, depression and lower life expectancy.Evidence / example Treatment by surgical replacement of the affected lens offers great value for the immense benefits it brings not only to the individual but also to society as a whole.Main conclusion Cataract surgery is usually quite quick, does not require large, big-budget equipment or an overnight stay in hospital, and has a more than 90 per cent success rate.Evidence / example This medical intervention is relatively inexpensive and straightforward if the necessary infrastructure and expertise are in place.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AApproximately half of all cases of blindness globally are cataract-related.✗ Evidence of how big the problem is.
  2. BCataracts often lead to social exclusion, unemployment, depression and lower life expectancy.✗ Evidence of the harm cataracts cause, backing up the “immense benefits”.
  3. CSurgical cataract treatment offers great value for the immense benefits it brings not only to the individual but also to society as a whole.✓ “Great value” is the claim. The harm of cataracts shows the benefits; the quick, cheap surgery shows the low cost.
  4. DCataract surgery is usually quite quick, does not require large, big-budget equipment or an overnight stay in hospital, and has a more than 90 per cent success rate.✗ Evidence about the surgery, backing up the low cost.
  5. ECataract surgery is relatively inexpensive and straightforward if the necessary infrastructure and expertise are in place.✗ Sums up the surgery evidence (cheap, simple). A step towards “great value”, not the end point.
Tip “Great value” has two halves: big benefits and low cost. Every other sentence supplies one half.

9(Q16 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer B
Stora Karlsö is a Swedish island that is now set aside for wildlife conservation and is uninhabited by people except for the presence of ‘day-trippers’, who visit for only a few hours at a time. The population of murre seabirds there declined in the past because of people hunting them and collecting their eggs, but started to recover once these activities were banned.Context During one recent summer, Stora Karlsö was closed to the tourists, and scientists monitoring the island made a surprising discovery. The percentage of eggs hatched by the murres dropped by 26% as dozens more white-tailed eagles flocked to the island.Evidence / example It turns out that the eagles were encouraged by the lack of human visitors, while the murres were scared away from their nests by the influx of the larger birds.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AFocusing the efforts of a nature reserve on one particular species is not always an effective conservation strategy.✗ The reserve is never said to focus on one species.
  2. BThe presence of people has a beneficial effect on wildlife species’ populations in some cases.✓ With no visitors, eagles moved in and murre hatching fell 26%. So here, having people around helped the murres: a benefit “in some cases”.
  3. CSome kinds of bird species are less of a conservation priority than others.✗ Nothing ranks species by priority.
  4. DLimiting certain kinds of human activity is the fastest approach to increasing the population of a wildlife species.✗ “Fastest” is never compared, and here limiting visitors made things worse.
  5. EIt is not always possible to conserve every species in some kinds of ecosystems.✗ Too strong. With day-trippers back, both birds seem fine, so nothing shows it is impossible.
Tip One example can support a modest “in some cases” conclusion, but nothing stronger.

Calling a colleague ‘immature’ is not unlawful if they are behaving in a genuinely childish manner, a tribunal has ruled.Context In a recent case an employee sued the director of the company where he worked for age discrimination. However, the director successfully argued that her calling the employee ‘immature’ was prompted by his abrasive manner and inability to lead a team, rather than his relatively young age. The tribunal dismissed the claims of discrimination,Evidence / example so directors of companies should not be overly concerned about the legality of accusing their employees of being immature.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AAn abrasive manner and inability to lead a team may be an indicator of immaturity.✗ This backs the director’s side of the case. It isn’t a flaw in the argument.
  2. BThe fact that a tribunal has dismissed a case does not mean that the company in question acted ethically.✗ The conclusion is only about legality, not ethics.
  3. CThe tribunal may have examined the character of the director rather than what had actually happened.✗ Guesswork about the tribunal. The real gap is going from one case to all cases.
  4. DWhether or not something is lawful may not be the only concern that directors might have.✗ The conclusion is only about legality, so other concerns don’t touch it.
  5. EThe fact that one case has been dismissed does not mean that all future cases will be dismissed.✓ One case, decided on its own facts, is stretched to every accusation of immaturity. Next time the remark might really be about age.

11(Q21 in the original paper) StrengthenAnswer C
In recent years, the production and visibility of colourised and ‘restored’ historical photographs has grown exponentially, owing to both social media and the introduction of user-friendly colourisation software. But the expansion of the technical capacity and popular appetite to alter historical photography has not been matched by sufficient debate about the assumptions and consequences of this practice.Context Colourisers do not address photographs as physical artefacts with unique histories of creation and reception: they treat original photos as ‘windows’ onto the past that can be ‘improved’ for viewers. But a photograph is never just a neutral representation of the past. Every photograph carries with it a complicated history of who took it, who looked at it, how it was shaped by the technology behind it, and what all this means for questions of representation, experience, and history.Reason Colourisation blocks out this rich repository of historical meaning, substituting a fleeting jolt of novelty for deeper understanding of the image.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. AArtificial intelligence is now being used to colourise photographs automatically.✗ Only says how colouring is done, not that it hides meaning.
  2. BColourisation techniques are almost as old as photography itself.✗ Makes colouring part of photography’s own history. If anything this weakens.
  3. CSome photographers of the past chose to use black and white film instead of colour.✓ If black and white was a deliberate choice, it is part of the photo’s history of creation. Colouring it in wipes that choice out, just as the argument says.
  4. DSome universities no longer teach courses on the history of photography.✗ About university courses, not about what colourisation does to a photo.
  5. EColourisers rarely have academic experience of studying history.✗ An attack on who does it. The argument is about what colourisation does, whoever does it.
Tip Strengthen the last sentence: find the option showing that the lack of colour itself carries meaning.

12(Q22 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer C
There has been discontent amongst students following the decision of a number of UK universities to use automated marking software to assess students’ written work. Of course, there are ethical issues surrounding the use of such software.Context But a simple reason why the students are right to feel aggrieved is that it is not clear that they were told, when embarking on the course, that this would happen. You have every right to complain about something which affects you if you have not previously been informed, consulted, or given your consent.Reason The students are therefore justified in their complaints.Main conclusion

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AA customer has every right to demand a refund rather than accept an alternative product offered by a supplier.✗ A right to a refund, with nothing about not being told in advance.
  2. BSomeone selling their house ought to inform the buyer if they cannot guarantee the property will be left satisfactorily clean.✗ A duty to inform beforehand, not a right to complain after not being told.
  3. CA patient who has been prescribed a medication that has unpleasant side effects is entitled to complain if they were not warned of this possibility.✓ The side effects affect the patient, and nobody warned them. So, like the students, they are entitled to complain.
  4. DA consumer who is offered a no-returns purchase ought to be fully informed about the implications of this transaction.✗ Again a duty to inform, not a right to complain.
  5. EA citizen who is arrested for a suspected crime has every right to complain if they believe the police used unnecessary force.✗ A right to complain, but about force, not about being kept in the dark.

Working: write the principle first

Marking software affects students they weren’t clearly told so they may complain.
So the principle: if something affects you and you weren’t informed, consulted or asked, you have the right to complain.

CSide effects affect the patient → not warned → entitled to complain. ✓ Same principle.
ACan demand a refund: no “not informed” part. ✗
BSeller ought to inform: a duty to tell, not a right to complain. ✗
DConsumer ought to be informed: a duty to tell again. ✗
ECan complain about force: right to complain, wrong trigger. ✗
Tip Both halves must match: the trigger (not informed) and the result (a right to complain).

13(Q23 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
The chef will not go ahead with her cooking class tomorrow unless at least fifteen people sign up for it by 5 pm today.Reason It is now 4 pm and twenty people have already registered,Evidence / example so she will be teaching the class tomorrow.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AUnless enough rain fell during the summer months to water the crops, they would all have died out by July. It is now August and the crops are still surviving, so there was adequate rainfall earlier in the year.✗ Valid: the crops survived, so the needed rain must have fallen. Reasons from the result back.
  2. BThe architect’s plan cannot be approved by the local authorities unless it meets all of the relevant regulations. The plan will be rejected since the architect has disregarded two of the rules.✗ Valid: the needed condition is missing, so no approval.
  3. CUnless more public transport options are made available, traffic congestion in town will never improve. The mayor has announced that new bus lines will be introduced next month, so traffic will start to ease up after that.✓ Same flaw: Y is needed for X, Y is met, so X will happen. Fifteen sign-ups and more transport are needed, not enough on their own.
  4. DThe competition is not conducted fairly unless all of the judges are completely impartial. One of the judges is biased in favour of one of the competitors because they are from the same country, so that competitor is more likely to win.✗ Condition missing, then a new conclusion about who wins. Different shape.
  5. EUnless the data has been analysed accurately, it cannot be included in the final report. The report was handed in today and did not contain the data, so mistakes must have been made with its analysis.✗ The data is missing, so it must be inaccurate. A different flaw: it could be missing for another reason.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: not X (class) unless Y (15 sign-ups), so Y is needed for X Y is met (20) so X. Flaw: needed ≠ enough.

CNot X (traffic improves) unless Y (more transport) → Y is coming (bus lines) → so X. ✓ Same flaw.
AWithout Y (rain) not X (survive) → X happened → so Y. Valid. ✗
BNot X (approved) unless Y (rules met) → not Y → so not X. Valid. ✗
DNot X (fair) unless Y (impartial) → not Y → “more likely to win”. New conclusion. ✗
ENot X (included) unless Y (accurate) → not X → so not Y. Different flaw. ✗
Trap! “X unless Y” makes Y a must-have. Having Y does not guarantee X: the chef could still fall ill.

14(Q27 in the original paper) FlawAnswer B
When hiring new employees for senior positions, many companies focus their search on applicants who already have some previous experience working in a similar job.Context This happens even though simply having had another job does not automatically mean that that person performed well in that role. Moreover, some people got their jobs because they have personal connections with the employers, not because they were the most competent or suitable candidate. And even if an applicant is highly qualified and did excel at another job, the different work environments at different organisations often make it challenging for them to apply their previous work experience to a new role.Reason This shows that companies that automatically reject inexperienced applicants are taking the wrong approach.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the flaw in the above argument?

  1. ACompanies may be trying to save time by focusing on candidates who have previous work experience.✗ A possible motive for the companies. It doesn’t point to the gap in the reasoning.
  2. BIt may be that good performance, although not guaranteed by previous experience, is impossible without it.✓ Every reason shows experience is not enough for good performance. It may still be needed, and then rejecting the inexperienced makes sense.
  3. CJust because someone got a job through personal connections does not mean that they are unqualified for it.✗ The argument never says such people are unqualified, only maybe not the most competent.
  4. DInexperienced applicants may be able to access internships and other training opportunities.✗ Training elsewhere is beside the point. It doesn’t touch the reasoning.
  5. EJust because companies focus on applicants with previous work experience does not mean they do not consider other qualifications.✗ The conclusion is about firms that automatically reject the inexperienced. Other qualifications don’t change that.
Tip “Does not guarantee” (not enough) is different from “not needed”. Classic sufficient vs necessary mix-up.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
According to legend, the ancient cities of both Thebes and Troy were founded, not at the summit of a hill, nor the junction of two waterways, but where a cow decided to lie down.Context The practice of allowing cows to choose where a city should be built would seem to be arbitrary and bizarre;Opposing view a little reflection tells us that it is perhaps not so strange after all.Main conclusion Since a city means a sizeable population,Reason there must be plentiful sources of meat, milk, leather and cheese, not to mention strong draught animals for ploughing fields and pulling carts.Intermediate conclusion If a cow is taken enough by the amenities of a place to feel able to lie down, then it is worth paying attention.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AAccording to legend, both Thebes and Troy were founded where a cow chose to lie down.✗ Background: the legend.
  2. BThe practice of allowing cows to choose where a city should be built would seem to be arbitrary and bizarre.✗ The opposing view, which the author rejects.

16(Q29 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer E
