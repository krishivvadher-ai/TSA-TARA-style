1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer D
In the twenty-first century, many people aged over 65 are perfectly capable of working, and also are likely to live longer than individuals did in previous times. If people work after age 65, there is more chance that they will be financially self-sufficient when they do retire.Reason Compulsory retirement ages have long been banned in the USA,Evidence / example and we should get rid of them in the UK also.Main conclusion This would be good for individuals and for the country.Reason We should ignore those employers who complain that they would be unable to get rid of older and inefficient employees.Opposing view After all, those who are really incompetent to do a job can be dismissed on the grounds of merit, rather than of age.Reason

Which of the following expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AIt is unfair to prevent people working after age 65.✗ “Unfair” is never mentioned. The argument is about what is good for people and the country.
  2. BPeople who are incompetent to do a job can be dismissed.✗ A reason used to answer the employers’ complaint.
  3. CRaising the retirement age would be good for the country.✗ Raising the age is not the same as abolishing it, and “good for the country” is a reason.
  4. DThe UK should abolish compulsory retirement ages.✓ The “should” sentence: get rid of compulsory retirement ages in the UK. Everything else gives reasons or answers an objection.
  5. EThose who work beyond age 65 can be financially self-sufficient.✗ A reason (more chance of being self-sufficient), not the conclusion.
Tip Two “shoulds” appear. The second one (ignore the employers) is just batting away an objection.

2(Q3 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
The government blames schools and teachers for boys underperforming.Opposing view However, science tells a different story.Intermediate conclusion Evolutionary biology shows that females have evolved to have better verbal and emotional skills than males because of the need in prehistoric times for women to take the lead in child rearing. By contrast, the need for males in prehistoric times to hunt in packs for food has made males more prone to violence and also skilled at calculating and planning. Neurologists have added to this insight by showing that the male hormone testosterone has an adverse impact on language skills.Evidence / example So clearly differences in educational performance between boys and girls cannot be explained in terms of failing teachers.Main conclusion

Which of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt assumes that scientific explanations apply to the average male or female ignoring exceptions.✗ Exceptions don’t matter here: the argument is about average differences between boys and girls.
  2. BIt assumes that biological differences come in degrees and are not absolute.✗ The argument never relies on how biological differences are graded.
  3. CIt assumes that skills in calculating and planning have a role in educational performance.✗ Boys’ calculating skills would if anything help them, so the argument doesn’t need this.
  4. DIt assumes that the differences in performance between the sexes are due solely to biological differences.✓ Showing biology plays a part doesn’t show teachers play no part. It treats biology as the whole story.
  5. EIt assumes that teachers are not trying to improve the performance of failing boys.✗ It never says teachers aren’t trying. It says they aren’t to blame.
Tip “Cannot be explained in terms of…” rules a cause out. Ask: could both causes be at work?

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer B
The effect of shortening degree courses at universities from three years to two would be that students would have two-thirds as much time to think about their subject.Reason The result would be graduates whose understanding of their subject was shallower and whose intellectual development was much less extended.Intermediate conclusion Any measure that leads to a lowering of the quality of graduates should be resisted by universities.Reason

Which one of the following conclusions can reliably be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AUniversity graduates at present have an in-depth understanding of their subject.✗ It only says graduates would be shallower than now, not that they are deep now.
  2. BUniversities should oppose the shortening of degree courses to two years.✓ Shortening → shallower graduates (lower quality). Universities should resist anything that lowers quality. So they should oppose shortening.
  3. CUniversities are not proposing to reduce the length of degree courses to two years.✗ Nothing says who is or isn’t proposing it.
  4. DStudents should oppose moves by universities to shorten degree courses.✗ The rule is about what universities should resist, not students.
  5. EUniversities should adopt more rigorous standards for the award of degrees.✗ Standards for awarding degrees are never discussed.
Tip General rule (“any measure that…”) + a case that fits it = the conclusion.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer E
Many people suffer from depression in modern society.Context This can be alleviated through drugs such as Prozac, which alter the chemical balance of the brain. However, the individual can undergo psychotherapy, which involves talking through problems with a sympathetic and skilled fellow human being with a view to putting the subject in a more positive frame of mind.Reason Depressed individuals who do not like the idea of their brain chemistry being altered should therefore choose psychotherapy.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. APsychotherapy is more effective than drugs in treating depression.✗ Not needed. It only has to be an alternative, not a better one.
  2. BAlternative medicine is preferable to conventional medicine.✗ Too broad: alternative versus conventional medicine is never discussed.
  3. CPsychotherapy cannot be combined with drug treatments.✗ Not needed. Even if they could be combined, people who dislike drugs would just choose therapy.
  4. DDepression is caused by the pace of modern life.✗ The cause of depression doesn’t matter to the choice of treatment here.
  5. EPsychotherapy does not alter the individual's brain chemistry.✓ Psychotherapy only suits people who don’t want their brain chemistry altered if it doesn’t alter it.
Tip Negation test: if psychotherapy did change brain chemistry, the conclusion collapses.

5(Q9 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer E
Zoos are entirely unsuitable places for animals.Intermediate conclusion People visit zoos to learn about animal behaviour but the animals they see are likely to be behaving in abnormal and neurotic ways because of the cramped and unnatural conditions in which they are kept.Reason Zoos should be closed down and the money saved should be reallocated to the protection of natural habitats.Main conclusion

Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AHumans living in cramped conditions can also become neurotic.✗ Humans getting neurotic too says nothing in zoos’ favour. It backs up the cramped-conditions point.
  2. BSchoolchildren can learn a great deal about animals from visiting zoos.✗ Doesn’t answer the point that the behaviour they see is abnormal.
  3. CMany of the animals at present in zoos would not be capable of living in the wild.✗ Only a snag about rehoming today’s animals. It gives zoos no positive role.
  4. DThe protection of wildlife habitats is very costly.✗ Costly habitats make the saved money more useful, so it supports the plan.
  5. EZoos enable endangered species to survive by breeding them in captivity and then re-introducing them to the wild.✓ Zoos keep endangered species alive. That attacks “entirely unsuitable”, and closing them would hurt the very wildlife the money is meant to protect.
Tip The argument judges zoos only as places to learn. Weaken it by showing zoos do something else valuable.

6(Q10 in the original paper) FlawAnswer C
Observations of the brains of adult human subjects before and after periods of intense memory recall (for instance preparing for the exams taken by London taxi drivers testing their knowledge of London) have shown surprising results. When comparisons were made between brain scans taken at the start of their preparations and at the end, it was found that the parts of the brain responsible for memory had actually increased in size.Evidence / example This would seem to suggest that, just like a muscle, the brain increases in size and power the more it is used.Intermediate conclusion People who want to improve their overall IQ (Intelligence Quotent), therefore, should simply take a very large number of IQ tests!Main conclusion

Which of the following is the best expression of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. ALondon taxi drivers are not necessarily representative of the population as a whole.✗ A possible worry about the sample, but the big leap is from memory to IQ.
  2. BIt assumes that there is a single part of the brain that is responsible for one's IQ.✗ It never claims one part controls IQ. Its mistake is treating memory growth as IQ growth.
  3. CIt draws a general conclusion about intelligence from the particular example of memory.✓ The evidence is about memory areas growing with memory practice. It leaps to overall IQ growing with IQ tests.
  4. DIt does not state how many IQ tests constitutes a very large number.✗ Vagueness about the number is not a fault in the reasoning.
  5. EBrain size is not necessarily dependent on the extent of mental activity undertaken.✗ This disputes the evidence (the scans), not the step from evidence to conclusion.
Tip Check the evidence and the conclusion are about the same thing. Memory ≠ intelligence (and the muscle comparison hides the jump).

7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
There is widespread and justified concern about the reliance on expert opinion in law court cases.Intermediate conclusion Where experts disagree on an interpretation of the facts, there is always the possibility that the more charismatic and persuasive expert's opinion will prevail for this reason alone. Their reason for holding the opinions they do could be more to do with their own personal prejudices than their professional expertise.Reason However, if we want justice to be done, we should distinguish sharply between this and expert evidence which is not reliant on interpretation.Main conclusion It would be a tragedy if key advances in tackling crime such as matching DNA were regarded with suspicion just because such evidence came from an expert witness.Reason

Which of the following is a statement of the main conclusion in the above argument?

  1. AThe widespread concern about the use of expert opinion in legal cases is justified.✗ The author agrees, but it comes before the “However”. It sets up the main point.
  2. BIt would be most regrettable if evidence such as DNA became suspect in legal cases.✗ The DNA “tragedy” is a reason for making the distinction.
  3. CIn the interests of justice we should distinguish between expert evidence and expert opinion.✓ “However … we should distinguish” is the author’s recommendation. The concern is conceded first, and the DNA point backs the distinction.
  4. DExpert opinion may arise from personal views rather than professional knowledge.✗ A reason why the concern is justified.
  5. EJuries may be swayed by the personal characteristics of those presenting expert opinions.✗ A rewording of the charismatic-expert reason (and juries aren’t mentioned).
Tip A “However” after a concession often flags the main conclusion. “This” = expert opinion.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer A
A dishonest act, such as stealing money from a handbag or cheating in school, may arise for one of many different reasons. But the reasons may be consistent with the personality of the individual involved.Reason One child may steal, for example, in order to show off; another, to get money to support a hobby (or to get a birthday present for a younger sister); another, to express aggression or hatred against the person from whom he or she steals. One child may cheat in order to avoid punishment for academic failure; another, because of personal ambition; another to express contempt for the system.Evidence / example

Which one of the following is a conclusion that can be drawn from the passage above?

  1. AIt is wrong to assume that a child who is dishonest in one kind of situation will be dishonest in others.✓ Each dishonest act comes from a particular motive that fits the child. So dishonesty in one setting needn’t carry over to another.
  2. BA child who cheats does so for very complex reasons.✗ “Very complex” isn’t shown. Each child’s reason is simple; they just differ between children.
  3. CA child whose motive is generosity towards someone else should not be blamed for stealing.✗ Blame is never discussed. The passage explains motives, it doesn’t judge them.
  4. DUnderlying all dishonest acts is hostility towards others.✗ “All” is wrong: hostility is only one motive among several.
  5. EChildren who are neither exhibitionist nor aggressive nor acquisitive are unlikely to steal money.✗ The list is only examples (“for example”), and it leaves out generosity (the birthday present).
Tip One act, many motives: “dishonest” is not a single fixed trait.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer E
Unlike motorways in many European countries, British motorways were built with hard shoulders for use in a breakdown or emergency.Context The proposal to use the hard shoulders to relieve congestion during peak traffic hours should not be adopted.Main conclusion It would be inconvenient for emergency services because when the hard shoulders are in use, it would be difficult for emergency vehicles to reach an accident. Nor would it be likely to significantly reduce congestion because it would be difficult to remove obstructions caused by broken down vehicles, and in any case the volume of traffic is increasing massively each year. But the most important reason is that we should seek to maintain Britain's relatively good record of safety on motorways.Reason Other ways of relieving congestion must be sought.Main conclusion

Which of the following is an assumption underlying the above argument?

  1. ABritain's record for motorway safety is the best in Europe.✗ “The best in Europe” is too strong. The passage only says “relatively good”.
  2. BCongestion on British motorways is not a major problem.✗ It wants “other ways of relieving congestion”, so it treats congestion as a problem.
  3. CAccidents are more likely to happen when motorways are congested.✗ If anything this would support using hard shoulders to ease congestion.
  4. DChanging the design of British motorways will not solve the problem of congestion.✗ Too broad. It only says this proposal won’t help much.
  5. EBritain's good safety record for motorways is partly attributable to the presence of hard shoulders.✓ The “most important reason” is keeping the safety record. That only counts against the plan if hard shoulders help keep motorways safe.
Tip Negation test: if hard shoulders did nothing for safety, the most important reason vanishes.

10(Q17 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
"I tried a speed reading course and read 'War and Peace' in three hours. It's about Russia." Woody Allen's joke strikes a chord with many who doubt the effectiveness of speed reading techniques. These techniques usually involve moving your finger smoothly and quickly along lines of text and training your eyes to follow, so preventing your eyes going back over words you have already read, which is what we normally do.Context There have been reports of readers achieving incredible speeds using these techniques,Opposing view but such achievements are of limited benefit to many readers, particularly students,Main conclusion who have to gain a deep understanding of what they read.Reason

Which of the following, if true, would weaken the above argument?

  1. AFor many tasks a student has to do, speed reading can lead to a superficial understanding.✗ This supports the argument: speed reading gives shallow understanding.
  2. BSpeed reading techniques are more difficult to acquire the older you are.✗ Age has nothing to do with whether speed reading harms understanding.
  3. CA student, after reading the argument, commented, "I find I understand what I am reading better if I read quickly."✓ The argument assumes reading fast costs understanding. A student who understands better when reading fast cuts against that.
  4. DA student, after reading the argument, commented, "I use speed reading for easy texts. I have to read slowly if the content is difficult."✗ Fast for easy texts, slow for hard ones: this fits the argument.
  5. ESpeed readers have found they have difficulty in reading for pleasure (for example when reading novels or magazines).✗ Another limit of speed reading, so it supports the argument (and it is about pleasure, not study).
Tip Find the hidden claim (speed reading cuts understanding) and attack that.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
The most common excuse for the poor performance of English school children at learning foreign languages is the fact that English is so widely spoken in the world. Thus, when the English travel to another country, they feel little need to become proficient in the local language.Opposing view But that cannot explain why, amongst children who have never travelled outside their own country, the English are still out-performed by their overseas counterparts in foreign language acquisition.Reason One is led to the inevitable conclusion that English schools are deficient in the teaching of languages.Main conclusion

Which of the following is the best statement of the flaw in this argument?

  1. AIt does not take into account the low levels of funding in English schools.✗ Low funding would be one way schools fall short, so it fits the conclusion rather than exposing a flaw.
  2. BIt overlooks the fact that other languages, for example Spanish, are also widely spoken in the world.✗ Other widely spoken languages don’t explain why English children do worse.
  3. CIt is based upon evidence taken from an unrepresentative sample: those children who have not travelled abroad.✗ Using non-travellers is deliberate: it fairly rules out the travel excuse.
  4. DIt reaches its conclusion without considering other possible explanations for poor performance in languages.✓ Ruling out one excuse (travel) doesn’t leave schools as the only cause. Motivation or exposure at home could explain it too.
  5. EIt fails to offer any proof that the English are poor at learning foreign languages.✗ Poor performance is the shared starting point. The flaw is in explaining it.
Tip “Inevitable” is a warning sign: knocking out one explanation rarely leaves only one other.

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer D
If I do more work tonight, I will earn more money. However, if I go for a bike ride, I will get fitter. I need to get fitter more than I need more money.Reason So, I will go for a bike ride.Main conclusion

Which of the following most closely parallels the reasoning in the above argument?

  1. AIf I eat that cake it will taste really good. But if I do not eat that cake I will lose weight. I don't care about losing weight anymore! So I'm going to eat that cake.✗ One benefit is thrown out (“don’t care”), not weighed against the other.
  2. BIf I learn to drive I will have a useful skill. If I save my money I will be able to travel round the world. I need to be able to drive, but I can learn that later. So I will travel the world while I am young.✗ Timing decides (“I can learn that later”), not which benefit matters more.
  3. CIf I work every evening in a bar, I will be able to buy my own clothes. But, if I stay in and do my homework, I will get better grades. Right now it is more important to me to earn my own money, but I know I will regret it if I get bad grades. So I will only work two evenings a week.✗ Ends in a compromise (two evenings), not a choice of one option.
  4. DIf I do the housework, the house will be clean and pleasant. If I go to the pub, I will be relaxed and pleasant. I prefer a pleasant me to a pleasant house, so I will go to the pub.✓ Same shape: two options, each with its own benefit; one benefit matters more; so pick that option.
  5. EIf I go out now, I will have fun with my friends. However, if I go out, I will not do my homework, and if I don't do my homework my teacher shout at me in front of my friends. So I had better not go out.✗ One option with a chain of bad results, so don’t do it. No ranking of two benefits.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if A (work) X (money). If B (bike ride) Y (fitter). I want Y more than X so do B.

DIf A (housework) → X (pleasant house). If B (pub) → Y (pleasant me). Prefer Y to X → so B (pub). ✓ Same shape.
AEat → tastes good; don’t eat → lose weight. “Don’t care about weight” → eat. A benefit is dismissed, not ranked. ✗
BDrive → skill; save → travel. “Can learn that later” → travel now. Decided by timing. ✗
CBar → clothes; homework → grades. Wants money more, but → work two evenings. A compromise. ✗
EGo out → fun, but → no homework → teacher shouts → don’t go. One option, weighed against its own cost. ✗
Tip Check the ending too: the original picks one option outright because its result is wanted more.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer A
The increasing use of mobile phones on trains and in restaurants shows a serious lack of consideration for other people.Intermediate conclusion Yes, of course, it is very convenient to be able to conduct your business while travelling or waiting for your food, and, whether we like it or not, the technology is here to stay.Opposing view But nobody really wants to listen to one end of someone else's boring conversations throughout a meal or a journey.Reason The same phone user would soon object if I were to read aloud from the newspaper and disrupt his or her call.Comparison Therefore, given that people should treat others in the way they expect to be treated themselves,Reason mobile phones should not be freely used on trains and in restaurants.Main conclusion

Which of the following best illustrates the principle underlying the argument above?

  1. AYou would object if your children made you listen to their music so you should not inflict your choice of music on them.✓ Principle: if you would object to it being done to you, don’t do it to others. A applies exactly that to music.
  2. BNo one should try to turn the clock back, especially when it comes to technology; therefore you should accept some annoyances for the sake of progress and convenience.✗ The opposite: put up with annoyances for the sake of convenience.
  3. CThere should be no first class accommodation on trains on the grounds that all paying customers should be treated equally.✗ Equal treatment of customers: a different principle (equality, not “treat others as you would be treated”).
  4. DOne person's annoyance is another person's convenience; therefore we should be more tolerant of each other's behaviour.✗ Asks for more tolerance: the opposite conclusion.
  5. ECalm and relaxation are the keys to a happy life; therefore we should avoid the unnecessary stresses that the modern technological world has brought with it.✗ Same topic (the stress of modern technology) but a different rule.

Working: write the principle first

Phone user would object to me reading aloud people should treat others as they expect to be treated so don’t inflict your phone calls on others.
So the principle: if you would object to something being done to you, don’t do it to others.

AYou’d object to their music → so don’t inflict yours on them. ✓ Same rule, new setting.
BAccept annoyances for progress. Tolerate, don’t refrain. ✗
CTreat all customers equally. Equality, not “do as you would be done by”. ✗
DBe more tolerant of others. The opposite conclusion. ✗
EAvoid the stresses of technology. Matches the topic, not the rule. ✗
Tip The principle is stated outright here (“given that…”). Find the option that reasons from “you would object” to “so don’t do it”.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
The so-called 'baby-boomer' generation (people born in Britain in the late 1940's /early 1950's) will enjoy a good chance of reaching a ripe old-age.Reason They benefited from a diet that was austere but healthy when they were children and this has influenced their adult tastes. In addition, people in this age group have given up unhealthy activities such as smoking cigarettes.Evidence / example In contrast, the generation represented by those currently in their 20's and early 30's are in danger of experiencing serious health problems which would decrease their life expectancy.Reason This is the 'Big Mac' generation whose childhood diet was high in fat and sugar, which they continue to crave. They also show worrying tendencies to indulge in 'binge drinking' and other health-threatening activities.Evidence / example

Which of the following is a conclusion that can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AHealth is largely determined by factors which the individual can control.✗ “Largely determined” goes too far. Childhood diet wasn’t their choice, and other factors aren’t discussed.
  2. BHealth education is becoming less effective in controlling behaviour.✗ Health education is never mentioned.
  3. CThe trend towards ever-increasing life expectancy will be reversed.✗ Too strong: they are only “in danger”, and one generation doesn’t decide the whole trend.

Tip A safe conclusion stays inside the passage: what follows for the people actually described.

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer B
A kidney can be taken safely from a living person, and can save the life of a dying patient. Given that the demand for kidney transplants exceeds the supply of donated kidneys,Reason the law in the UK that forbids people to sell one of their kidneys should be abolished,Main conclusion because this would result in a greater number of successful kidney transplants.Reason People are worried that it would involve rich patients exploiting poor potential donors.Opposing view But selling one’s kidney to support one’s family is not very much different from supporting one’s family by working in a dangerous occupation.Comparison Some patients have bought kidneys and received transplants in other countries, where the risks to their life from such operations can be great.Evidence / example

17(Q33 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer A
In cricket, the world's fastest bowlers bowl at speeds of over ninety miles per hour. When the bowler releases the ball he is less than twenty metres away from the batsman, which means that the batsman has less than half a second to react to the ball and select his shot.Evidence / example According to scientists this is impossible, as there is simply not enough time for the necessary thought processes and muscle responses to occur.Opposing view And yet this is exactly what happens.Evidence / example This just shows how science can all too easily fail to explain things due to its own limitations.Main conclusion

Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AIn order to play against bowling of this pace, the batsman's response has become a learned reflex rather than a conscious decision.✓ The scientists only said there isn’t time for thought processes. A learned reflex skips conscious thought, so science can explain it after all.
  2. BIt is only with the aid of high technology speed cameras that the speed of the bowler's delivery can be established.✗ Speed cameras are about measuring the ball. They don’t touch the reaction puzzle.
  3. CTennis players often have to return serves which are travelling in excess of a hundred miles per hour over similar distances.✗ Another example of the same “impossible” feat, so it supports the argument.
  4. DThere is much that science has yet to discover about the ways that the mind works and how it affects the body.✗ Says science has limits: this strengthens the conclusion.
  5. EBatsmen can often be too slow to react to fast bowling and consequently get badly hurt.✗ Some batsmen failing doesn’t explain how the others manage it.
Tip Weaken by showing science can explain the feat: look for a route that needs no conscious thought.

18(Q34 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
Education in this country has entered a spiral of decline.Intermediate conclusion In recent attainment tests, almost half of 11-year-olds performed at an under-average level.Evidence / example The government claims that standards are improving as a result of league tables and National Curriculum Strategies,Opposing view but these results prove that tests are simply getting easier.Intermediate conclusion We badly need to return to the teaching and assessment methods of the fifties.Main conclusion

Which of the following best explains a flaw in the above argument?

  1. AMore than 50 % of students were over-average in this test, which shows that standards have improved.✗ Makes the same mistake the other way: being over the average says nothing about standards.
  2. BLeague tables have increased differences between schools in middle class and deprived areas.✗ The gap between schools in different areas is a separate issue.
  3. CThe author appeals to parents' fears about their children's education.✗ No appeal to parents’ fears is made.
  4. DNo matter how hard the test, nearly 50 % of students will achieve under-average results.✓ The average sits in the middle, so about half will always be below it. “Almost half under average” is normal, not proof of decline.
  5. EWe need a return to the social attitudes rather than the teaching methods of the fifties.✗ A different recommendation, not a fault in the reasoning.
Tip Watch for figures that are true by definition: about half are always below average.

20(Q39 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer A
In attempting to reduce the high injury toll from road offences such as drink-driving, successive governments have tended to rely on the deterrent effect of increasingly severe penalties.Context However, severe penalties can act as a deterrent only if potential offenders believe that the chances of being detected are moderate or high.Reason Recent surveys have shown that people perceive the chance of detection for the offence of drink-driving as being very small.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can be concluded from the passage above?

  1. ASevere penalties for the offence of drink-driving do not constitute an effective deterrent.✓ Penalties deter only if people think they might be caught. People think the chance is very small, so the penalties don’t deter.
  2. BSuccessive governments have not tackled the problem of drink-driving.✗ They have tackled it, with severer penalties. The point is that the method doesn’t work.
  3. CDrink-driving offenders have a misguided perception of their chances of detection.✗ “Misguided” assumes the real chance is higher. We only know what people perceive.

24(Q46 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer A
The proportion of time people devote in their lives to travelling from A to B has been constant over history.Reason So the advent of high speed forms of transport simply means that people will travel greater distances.Main conclusion

Which of the following most closely parallels the above reasoning?

  1. AMortgage loans have always represented around 3 to 4 times a person's annual income. Therefore a rise in average earnings will mean an increase in mortgage lending.✓ Same shape: one factor stays fixed (time / the 3 to 4 multiple), so when the other rises (speed / earnings), the result rises too (distance / lending).
  2. BThe ratio between mortgage repayments and total expenditure per household has always been constant. So more expensive food will mean more debt.✗ The ratio is about repayments, but the conclusion jumps to “debt”, which the ratio says nothing about.
  3. CWith a fixed rate of mortgage interest, an increase in house prices will mean that inflation will rise.✗ No constant proportion at all, just a fixed interest rate and a claim about inflation.
  4. DThe increase of home ownership has been in proportion to the rise in average earnings. So the increase in house building means people are earning more.✗ Runs backwards (from house building to earnings) and swaps home ownership for house building.
  5. EThe proportion of new to old homes on the market has always been constant. So an increase in housing development means that more old houses are being renovated.✗ Starts well (fixed new-to-old ratio) but ends on old houses being renovated, not old homes on the market.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: travel time is a fixed share speed goes up so distance goes up (distance = speed × fixed time).
Pattern: result = fixed factor × X. X rises result rises. Same things at both ends.

ALending = 3 to 4 × income (fixed) → earnings rise → lending rises. ✓ Same shape.
BRepayments : spending fixed → food dearer → “more debt”. The conclusion brings in a new term. ✗
CFixed interest rate → house prices up → inflation up. No fixed proportion linking them. ✗
DOwnership rises with earnings → house building → so earnings up. Backwards, and a swapped term. ✗
ENew : old fixed → more new homes → “more old houses renovated”. Should be “more old homes on the market”. ✗
Trap! Check that the conclusion uses the same terms as the fixed relationship. B and E both slip in something new.
