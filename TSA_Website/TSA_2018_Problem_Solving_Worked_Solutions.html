1(Q2 in the original paper) Equations & word problemsAnswer D

There are 24 pupils1 in a class. They decided to help the local park by planting a total of 24 plants2, a mixture of birch trees and roses. Each girl planted three roses3, and every three boys planted one birch tree between them4.

How many more boys than girls5 are there in the class?

  1. A3
  2. B6
  3. C8
  4. D12
  5. E18

Reading it properly

  • 1Girls + boys = 24.
  • 2Roses + trees = 24 as well. Two totals, two unknowns.
  • 31 girl → 3 plants. Girls make lots of plants!
  • 4Trap: it’s 1 tree per three boys, so boys make very few plants. Boys 3 = trees.
  • 5They want boys − girls, not the number of boys.

Working it out

  1. Write the two facts as sums:

    girls + boys = 24
    roses + trees = 3 × girls + boys3 = 24

  2. Times the second line by 3 to clear the fraction, then take away the first line:

    9 × girls + boys = 72
    1 × girls + boys = 24
    subtract 8 × girls = 48 girls = 6
    boys = 24 − 6 = 18
    Tip No algebra? Try the options. The number of boys must divide by 3 (they work in threes), and boys + girls = 24.

  3. Check it with a picture:

    18 − 6 = 12 more boys
D12: 18 boys and 6 girls.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 3 is impossible: boys + girls = 24 is even, so boys − girls must be even too.
  • B✗ 6 is the number of girls (or of trees), not the difference.
  • C✗ 8 would mean 8 girls and 16 boys. The 8 girls alone plant 24 roses, so the boys’ trees push the total past 24 (and 16 boys don’t split into threes).
  • E✗ 18 is the number of boys. The question asks how many more boys than girls.
Tip Boys must be a multiple of 3. Girls g, boys 24 − g: 3g + (24 − g) 3 = 24 gives g = 6. 18 − 6 = 12.

2(Q6 in the original paper) Ratio & percentagesAnswer D

Jake has a 500 ml1 bottle of orange squash that he has made according to the instructions on the bottle of concentrate. The instructions dictate that he should add 4 parts water to 1 part concentrate2. He accidentally spills his squash, and now there is only 400 ml3 in the bottle. He then tops up the remainder with concentrate4.

What percentage5 of the squash in his 500 ml bottle is now concentrate?

  1. A18%
  2. B20%
  3. C28%
  4. D36%
  5. E40%

Reading it properly

  • 1Start: a full 500 ml of ready-made squash.
  • 24 + 1 = 5 parts, so concentrate is 1 part in 5 = 20%. (Not 1 in 4!)
  • 3The spilt squash takes concentrate with it: 400 ml is still 20% concentrate.
  • 4He refills the missing 100 ml with pure concentrate.
  • 5Concentrate 500 × 100.

Working it out

  1. Follow the concentrate through the three stages:

  2. start: 500 5 = 100 ml concentrate
    after the spill: 400 5 = 80 ml concentrate
    top-up: 80 + 100 = 180 ml concentrate
  3. 180500 = 36100 = 36%
    Tip Out of 500, double it and move the point: 180 × 2 = 360, so 36%.
D36%: 180 ml of the 500 ml is concentrate.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 18% is 180 ml divided by 1000 instead of 500.
  • B✗ 20% is the strength before the accident: it forgets the 100 ml of pure concentrate added.
  • C✗ 28% would need 140 ml of concentrate, so only 40 ml would have survived the spill. But 400 ml of squash at 1 part in 5 still holds 80 ml.
  • E✗ 40% reads “4 parts water to 1 part concentrate” as 1 part in 4 (125 ml at the start, 100 after the spill, 200 after the top-up). It is 1 part in 5.
Tip Concentrate is ⅕. After the spill 400 5 = 80 ml, plus 100 ml = 180 ml. 180 500 = 36%.

4(Q8 in the original paper) Digital displaysAnswer C

Most railway stations have digital clocks that display the time in 24-hour format1. I recently undertook a train journey from Exeter to Aberdeen. As the train left Exeter, the clock on the platform showed:

I lost track of time as I read a book and then dozed. I eventually became aware of the train coming to rest and the guard announcing ‘Carlisle’.

I opened my eyes, and found that I could only see the top of the platform clock2 (because of the hoarding in the way) as follows:

Approximately what time was it, to the nearest hour3, when the train stopped at Carlisle?

  1. A1 o’clock
  2. B2 o’clock
  3. C3 o’clock
  4. D4 o’clock
  5. E5 o’clock

Reading it properly

  • 1So the afternoon shows as 13, 14, 15…
  • 2Each digit only shows its top bar and two upper side strokes. Use the 08:34 clock to see how each digit is built (look at its 3 and 4).
  • 3Round the time you decode to the nearest hour. Don’t forget the minutes!

Working it out

  1. Work out what the top half of every digit looks like, then match the four tops on the Carlisle clock:

  2. 1st digit: just a right-hand stroke → 1
    2nd digit: two side strokes, no top bar → 4 (just like the 4 in 08:34)
    3rd digit: top bar + left stroke → 5 or 6, but minutes only go to 5 → 5
    4th digit: top bar + right stroke → 2, 3 or 7
    time = 14:52, 14:53 or 14:57 nearest hour 15:00 = 3 o’clock
    Check Sense check: left Exeter at 08:34, reached Carlisle about 6½ hours later. Mid-afternoon is believable.
C3 o’clock: the clock reads 14:5_, which rounds to 15:00.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 1 o’clock would need 12:30 to 13:29. The tops of 2 and 3 have a top bar, which isn’t there.
  • B✗ 2 o’clock reads the hour as 14 = 2 pm but forgets the minutes are in the 50s, which round up to 3.
  • D✗ 4 o’clock reads the second digit, 4, as the hour and ignores the 1 in front (24-hour clock).
  • E✗ 5 o’clock takes the minutes digit 5 as the hour.
Tip Tops: 1, 4, 5, then 2/3/7. So 14:5x, which is nearly 15:00: 3 o’clock.

When did Lucy’s shares have the biggest percentage increase1?

  1. A2011–2012
  2. B2012–2013
  3. C2013–2014
  4. D2014–2015
  5. E2015–2016

Reading it properly

  • 1Percentage increase = rise starting value. Compare the multipliers, not the pounds.
  • 2Trap: the last jump is the biggest in pounds (£7.56), but it is from a big starting value.

Working it out

  1. Find each year’s multiplier (new old). Quick trick: find the rise as a fraction of the old value.

    3.00 → 4.20: rise 1.20 = 0.4 × 3.00 → +40%
    4.20 → 6.30: rise 2.10 = half of 4.20 → +50%
    6.30 → 9.45: rise 3.15 = half of 6.30 → +50%
    9.45 → 15.12: rise 5.67 = 0.6 × 9.45 → +60%
    15.12 → 22.68: rise 7.56 = half of 15.12 → +50%
    Tip 0.6 × 9.45: 9.45 × 6 = 56.70, then 10 = 5.67. Exactly the rise!
  2. biggest percentage rise: 60% in 2014–2015
D2014–2015: the value went up by 60% (from £9.45 to £15.12).

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 2011–2012 is +40%, the smallest.
  • B✗ 2012–2013 is +50%.
  • C✗ 2013–2014 is +50%.
  • E✗ 2015–2016 is the biggest rise in pounds (£7.56), but only +50% because it started from £15.12.
Tip Halve the old value and add it on: that gives 6.30, 9.45 and 22.68 exactly, so those are +50%. 9.45 → 15.12 is more than half again (+60%). D.

7(Q14 in the original paper) Folding & page orderAnswer C

The diagram below shows the method of construction of a book with four folded sheets1 in each block, and three blocks in total. Page one is the first page of the first block of four sheets. At the centre of each block, you can see the stitching that binds the book together.

A book is to be made using the same technique but with eight sheets of paper in each of the three blocks2.

Which of the following pairs of pages could be in the centre of a block3 (that is, if opened there, the stitching would be visible)?

  1. A46 and 47
  2. B47 and 48
  3. C48 and 49
  4. D63 and 64
  5. E64 and 65

Reading it properly

  • 1One sheet folded in half = 2 leaves = 4 pages (front and back of each leaf).
  • 28 sheets × 4 pages = 32 pages per block; 96 pages in the book.
  • 3The centre is the middle of a block’s pages, not the gap between two blocks.

Working it out

  1. Pages per block: 8 sheets × 4 = 32. So the blocks are pages 1–32, 33–64 and 65–96.

  2. The middle of 32 pages is between the 16th and 17th page of the block:

    block 1: 16 | 17
    block 2: 32 + 16 = 48 | 49
    block 3: 64 + 16 = 80 | 81
    in the list: 48 and 49
    32 + 16 · column addition
    32
    +16
    48
    64 + 16 · column addition
    64
    +16
    80
    1
    Check A spread always has an even page on the left and the next odd page on the right. 47|48 and 63|64 are the two sides of one leaf, so you can never see them together.
C48 and 49: the middle of the second block (pages 33 to 64).

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 46 and 47 do face each other in block 2, but one leaf before the centre. The stitching is between 48 and 49.
  • B✗ 47 and 48 are the front and back of the same leaf, so they can’t be seen as an open pair.
  • D✗ 63 and 64 are the two sides of one leaf too, near the end of block 2.
  • E✗ 64 and 65 is where block 2 ends and block 3 starts: the join between blocks, not the stitched centre.
Tip 8 sheets = 32 pages a block. Centres at 16|17, 48|49, 80|81. Only 48 and 49 is listed.

8(Q18 in the original paper) Working backwardsAnswer B

Five girls are collecting shells on a beach. They are putting them in buckets that each hold 24 shells1. At the end of the collecting time, they are going to ensure that they all have the same number of shells2. Four of them are only two short of filling their bucket3, but the youngest one is lagging behind with her total. Each of the others give her three shells4 from their buckets in order to equalise the numbers collected.

How many shells did the youngest girl collect for herself5?

  1. A4
  2. B7
  3. C10
  4. D12
  5. E19

Reading it properly

  • 1Full bucket = 24.
  • 2After the sharing, all five have the same number.
  • 324 − 2 = 22 each for the four older girls.
  • 4Each of the four gives 3, so the youngest receives 4 × 3 = 12.
  • 5Her own number before the gifts.

Working it out

  2. older girls: 22 − 3 = 19 each
    so the youngest also ends with 19
    she received 4 × 3 = 12
    her own shells = 19 − 12 = 7
    Check Check: 4 × 22 + 7 = 95 shells, and 95 5 = 19 each. ✓
B7 shells: she ends with 19 after receiving 12.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 4 = 19 − 15: it counts five girls giving 3 shells, but only four girls give.
  • C✗ 10 = 22 − 12: it uses the others’ total before they gave shells away.
  • D✗ 12 is how many shells she was given, not how many she collected.
  • E✗ 19 is what everyone ends up with after the sharing.
Tip Others end on 22 − 3 = 19. She got 12, so she had 19 − 12 = 7.

The manager has decided not to reward any player who has missed two consecutive training sessions more than twice1, and the manager will also exclude anyone who has failed to score a goal from a penalty kick more than twice2. After these criteria have been applied, the prize will go to the player who has scored the highest number of goals3.

  • B✗ 5:25 am comes from averaging the three speeds to 60 km/h and dividing the total distance (395 km) by it. You can’t average speeds like that.
  • C✗ 5:45 am adds the two 15-minute stops on top of the 5 hours, but they are already included.
  • E✗ 6:45 am leaves out the final 30-minute drive.
Tip 15 min + 5 h + 30 min = 5 h 45 min. 12:00 − 5:45 = 6:15.
