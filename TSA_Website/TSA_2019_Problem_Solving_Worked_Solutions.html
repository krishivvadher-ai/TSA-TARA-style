1(Q2 in the original paper) PercentagesAnswer C

Each region in the country of Bolandia sets its own tax rate which applies to the whole of an individual’s annual income1.

Below is a table showing tax information relating to six different people.

nameregionannual incometax rate
Tim2North$10 00030%
NeilCentral$20 00025%
VeronicaWest$150 0003%
DavidNorth West$50 0006%
AmyEast$80 0005%
RozFrontier$12 00030%

Who pays the same amount of tax3 in a year as Tim?

  1. ANeil
  2. BVeronica
  3. CDavid
  4. DAmy
  5. ERoz

Reading it properly

  • 1No tax-free allowance, no bands. Tax = rate × whole income. Simple!
  • 2Tim’s row is the target. Work out his tax first.
  • 3The same amount in dollars, not the same rate. Roz has the same rate, which is the trap.

Working it out

  1. Tim first:

    30% of $10 000 = 0.3 × 10 000 = $3 000

  2. Now everyone else. Trick: find 1% (move the decimal point two places), then multiply by the rate.

    Neil: 1% = 200 → 25 × 200 = $5 000
    Veronica: 1% = 1 500 → 3 × 1 500 = $4 500
    David: 1% = 500 → 6 × 500 = $3 000 ✓
    Amy: 1% = 800 → 5 × 800 = $4 000
    Roz: 1% = 120 → 30 × 120 = $3 600
  3. same as Tim David
    Trap! Roz is the trap: same 30% rate as Tim, but her income is bigger, so she pays more.
CDavid: 6% of $50 000 = $3 000, exactly the same as Tim’s 30% of $10 000.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ Neil pays 25% of $20 000 = $5 000.
  • B✗ Veronica pays 3% of $150 000 = $4 500. A tiny rate, but a huge income.
  • D✗ Amy pays 5% of $80 000 = $4 000.
  • E✗ Roz has the same rate as Tim (30%), but on $12 000 that is $3 600, not $3 000.
Tip Tim pays $3 000. Scan for rate × income = 3 000: 6% of 50 000 = 6 × 500 = 3 000. David.

2(Q6 in the original paper) Area & ratioAnswer D

Below is a greyscale image of the flag of Canada. The flag consists of two red strips on the left and the right, a white square in the middle, and a red maple leaf in the centre.

[diagram not to scale]

The width of the white square is twice as large as the width of a red strip1. The red maple leaf occupies a quarter of the square in the middle2.

What is the ratio of red to white3 in the flag?

  1. A1 : 1
  2. B2 : 1
  3. C3 : 2
  4. D5 : 3
  5. E5 : 4

Reading it properly

  • 1Widths 1 : 2 : 1. And it is a square, so it is 2 tall as well.
  • 2The leaf turns a quarter of the white square red. Add it to red and take it off white.
  • 3Compare areas: red area : white area.

Working it out

  1. Give the strip width 1 unit. The square is 2 by 2, so the strips are 1 by 2. Draw it on a grid of unit squares:

  2. flag = 4 × 2 = 8 small squares
    red strips = 2 + 2 = 4
    white square = 4, leaf = ¼ of 4 = 1
    red = 4 + 1 = 5, white = 4 − 1 = 3 5 : 3
    Check 5 + 3 = 8, the whole flag. Always check the two parts add back up to the total.
D5 : 3. Red = 4 (strips) + 1 (leaf) = 5 squares; white = 4 − 1 = 3 squares.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 1 : 1 forgets the leaf: strips 4 against square 4.
  • B✗ 2 : 1 would need the leaf to cover a third of the square (red 4 + 4/3 against white 8/3). It only covers a quarter.
  • C✗ 3 : 2 would need the leaf to cover only a fifth of the square.
  • E✗ 5 : 4 adds the leaf to the red but forgets to take it away from the white.
Tip Units: strips 1 × 2 each, square 2 × 2. Red 2 + 2 + 1 = 5, white 4 − 1 = 3. 5 : 3.

4(Q8 in the original paper) Charts & reasoningAnswer E

The bar chart shows the total numbers of tickets1 sold for a concert over three nights.

Premium tickets are more expensive than Standard tickets2.

Which one of the following statements is correct?

  1. AThe highest total sales income was for Thursday.
  2. BThe highest total sales income was for Friday.
  3. CThe highest total sales income could have been for Thursday or Friday.
  4. DThe highest total sales income could have been for Thursday or Saturday.
  5. EThe highest total sales income could have been for Friday or Saturday.

Reading it properly

  • 1Numbers of tickets, not money. We don’t know the prices!
  • 2The only price fact: each Premium costs more than each Standard. Use it to compare nights.

Working it out

  1. Read the chart:

    Thursday: 200 Standard, 150 Premium
    Friday: 150 Standard, 225 Premium
    Saturday: 300 Standard, 125 Premium

  2. Picture each night as blocks of 25 tickets:

  3. Thursday v Friday: Friday has 50 fewer Standard but 75 more Premium. 75 dear tickets are worth more than 50 cheap ones, whatever the prices. So Thursday can never be the highest A, C, D are out.

  4. Friday v Saturday: try two price pairs.

    Standard £10, Premium £11: Fri = 1500 + 2475 = 3975, Sat = 3000 + 1375 = 4375 → Saturday wins
    Standard £10, Premium £20: Fri = 1500 + 4500 = 6000, Sat = 3000 + 2500 = 5500 → Friday wins
    either could be highest E
EFriday or Saturday: Friday always beats Thursday, but Friday v Saturday depends on the prices.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ Thursday always loses to Friday: Friday swaps 50 cheap tickets for 75 dear ones.
  • B✗ Friday is not certain: if Premium is only a little dearer (e.g. £11 v £10), Saturday’s 300 Standard tickets win.
  • C✗ Thursday can’t be highest, because Friday always beats it.
  • D✗ Same problem: Thursday can’t be highest.
Tip Fri v Thu: −50 S, +75 P → Fri always more. Fri v Sat: +150 S, −100 P → depends on prices. So Friday or Saturday.

5(Q12 in the original paper) Cycles & timingsAnswer C

When riding my bike in the dark, I use two rear bike lamps. Both of the lamps are in ‘flashing mode’, which means that they repeatedly emit light for a set period of time at set intervals. The reasons for using two lights are that it reduces the amount of time when neither of the lamps emit light, and that if one of the lamps stops working, other road users can still see me on the road. One of the lamps shines for one second and goes dark for one second repeatedly1; the other lamp shines for two seconds and goes dark for two seconds repeatedly2. I always turn both lamps on simultaneously3 at the start of my ride.

For how many seconds in each minute4 do neither5 of the lamps shine when they are in use?

  1. A10
  2. B12
  3. C15
  4. D20
  5. E30

Reading it properly

  • 1Lamp 1: a 2-second cycle (on 1, off 1).
  • 2Lamp 2: a 4-second cycle (on 2, off 2).
  • 3Both start “on” at time 0, so the patterns are lined up.
  • 460 seconds.
  • 5Count only the moments when BOTH are dark.

Working it out

  1. Draw both lamps on a timeline:

  2. The whole pattern repeats every 4 seconds (both cycles fit into 4). In each 4 seconds only 1 second is dark for both (from 3 s to 4 s).

    60 4 = 15 repeats in a minute
    15 × 1 second = 15
    Check Lamp 2 is dark for 2 s of every 4. Lamp 1 shines for half of that time. So both are dark for 1 s in every 4: a quarter of 60 = 15.
C15 seconds: 1 dark second in every 4-second cycle, and 60 4 = 15.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 10 would mean one dark second every 6 seconds, but the pattern repeats every 4.
  • B✗ 12 would mean one dark second every 5 seconds: the cycle is 4 seconds, not 5.
  • D✗ 20 comes from a 3-second cycle (adding 1 + 2), but the lamps line up again every 4 seconds.
  • E✗ 30 is how long lamp 1 is dark. Lamp 2 is shining for half of those seconds.
Tip Cycle = 4 s. Both dark only in second 4 of each cycle. 60 4 = 15.

7(Q14 in the original paper) Reading graphsAnswer B

Peter has drawn two diagrams1 showing information about the two cars owned by his parents:

Which one of the following statements is not2 correct?

  1. AThe more expensive car is newer.
  2. BThe cheaper car is bigger.
  3. CThe older car is slower.
  4. DThe newer car is bigger.
  5. EThe slower car is smaller.

Reading it properly

  • 1No numbers: just compare the two dots on each diagram. Further right = more, higher up = more.
  • 2Find the false statement. Four are true.

Working it out

  1. Turn each diagram into words:

    Careful with age: further right = older, so car 2 (on the left) is the newer one.

  2. Now test each statement:

    A: dearer = car 2, newer = car 2 ✓
    B: cheaper = car 1, but car 1 is smaller ✗
    C: older = car 1, slower = car 1 ✓
    D: newer = car 2, bigger = car 2 ✓
    E: slower = car 1, smaller = car 1 ✓
    not correct B
BB: the cheaper car (car 1) is the smaller one, not the bigger one.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ True: car 2 is both dearer and newer (lower age).
  • C✗ True: car 1 is older and slower.
  • D✗ True: car 2 is newer and bigger.
  • E✗ True: car 1 is slower and smaller.
Tip Car 2 wins everything (newer, dearer, faster, bigger). B pairs “cheaper” (car 1) with “bigger” (car 2): false.

8(Q18 in the original paper) Cycles & LCMAnswer B

Three friends are training to run a marathon. Part of this training involves running steadily round a track in the local park. They are all at different stages in their training schedules so they run at different speeds. Alec completes each lap in 3 minutes, Barry takes 5 minutes and Colin takes 6 minutes1.

They all leave the start line at the same time2.

If they each maintain their running speed throughout the session, how many more laps will Alec have completed compared to Barry3 when the three of them next cross the start line together4?

  1. A2
  2. B4
  3. C5
  4. D6
  5. E10

Reading it properly

  • 1Lap times 3, 5 and 6 minutes.
  • 2They start together at time 0.
  • 3Alec’s laps minus Barry’s laps.
  • 4All three, not just Alec and Barry. Find the first time that is in all three times tables.

Working it out

  1. They are together at the line when the time is a multiple of 3, 5 and 6 (the lowest common multiple).

    multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30
    30 is in the 3 and 5 times tables too ✓
    together again after 30 minutes
  2. Alec: 30 3 = 10 laps
    Barry: 30 5 = 6 laps
    10 − 6 = 4
    Trap! At 15 minutes Alec and Barry are both at the line (5 and 3 laps), but Colin is halfway round. That gives the trap answer 2.
B4 more laps: after 30 minutes Alec has run 10 laps and Barry 6.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 2 is the gap at 15 minutes, when only Alec and Barry are at the line. Colin isn’t there (15 6 = 2.5 laps).
  • C✗ 5 is Colin’s number of laps (or Alec minus Colin), not Alec minus Barry.
  • D✗ 6 is Barry’s total number of laps, not the difference.
  • E✗ 10 is Alec’s total, not how many more than Barry.
Tip LCM of 3, 5, 6 = 30. Alec 10 laps, Barry 6. Difference 4.

10(Q20 in the original paper) 3D viewsAnswer E

The see-through1 glass block below can be viewed from six sides, including from above and below2.

Which one of the following is not3 a possible view?

  1. Aview A
  2. Bview B
  3. Cview C
  4. Dview D
  5. Eview E

Reading it properly

  • 1See-through: hidden edges show as lines too. So the view from the back is just the front view flipped, and so on.
  • 2Six views: front, back, left, right, top, bottom. Find which drawing matches none.
  • 3Four drawings are real views; find the odd one out.

Working it out

  1. Measure the block using the drawing’s edges as units: 7 long, 5 deep, 4 tall. At the front is a step 2 deep and 2 high. Behind it the block is 4 high, with a slot 2 wide and 1 deep cut into the back, all the way down.

  2. Now match each pair of views (opposite views are mirror images, because it is see-through):

    front / back: 7 × 4, a line across halfway (the step), two lines up (the slot sides) B ✓
    left / right: an L-shape 5 wide, tall part 3, step 2, and a line 1 in (the slot) A and D ✓
    top / bottom: 7 × 5 with a 2 × 1 notch in the back edge and a line 2 from the front C ✓

  3. E is 5 wide and 6 tall, and draws the slot as a closed box. No edge of the block is 6 long, and the slot is cut through the back face, so it must be a gap in the outline (as in C), not a box inside it.

    not possible E
EE: wrong shape (5 × 6) and the slot is drawn boxed in, but it is open at the back.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ A is the view from the left: L-shape with the tall part on the left and the slot’s face 1 unit in.
  • B✗ B is the view from the back: the step line across the middle and the two slot sides (3, 2, 2).
  • C✗ C is the view from below: 7 × 5, the slot as a notch in the back edge, the step line 2 from the front.
  • D✗ D is the view from the right: A’s mirror image.
Tip Side views are L-shapes (A, D), front/back is the 2 × 3 grid (B), top/bottom has the notch (C). E has a size the block doesn’t have.

What is the maximum and minimum2 number of times that any one segment3 lights up during this sequence?

  3. Cmaximum 8, minimum 3

  2. top 7, top-right 7, middle 7, bottom 6, top-left 5

    bottom-left: only 2, 6 and 8 3

13(Q26 in the original paper) Rolling a cubeAnswer D

A solid cube has 6 different shapes drawn on each of its 6 faces. A plan view showing all the shapes is given below. It is placed on the path drawn below with the triangular shape facing up1. The cube is rolled, one face at a time2, forward along the route shown, from start position until it reaches the end position.

Which one of the following shapes is facing up3 when it reaches the end position?

  1. Asquare
  2. Bblack circle
  3. Ctriangle
  4. Dtall bar
  5. Ecircle with a white dot

Reading it properly

  • 1Start: triangle on top. So the face opposite the triangle is on the bottom.
  • 2Each move tips the cube over one edge: count the moves along the path.
  • 3Only the top face matters at the end.

Working it out

  1. Opposite faces from the net: in a straight row of four, faces two apart are opposite.

    bar ↔ triangle
    square ↔ cross
    dotted circle ↔ black circle (above and below the triangle)

    So at the start the bar is underneath.

  2. Key trick: rolling twice in the same direction turns the cube upside down.

  3. The path is 2 rolls right, 2 rolls down, 2 rolls right: three “double rolls”, so the cube flips three times.

    triangle → bar → triangle → bar D
DThe tall bar: three double rolls flip the cube three times, so the face that started underneath ends on top.

Why not the others?
