1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
Sometimes poverty is defined in relation to average income. For example, people may be said to be poor if their income is less than 60 per cent of the average income for the population.Context This is not a legitimate definition,Main conclusion because it is a measure of inequality rather than poverty.Reason This is obvious if we consider the consequences of using the definition. It means that a huge pay rise for middle managers automatically throws some people into poverty, even though their income has not changed. On the other hand, the definition implies that in a society where almost everyone is starving, no one is poor.Evidence / example

Which of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AHaving a relatively low income is not the same as being poor.✗ This is the reason (it measures inequality, not poverty), not the conclusion.
  2. BIt is impossible to find an appropriate definition of poverty.✗ Too strong. It only rejects this definition, not every possible one.
  3. CIt is wrong to define poverty in relation to average income.✓ “This is not a legitimate definition” = it is wrong to define poverty by average income. The “because” and both examples support it.
  4. DA huge pay rise for middle managers makes other people poor.✗ Part of an example, and it only says the definition would call them poor.
  5. EThere is no inequality in a society where most people are starving.✗ Misreads the example. It says no one would count as poor, not that there is no inequality.
Tip “X, because Y”: X is the conclusion. The examples after it are there to make Y obvious.

2(Q3 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
Over the past ten years, there has been a four-fold increase in the number of people killed in road accidents who are found to have illegal drugs in their bodies. The rate of increase is much greater than the corresponding rate for those people killed in accidents who were found with alcohol in their blood.Evidence / example This shows that the campaign against drink-driving has succeeded.Intermediate conclusion Consequently, the Government ought now to concentrate on targeting those people who drive whilst under the influence of illegal drugs.Main conclusion

Which of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the argument above?

  1. AThose people who take illegal drugs and drive are likely to be different from those who drink and drive.✗ Different people is fine: the argument wants to target a new group anyway.
  2. BTargeting people who drive under the influence of illegal drugs ignores those people who take drugs but do not drive.✗ Non-drivers are irrelevant to road deaths.
  3. CThe success of the drink-driving campaign does not mean that a campaign against drivers who take drugs would succeed.✗ The argument never claims a drugs campaign would succeed, only that the focus should switch.
  4. DThe increase in drug-related road deaths is insufficient to conclude that the drink-driving campaign has succeeded.✓ Drug deaths rising faster than alcohol deaths says nothing about whether alcohol deaths fell. They may have risen too, just more slowly.
  5. EEvidence on the number of drug-related road accidents needs to distinguish between illegal drugs and legal drugs.✗ The passage is only about illegal drugs, so legal drugs do not affect the reasoning.
Tip “This shows…” is the weak link. Check the evidence really proves it: a faster rise elsewhere is not a fall here.

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer B
Socialist politicians are often taunted by their opponents for leading lifestyles similar to those of their capitalist counterparts.Context The theme of the taunts runs like this; 'You object on socialist principles to gross inequalities in the distribution of wealth; yet you enjoy a higher personal standard of living than the majority of the population. Therefore you are not really a socialist'.Opposing view But the jibe can easily be answered,Intermediate conclusion for there is no hypocrisy in arguing, even from a privileged position, for a fairer and more equal society.Reason

Which of the following is a conclusion which can be reliably drawn from the passage as a whole?

  1. ASocialists who enjoy personal wealth cannot object to others who amass even greater wealth.✗ The passage says they can argue for equality, so this goes the wrong way.
  2. BSomeone can be a genuine socialist whilst enjoying a high standard of living.✓ The taunt says “rich, so not really a socialist”. The passage rejects it, so a well-off person can be a genuine socialist.
  3. CCalls for reform are more effective if they come from the more privileged classes of society.✗ Effectiveness is never discussed, only hypocrisy.
  4. DThere is nothing immoral about inequalities in the distribution of wealth.✗ Socialists object to inequality, and the passage never says inequality is moral.
  5. EIt is hypocritical to claim to be a socialist if one enjoys a standard of living above the average.✗ This is the taunt the passage rejects.
Tip Find the view being knocked down (the taunt), then state its opposite.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer D
The Executive of the Students' Union has recently carried out a survey to identify dangerous areas on campus. The survey led to the recommendation and implementation of measures such as improved lighting in suspect areas.Context Such reforms can only help to reduce the number of attacks on campus.Reason However, the University lake and its surrounding area was not included in the survey as, despite being considered part of the campus, it actually belongs to the City Council.Evidence / example This means the University has no direct influence over safety standards in the area.Intermediate conclusion It should therefore definitely be avoided by lone students at night.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of this argument?

  1. AStudents do not look after their own safety but rely on others to take safety measures on their behalf.✗ Not needed. The advice to avoid the lake is students looking after themselves.
  2. BThe City Council regard the lake area as part of the campus.✗ Who regards it as campus does not affect whether it is safe.
  3. CStudents walking on and off campus at night would go via the university lake.✗ Not needed. The advice is to avoid the lake, whatever route students would take.
  4. DThe safety measures taken by the City Council are either non-existent or are inferior to those implemented by the Student Union.✓ “The University has no control, so avoid it” only works if the Council’s safety is worse. If the Council’s measures are as good, there is no reason to avoid the lake.
  5. EThe Executive of the Student Union is not merely seeking popularity in implementing its reforms but is genuinely concerned about safety risks.✗ The Union’s motives do not affect whether the lake is dangerous.
Tip Negation test: “the Council’s safety measures are just as good”. Then “definitely avoid” collapses, so D is assumed.

5(Q9 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
Some of the great sea-mammals, such as the sperm whale, have brains many times larger than ours.Evidence / example It is a fact of evolution that organs do not grow or remain large unless they are used; if they are not used they shrink or even disappear.Reason It must be concluded therefore that the sperm whale makes intelligent use of the vast brain it possesses, perhaps at thought levels well beyond our understanding.Main conclusion

Which of the following would, if true, weaken the argument?

  1. AHumans have developed intelligence despite having smaller brains than whales.✗ About human brains. It says nothing about how whales use theirs.
  2. BWhales lack the ability to communicate in language.✗ No language does not mean no intelligence; the passage even allows thought “beyond our understanding”.
  3. CLarge brains may have uses that do not involve intelligence.✓ The evidence only shows the brain is used. If it can be used for something other than thinking, the jump to intelligent use fails.
  4. DThere is no correlation between the size of the animal and the size of its brain.✗ If brain size is not just a side effect of body size, the big brain is more likely to be for something. That does not weaken it.
  5. EWhale intelligence may be of a kind that humans do not recognise.✗ This fits the “beyond our understanding” idea, so it supports the argument.
Tip Spot the gap between the evidence word (“used”) and the conclusion word (“intelligent use”).

6(Q10 in the original paper) FlawAnswer C
Mrs Jackson said that, if Mrs List were promoted, she would resignReason and we definitely do not want to lose Mrs Jackson.Context However, we are not going to promote Mrs List: we are going to offer the promotion to Mr Wade.Reason Therefore Mrs Jackson will not resign.Main conclusion

Which of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the argument?

  1. AIt implies that Mrs Jackson threatened to resign because she wanted the promotion herself.✗ The argument says nothing about why she threatened to resign.
  2. BIt does not explain why it is so important to keep Mrs Jackson.✗ Why she matters is background. It is not part of the reasoning to “will not resign”.
  3. CIt overlooks the possibility that Mrs Jackson might resign even if Mrs List is not promoted.✓ “If List, then resign” does not mean “no List, no resign”. She could still resign for another reason (perhaps over Mr Wade).
  4. DIt overlooks the possibility that Mrs Jackson would not have resigned even if Mrs List had been promoted.✗ Questions whether she meant her threat, but the flaw is in the step from “List not promoted”.
  5. EIt overlooks the possibility that Mr Wade might not accept the promotion.✗ Even if Wade refused, they still say they will not promote Mrs List.
Tip Skeleton: if A then B; not A; so not B. That is a classic flaw (denying the first part).

7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer D
There is much evidence to suggest that cannabis has therapeutic uses for people suffering from conditions such as multiple sclerosis.Evidence / example Given this, the prescription of cannabis should be made legal.Main conclusion If doctors were legally allowed to prescribe cannabis, multiple sclerosis sufferers and others might be spared much pain. Moreover, if cannabis could be legally prescribed, it would be possible to conduct large-scale surveys to establish whether cannabis really is of benefit in such cases.Reason

Which of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AThere is no justification for existing laws against cannabis.✗ Too broad. It is only about prescription, not all cannabis laws.
  2. BCannabis can relieve the suffering of people with multiple sclerosis.✗ The evidence, and stated more firmly than “evidence to suggest”.
  3. CDoctors are opposed to existing laws on cannabis.✗ Doctors’ views are never mentioned.
  4. DIt should be legal for doctors to prescribe cannabis.✓ “Given this, the prescription of cannabis should be made legal.” Everything else is a reason for it.
  5. ELarge-scale surveys are needed to establish whether cannabis really is beneficial.✗ A benefit of legal prescription (the “Moreover” reason), not the conclusion.
Tip “Given this” points straight at the conclusion. “Moreover” adds another reason.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
Random drug-testing of prisoners was introduced in 1995 in order to solve the many problems associated with prisoners taking drugs.Context Since cannabis can be detected in the body up to a month after having been smoked, prisoners are tempted to switch to heroin, which stays in the system for only 48 hours.Reason As a result, since drug-testing was introduced, cannabis use has declined by a fifth whereas heroin use has doubled.Evidence / example Heroin is not only a much more damaging drug than cannabis, but it is also extremely addictive.Reason There is evidence that heroin addiction encourages prisoners to intimidate others in order to pay for the drug.Evidence / example

Which one of the following is a conclusion which can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AThe test for detection of heroin in the body should be improved.✗ A possible fix, but the passage never says how testing could be improved.
  2. BSince 1995, cannabis use in prisons is at a lower level than heroin use.✗ We only know the changes (down a fifth, doubled), not the actual levels.
  3. CThe drug-problem in prisons cannot be solved by random drug-testing.✗ Too strong. “Has not” is supported; “cannot ever” is not.
  4. DThe system of drug-testing of prisoners has not solved the drug-problem in prisons.✓ Testing was meant to solve the drug problems, but it pushed prisoners onto a worse, more addictive drug. So it has not solved the problem.
  5. EThe level of intimidation in prisons has doubled since the introduction of random drug-testing.✗ Heroin use doubled; nothing says intimidation doubled.
Tip Prefer the cautious conclusion. “Has not solved” beats “cannot solve”.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer D
It is not always clear whether a doctor should tell the truth to a patient or not.Context On the one hand, patients have a right to know what is the matter with them and what the future holds, so that they can make their own informed decisions, however upsetting the truth may be. It is a simple issue of human rights.Opposing view But on the other hand, a patient's health may sometimes be better served by not knowing a frightening truth.Reason For instance, a doctor may believe that a patient's medical condition will actually worsen if they realise how serious it is.Evidence / example Therefore, there are cases in which doctors have no choice: they must decide in favour of deceiving the patient.Main conclusion

Which of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. APatients accept that a doctor has a responsibility to decide what will be in their best interests.✗ What patients accept is not needed; the argument is about what doctors must do.
  2. BDoctors have a duty to conceal the truth if they believe it will frighten the patient.✗ Too broad. The reason is that health would worsen, not just that the truth is frightening.
  3. CDoctors have a duty to tell their patients the truth even when the truth would upset them.✗ The opposite of the conclusion.
  4. DDoctors have a greater responsibility for a patient's well-being than for their right to know the truth.✓ The passage weighs the right to know against health, then says doctors “must” deceive. That only follows if health matters more.
  5. EIt is the responsibility of doctors to respect a patient's human rights whatever their medical condition.✗ “Whatever their condition” goes against the conclusion that health can override rights.
Tip Two sides, then a verdict for one of them: the hidden assumption is that this side outweighs the other.

10(Q17 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer A
Birds of the tit family are very fond of tearing paper - wallpaper, newspaper, notices and bank notes included. They make no use of the paper they have torn.Evidence / example We might explain this puzzling behaviour by noting that the technique that they use for tearing paper is identical to their technique for stripping tree bark to search for insects to eat.Comparison This indicates that tits that tear paper are searching for food.Main conclusion

Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AOn many occasions tits reject food on bird tables to tear paper.✓ If they ignore real food to tear paper, they are hardly tearing paper to find food.
  2. BTits carry on tearing paper even when it is obvious to humans that no food is forthcoming.✗ Obvious to humans, not to the birds. They could still be looking for food.
  3. CEighty per cent of householders who reported paper tearing behaviour fed the birds.✗ Being fed by householders does not tell us why they tear paper.
  4. DTits use moss from trees and wool from dead sheep for nesting materials.✗ About nest material from moss and wool, but the paper is not used at all.
  5. EAnimals often engage in apparently pointless activities that turn out to have a purpose.✗ Says odd behaviour often has a purpose, which fits the argument.
Tip The conclusion is about a motive (food). Look for behaviour that clashes with that motive.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
The government has announced that it wants to reduce the level of ill-health due to workplace stress.Context Ministers could learn a lot from a recent study of 8000 white-collar workers in America which found that men who had a high level of control over the way they carried out their jobs had a low rate of heart disease, a stress-related disease.Evidence / example Clearly, therefore, the most stressful jobs are those in which employees have little control over the pace of their work and how it is organised.Intermediate conclusion If the government is serious about wanting to reduce the level of stress-related disease, then it needs to encourage employers to give their workers greater control over their work.Main conclusion

Which of the following is the best statement of a major flaw in the argument?

  1. AIt contradicts itself by implying that the government is not serious about wanting to reduce stress-related disease.✗ There is no contradiction; “if the government is serious” is just a condition.
  2. BIt assumes that in general workers want to be given more control over the organisation of their work.✗ What workers want does not matter to whether control reduces stress.
  3. CIt ignores the practical question of how much control workers can be given.✗ A practical issue, not a flaw in the reasoning.
  4. DIt assumes that where there was a low incidence of heart disease this was caused by a reduction in stress.✓ Heart disease can be stress-related, but low heart disease could have other causes (diet, exercise, wealth). It jumps from “less heart disease” to “less stress”.
  5. EIt assumes that stress in the workplace only affects white-collar workers.✗ It uses white-collar data but never says only white-collar workers are affected.
Tip Correlation (control + low heart disease) is treated as proof of one particular cause (low stress).

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer B
You can become a good pianist if you start to learn at an early age. You did not start to learn until you were an adult,Reason so you cannot become a good pianist.Main conclusion

Which of the following has the same structure as the above argument?

  1. AChildren can become fluent in two languages if they start learning when very young. My children learnt two languages when young, so they speak them fluently.✗ If A, can B; has A; so B. The condition is met, not missing.
  2. BYou can cure your headache if you are willing to try acupuncture. You refuse to try acupuncture, so you will not be able to cure your headache.✓ Same shape and same flaw: if A, you can get B; you don’t have A; so you can’t get B.
  3. CYou can avoid being overweight if you take sufficient exercise. You take sufficient exercise, so you will not become overweight.✗ If A, can B; has A; so B. Same as A.
  4. DChildren have fewer ear infections if they take cod liver oil. My children have very few ear infections, so they do not need to take cod liver oil.✗ If A, fewer B; already few B; so A not needed. A different shape.
  5. EYou can become a good singer if you have perfect pitch. You are a good singer, so you must have perfect pitch.✗ If A, can B; has B; so must have A. Works backwards from the result.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if A (start early) you can get B (good pianist). Not A (started as an adult) so not B.

The flaw: A is one way to get B, not the only way.

BIf A (try acupuncture) → can B (cure). Not A (refuse) → so not B. ✓ Same shape, same flaw.
AIf A (learn young) → can B (fluent). A (did learn young) → so B. ✗
CIf A (exercise) → can B (avoid overweight). A → so B. ✗
DIf A (cod liver oil) → fewer B (infections). Few B → so A not needed. ✗
EIf A (perfect pitch) → can B (good singer). B → so must have A. ✗
Tip Check the middle line: does it say the condition is missing (“did not”, “refuse”)? Only B does.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer C
If something I do results in someone's death, I am, of course, responsible for that death.Reason But am I similarly responsible if someone's death results from my failure to act?Context For example, if I go and shoot someone, their death is my fault. But if I refuse to give money to a starving person and that person dies as a result of not having money to buy food, is their death my fault?Evidence / example If I had never lived, the starving person would have died anyway, whereas the person whom I shoot would not have died had it not been for me.Reason So, doing something makes me more responsible for the consequences than when I fail to act.Main conclusion

Which of the following illustrates the principle that the author argues for in the above passage?

  1. AThe government's refusal to fund the crime prevention programme means that they alone are responsible for the high crime rate.✗ The reverse: it puts all the blame on a failure to act (refusing to fund).
  2. BMany people who are sent to prison commit further crimes when they come out, which shows that prison fails to make criminals feel responsible for their actions.✗ About whether prison makes criminals feel responsible. No act versus failure to act.
  3. CThe people more responsible for the high crime rate are the people who break the law, not the local authority for failing to support the crime prevention programme.✓ Law-breakers act; the local authority only failed to act. So the law-breakers are more responsible.
  4. DThe crime prevention programme does not work because it places the responsibility for crime on the local authority which has insufficient funds to implement it.✗ About why a programme fails (lack of funds), not who is more responsible.
  5. ECommunity service orders work better than prison because they focus the convicted person's mind on the direct consequences of anti-social behaviour.✗ About which punishment works better.

Working: write the principle first

Shooting someone (an act) my fault. Not giving money (a failure to act) they would have died anyway.
So the principle: doing something makes you more responsible than failing to do something.

CLaw-breakers (act) more responsible than the authority (failed to support). ✓ Same principle, new setting.
AThe one who failed to act is alone responsible. Opposite direction. ✗
BPrison and feeling responsible: no act versus omission. ✗
DA programme failing for lack of money. ✗
EWhich punishment works better. ✗
Tip Same topic (crime) in every option, so match the rule: whoever acts carries more blame than whoever fails to act.

14(Q27 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
The quantity of food produced has always fluctuated according to environmental conditions: gluts and shortages have been with us for five hundred years or more. In the past, the free operation of market forces limited the adverse effects of these fluctuations, usually with far more success than any attempts at planned responses.Context But now we are changing the environment irreversibly.Reason These changes will not only be permanent, but will have such devastating effects on world agriculture that market forces alone will be unable to prevent a global famine.Intermediate conclusion So it is now vital to seek some sort of planned response.Main conclusion

Which one of the following summarises the main conclusion of the argument above?

  1. AEnvironmental changes will have devastating effects on world agriculture.✗ A reason given for the conclusion.
  2. BMarket forces cannot prevent global famine.✗ The intermediate conclusion, and it drops “alone”: market forces alone cannot prevent it.
  3. CA planned response to current environmental changes is necessary.✓ “So it is now vital to seek some sort of planned response.” The rest leads up to it.
  4. DThe recent environmental changes cannot be reversed.✗ A reason (“irreversibly”).
  5. EMarket forces have limited the adverse effects of the environment on food production.✗ Background about the past.
Tip The last “So” gives the conclusion. The step before it (market forces alone will fail) is an intermediate one.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer A
Scientists are now developing genetically modified crop plants that produce their own pesticide.Context But pesticides are only effective if they are not overused; if they are applied continuously for long periods, then the pests that they are supposed to kill develop resistance to them.Reason Pesticides only remain effective against pests, therefore, if periods are left during which they are not used.Intermediate conclusion The genetically modified crop plants will produce pesticides continuously.Reason

Which of the following is a conclusion that can reliably be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AThe pesticides produced by the genetically modified crop plants become ineffective against pests.✓ Effective only with gaps; GM plants give no gaps (continuous). So their pesticides stop working.
  2. BThe genetic modification of crop plants will make the crops unusable, as they will be contaminated with pesticide.✗ Contamination and unusable crops are never mentioned.
  3. CThe genetically modified crop plants will help to prevent the overuse of pesticides.✗ The opposite: continuous production is overuse.
  4. DThe development of genetically modified crop plants will enable more effective use of pesticides, since periods may be left when they are not used.✗ Contradicts the passage. The plants produce pesticide continuously, so there are no gaps.
  5. EThe development of genetically modified crop plants will mean that pesticides need no longer be sprayed onto fields.✗ Not stated, and spraying may still be needed once the plant pesticide stops working.
Tip Two premises line up: “only if gaps” + “no gaps” → not effective.

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer D
In the case of plastic drink bottles, recycling is not the best answer to the problem of garbage disposal.Main conclusion Ninety five per cent of soft-drink bottles sold in the United States are made of a compound of polyethylene (PET) which poses recycling problems.Evidence / example They are potentially carcinogenic if recycled into new food or drink containers. Recycled PET tends to absorb other chemicals before re-melting and these chemicals could leach into food or drink that is stored in a recycled container. However, if disposed of in landfill sites, plastic is not harmful to the environment.Reason It can be squashed flat, so that it occupies little space and it is inert. Unlike other waste, it does not leach into soil or water, nor does it produce a dangerous build-up of methane.Evidence / example

Which of the following is an underlying assumption of the argument above?

  1. AThe space in presently available landfill sites will soon be exhausted.✗ This would weaken the case for landfill.
  2. BSoft-drink bottles should be made from materials which can be recycled.✗ A different suggestion. The argument compares recycling with landfill for the bottles we have.
  3. CThe burning of plastic rubbish is inadvisable because it produces toxic chemicals.✗ Burning is never discussed, and the argument does not need it.
  4. DApart from food and drink containers, there is little use for recycled PET.✓ The only problem given is recycling PET into food or drink containers. If it could be recycled into other things, recycling might still be best.
  5. EA reduction in the amount of plastic produced would ease the problems of garbage disposal.✗ Producing less plastic is a separate idea the argument does not rely on.
Tip Negation test: “recycled PET has lots of other safe uses”. Then the case against recycling falls, so D is assumed.

17(Q33 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
Catalytic convertors are increasingly being fitted to cars as a means of reducing some of the harmful emissions from car exhausts.Context But, though they eliminate one form of pollution, their action serves only to create another one.Intermediate conclusion Ground-level ozone is kept at very low levels in the cities by some of the pollutants catalysts remove.Reason So the more catalytic convertors that are fitted, the more such ozone can build up.Intermediate conclusion Since ozone is a major cause of choking summer smogs,Reason we can assume that people with breathing difficulties, such as asthmatics, will be much worse off the more that catalytic convertors are used.Intermediate conclusion We should stop fitting cars with catalytic convertors.Main conclusion

Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AMost cars are not fitted with a catalytic convertor.✗ Few fitted now does not change what happens as more are fitted.
  2. BCatalytic convertors are the best-known method of reducing harmful car emissions.✗ Says they work well, but does not touch the ozone harm the argument is about.
  3. CThe pollutants that catalytic convertors remove can create breathing difficulties.✓ If the removed pollutants also cause breathing problems, convertors help asthmatics as well as harm them. So they need not be “much worse off”.
  4. DThe number of cases of people with breathing difficulties has risen over the past few years.✗ More breathing problems, if anything, supports the worry.
  5. EAir pollution affecting people with breathing difficulties is also caused by emissions from industry.✗ Another source of pollution does not stop convertors making things worse.
Tip The argument only counts the harm of convertors. Look for a benefit to the same group (asthmatics).

18(Q34 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
Membership of the European Union (EU) has resulted in the maintenance of economic growth and political stability for the countries that make it up.Evidence / example It is desirable that the countries that used to be part of the Communist Eastern Bloc become comparably prosperous and stable.Reason Therefore the European Union should be enlarged as soon as possible to include more Eastern Bloc countries.Main conclusion

This argument is flawed. Which of the following identifies an error in its reasoning?

  1. AIt assumes that the Eastern Bloc countries have the potential for growth and stability that the existing members had prior to entry.✓ It worked for the existing members, so it will work for the Eastern Bloc? Only if those countries start from a similar position.
  2. BIt fails to acknowledge that there are countries outside the EU that are just as prosperous and stable.✗ Other routes to success do not stop EU membership being a good one.
  3. CIt relies on there being no opposition to the enlargement of the EU from existing member countries.✗ Opposition is a practical problem, not a gap in the reasoning.
  4. DIt implies that exclusion of the Eastern Bloc countries from the EU would result in their being unstable.✗ It never says staying out would make them unstable, only that joining would help.
  5. EIt assumes that the success of a political affiliation like the EU can be measured in purely economic terms.✗ It mentions political stability as well, so it is not purely economic.
Tip “It worked for them, so it will work for these” assumes the two groups are alike.

19(Q35 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer A
All's fair in love and war - and motor racing. That's how some commentators view the failed attempt by a great driver to disable his main rival's car by steering into it. Had he succeeded, he would have won the championship, because he was ahead on points.Context Fair? Why not?Main conclusion It may have been dangerous and irresponsibleOpposing view but it was not unfair.Main conclusion This driver had earned his one-point lead in the championship and was trying, like any serious competitor, to defend it. In a reversed situation, the other driver could - and arguably would - have done the same. Motor racing is a tough and uncompromising sport with huge incentives to win at all costs and all the drivers know what to expect.Reason

Which of the following best summarises the main conclusion of the argument above?

  1. AIt was not unfair of the leading driver to steer into his rival's car.✓ “It may have been dangerous and irresponsible but it was not unfair.” The three sentences after it are the reasons.
  2. BMotor racing is a fierce and ruthless sport with big rewards for winning.✗ The last reason given.
  3. CIt is not irresponsible to try to win at all costs.✗ The passage concedes it may have been irresponsible.
  4. DSome commentators thought that the race had been fought fairly.✗ Context: what some commentators think.
  5. EAnother driver in the same position would have acted in the same way.✗ A reason, and overstated: the passage says “arguably would”.

  2. BTo pass your exam you need to be hard-working and intelligent. You have done very little revision and your score in intelligence tests is very low. Therefore you are going to fail your exam.✗ Needs A and B, but both are missing. The original has one of the two.

DEither A or B (motorway or train). Not A → so B. ✗
