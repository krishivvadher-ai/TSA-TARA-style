1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusion
We need to accept flooding as a regular occurrence in the UK.Intermediate conclusion Statistics show that days of ‘extreme rain’ (very heavy rainfall) have become more common since 1960, moving from one in a hundred days on average in the 1960s to one in seventy days in 2015. The apparent trend mirrors increases in extreme rain seen in other parts of the world.Evidence / example Countries such as India and China expect and prepare for this,Comparison but in the UK we have to change our thinking and plan our infrastructure to cope with it.Main conclusion The damage caused to homes and businesses as well as the risk of injury and death through flooding are very significant.Reason Scientists say that, as the world has warmed by 0.7°C, the atmosphere is able to hold 4% more moisture, which means more potential rain.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the argument in the above passage?

  1. AThe UK has been underprepared for the effects of flooding.✗ Hinted at (“change our thinking”), but it is background, not what we are told to do.
  2. BThe UK could learn from countries such as India and China about flood defences.✗ India and China are a comparison. The passage never says the UK should learn their flood defences.
  3. CGlobal warming could be halted if there was political will.✗ Halting global warming is never discussed.
  4. DThe UK must plan its infrastructure to deal with a high risk of flooding.✓ “We have to … plan our infrastructure to cope with it” is what all the rain statistics and the damage point lead to. D rewords it.
  5. EThe world temperature will continue to rise in the future.✗ A prediction the passage never makes. It only says the world has warmed.
Tip The first sentence (“accept flooding”) is a step. Look for what we must do because of it.
2(Q3 in the original paper) Flaw
Researchers have discovered the first physical evidence of structural brain differences that distinguish early risers from people who like to stay up late.Evidence / example Around 10 per cent of people qualify as ‘morning people’ or ‘larks’, and a further 20 per cent are ‘night owls’ – with the rest of us falling somewhere in between.Context The brain differences might help to explain why ‘night owls’ seem to be at greater risk of depression than ‘larks’.Intermediate conclusion Brain scans have shown ‘night owls’ have a lower quality of white matter (tissue largely comprised of fatty insulating material that speeds up the transmission of nerve signals) in areas of the brain associated with depression.Evidence / example ‘Night owls’ should try to adjust their sleep patterns if they wish to avoid mental health problems.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt fails to refer to the brains of those who fall between ‘lark’ and ‘night owl’ types.✗ The in-between group is not needed to compare larks with owls.
  2. BIt ignores the fact that depression is very common in the population.✗ How common depression is does not affect the step from brain scans to advice.
  3. CIt assumes that changing one’s sleep pattern will cause a change in the quality of white matter.✓ The scans only show night owls have poorer white matter. The advice assumes that getting up earlier would change that brain tissue.
  4. DIt implies that ‘night owls’ would wish to change their sleep patterns.✗ It says “if they wish”, so it does not assume they want to.
  5. EIt associates depression with other mental health problems.✗ Depression is a mental health problem, so this link is fine. It is not where the reasoning breaks.
Tip Brain difference found → change your habit to fix it? Ask whether the habit causes the difference.
3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
There have been a growing number of calls to phase out the smallest denomination coins. The single penny is almost useless for purchasing items by itself. It would be simpler if prices were rounded to the nearest five pence and this would reduce the amount of small change carried around.Opposing view But this view does not take into consideration that this will inevitably lead to price increases.Reason Very few people would be concerned about paying £1 instead of 99 pence,Opposing view but it is not the consumer who benefits from the 1% price increase.Reason A single penny may not be worth much,Opposing view but those who save them do eventually have enough to purchase something useful. There are many charities that also benefit from these small coins as donations.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. ASmall denomination coins should be donated to charity.✗ Charities benefit from pennies, but the passage never says we should give them away.
  2. BCoins are an unimportant part of currency.✗ The opposite: the passage defends small coins.
  3. CPeople should not worry about price increases if small coins are withdrawn.✗ The opposite: the consumer does not benefit from the price rise.
  4. DPeople should be encouraged to save their small denomination coins.✗ Saving is one benefit mentioned, but no one is told to encourage it. It also leaves out charities and prices.
  5. EConsumers and charities would lose out if small denomination coins were withdrawn.✓ Each “but” gives a loser: consumers pay the rounded-up prices, savers lose their pennies, charities lose donations. E sums them up.
Tip Three “but” replies to the critics. A drawn conclusion should cover all of them, not just one.
4(Q5 in the original paper) Assumption
There is a lot more to interpreting and recognising faces than you might think. Forgetting people we used to know is not that uncommon, and is something we are in fact all guilty of.Context But next time you’re standing in the supermarket having a conversation with someone you don’t recognise but act like you remember them, take heed that the likelihood is that they will know you are lying.Intermediate conclusion Lying is a lot harder than most people realise, and hiding the fact we are lying is harder still.Reason This is because we all make brief, involuntary facial expressions when we try to conceal or repress something, such as trying to remember someone. These have been termed micro-expressions.Evidence / example So when someone you don’t recognise starts talking to you, you should just confess straight away because your micro-expressions will probably give you away anyway.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AWhen someone that you don’t recognise starts to talk to you, it is never because they have mistaken you for someone else.✗ “Never” is too strong, and confessing would still make sense if they had mistaken you.
  2. BPeople are able to recognise the micro-expressions that show that someone is lying.✓ We all make micro-expressions. They only “give you away” if the other person can spot them.
  3. CPeople would prefer to know that they have not been recognised rather than have someone pretend to know them.✗ The argument rests on being found out anyway, not on what people prefer.
  4. DThe person talking to you will not be embarrassed that you did not recognise them.✗ Embarrassment is never part of the reasoning.
  5. EPeople who are talking to someone that they know never lie.✗ “Never lie” is far too strong and has nothing to do with the advice to confess.
Tip Negation test: “people can NOT recognise micro-expressions”. Then nothing gives you away and the advice falls.
5(Q9 in the original paper) Weaken
The health service urgently needs more blood donors to meet demands on its ‘blood bank’ for transfusions. Most of us ignore this need, but we are only too glad to receive the blood of other donors in emergencies.Reason To boost the blood bank, transfusions should only be given to those who have indicated they are donors or agree to become donors.Main conclusion In this fairer system, receiving and giving within the blood service would become clear in everyone’s mind and the blood bank given all the donations it needs.Reason

Which one of the following, if true, weakens the above argument?

  1. ASome people are ineligible to give blood.✓ People who are not allowed to give blood could never qualify for a transfusion. That is not “fairer”, and they add nothing to the blood bank.
  2. BThere is greater demand for certain blood types.✗ Which types are in demand does not affect whether the rule would bring in more donors.
  3. CA high proportion of existing blood donors have themselves had blood transfusions.✗ If anything this fits the give-and-receive idea. It does not weaken it.
  4. DBlood donations are at an all-time low.✗ An all-time low makes the need more urgent: this strengthens.
  5. EMany people are wary of receiving blood because of infections.✗ Wary or not, they would still need blood in an emergency, so the push to sign up still works.
Tip The argument leans on the word “fairer”. Look for people the rule would treat unfairly.
6(Q10 in the original paper) Flaw
A recent study has found that those businesses which engaged more with social media had more financial success than businesses which did not. The businesses which were the most engaged saw their revenue grow by 18 per cent over the last year, while the least engaged saw losses of 8 per cent.Evidence / example Since social media is the future of communication,Reason it can be seen that businesses should interact more with social media in order to maximise their profits.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt ignores the fact that not all businesses use social media.✗ The study compares more and less engaged businesses, so that is already covered.
  2. BIt assumes that businesses that do not use social media will collapse.✗ No collapse is claimed. The argument is only about maximising profits.
  3. CIt assumes that participation in social media causes an increase in profits.✓ The study only shows engaged businesses did better. It may be the other way round: successful businesses have more to post about and money to spend on it.
  4. DIt ignores the fact that businesses can use other forms of communication.✗ The argument never says social media is the only way to communicate.
  5. EIt assumes that a growth in revenue of 18 per cent is possible for all businesses.✗ It says “maximise their profits”, not that every business will grow 18 per cent.
Tip Linked with ≠ caused by. Always ask which way round the cause could run.
7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusion
The apostrophe has limited usefulness as a punctuation mark,Reason so it will be no loss if it disappears altogether from the English language.Main conclusion The fact that it can’t be heard at all in speech suggests we can make our meaning clear without it.Reason It is already disappearing from much business correspondence without commerce grinding to a halt.Evidence / example The apostrophe is also widely misused, which is surely another measure of its unimportance.Reason Many people apparently think it belongs in any word that ends with an ‘s’, as demonstrated by this sign seen in an off-licence window: ‘Beer’s, wine’s and spirit’s at pre-budget price’s’. Yet judging by the queue in the shop, nobody had failed to get the message that there were bargains to be had.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AIt doesn’t matter whether we use the apostrophe correctly or not.✗ What the sign example suggests, so it is a step towards the conclusion.
  2. BIt will be no loss if the apostrophe disappears from English punctuation.✓ “So it will be no loss if it disappears”. Speech, business letters and the off-licence sign are all reasons for it.
  3. CThe apostrophe is particularly unnecessary in business correspondence.✗ Business letters are one piece of evidence. “Particularly” is never claimed.
  4. DThere is no sound in speech that corresponds to the apostrophe.✗ A reason: it “can’t be heard at all in speech”.
  5. EWe should all stop using apostrophes from now on.✗ Too strong. “No loss if it disappears” is not “we should all stop using it”.
Tip “So” in the first sentence flags the conclusion. Everything after it is backing.
8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
Scientists at University College London investigating the effects of bedtime routine on child development asked children to complete a number of tests including basic number skills, reading out word cards, and constructing designs from flat or solid shapes.Context Although the hour that children went to bed had little or no effect on their performance, having no set bedtime often led to lower scores. The effect was most striking in three-year-olds, where boys and girls scored lower on reading, maths and spatial skills tests than children of the same age who kept to a more rigid schedule.Evidence / example

Which one of the following is a conclusion that can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AThe more fixed the bedtime routine, the better the child’s intellectual development.✗ Too strong: the study compares set and no set bedtime, not a sliding scale of “more fixed”.
  2. BIrregular bedtimes may disrupt cognitive development in young children.✓ No set bedtime went with lower test scores, most of all at age three. B is suitably careful (“may”) and sticks to young children.
  3. CParents ought to take more care when thinking about their children’s bedtime.✗ A vague instruction to parents that goes beyond the findings. The hour of bedtime hardly mattered.
  4. DReduced or disrupted sleep, especially if it occurs at key times in development, could have important impacts on health throughout life.✗ Health throughout life was never tested. Only test scores were.
  5. EVarying a child’s bedtime routine is likely to lead to cognitive disorders.✗ “Cognitive disorders” is far too strong. Lower scores are not disorders.
Tip Drawn conclusions should be as cautious as the evidence. Here “may” beats “the more… the better”.
9(Q16 in the original paper) Assumption
The funding situation for educational provision in UK prisons is a national disgrace.Main conclusion Prisons provide free room and board, and inmates are frequently given access to the internet, cable TV, and gym facilities. At the same time, lengthy waiting lists exist for prison literacy classes, and ever increasing budget cuts put pressure on prison library resources. Meanwhile, it is estimated that at least half of UK prisoners possess a reading age of eleven or less.Evidence / example If society wants to reduce the chances of prisoners reoffending upon their release, we must give them the opportunity to develop the literacy skills they need to build a successful life outside prison.Reason

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. APrison is too easy for inmates.✗ The complaint is about education funding, not that prison is too comfortable.
  2. BThere is no educational value associated with watching TV or using the internet.✓ The passage sets internet and TV access against the lack of education. That contrast only works if TV and the internet teach prisoners nothing.
  3. CEducation is a right, not a privilege.✗ Rights are never mentioned. The reason given is cutting reoffending.
  4. DPrisoners have lower levels of intelligence than those of the general population.✗ Reading age is not intelligence, and no comparison with everyone else is needed.
  5. EPrison sentences should aim to rehabilitate criminals, not just punish them.✗ The last sentence is an “if”: if society wants less reoffending. It does not need to claim what sentences should aim at.
Tip When a passage sets two things side by side (“At the same time”), ask what it takes for granted about the first one.
10(Q17 in the original paper) Weaken
Some say that religion is crucial for providing us with a moral framework, for giving clear strong moral guidance. Without religion, some say, people might lose their sense of morality altogether.Opposing view But those who argue this ought to consider the Ancient Greeks.Reason The Greeks pondered obsessively the question of what makes a good life and how to live well. In fact the word ‘ethics’ comes from them. And although they had their own set of gods, those gods were hardly moral role models (Zeus himself was a serial adulterer). Moreover, there was nothing by way of an agreed religious text from which to obtain their moral rules. There was even an atheist movement in Ancient Greece.Evidence / example The Greeks alone are proof that if we are to lose our faith in God, this does not mean we must also lose our faith in morality.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ASome Greek gods were capable of great and heroic deeds.✗ Heroic deeds are not moral guidance. Zeus is still a poor role model.
  2. BThe Ancient Greeks took their moral guidance as much from poetry and drama as from religion.✗ Morality from poetry and drama is a non-religious source, so this if anything supports the argument.
  3. CMany Ancient Greeks questioned the value or purpose of being moral.✓ The Greeks are meant to prove morality survives without strong religious guidance. If many Greeks doubted the point of being moral, they look like the critics’ warning come true.
  4. DIn many parts of the world faith in God is currently getting stronger.✗ Faith today says nothing about whether morality needs religion.
  5. EBelievers often disagree about how best to interpret their own religious text.✗ If anything this shows religion does not give clear guidance, which helps the argument.
Tip The whole case rests on one example. Weaken it by showing the example does not fit.
11(Q21 in the original paper) Flaw
It is all very well for Europeans to expect African farmers and villagers to show the same enthusiasm for lion protection schemes as ‘wildlife tourists’ and armchair conservationists who deplore the killing or even the fencing-in of these noble and celebrated creatures.Opposing view But it is estimated that the average farm in Tanzania loses the equivalent of £600 a year in livestock due to lions straying outside the boundaries of the national parks where they are protected and growing in numbers.Evidence / example When out hunting, lions do not respect the difference between another wild animal and a domesticated beast that is owned by someone and often of priceless value to that person.Reason Most European countries would not tolerate the presence of huge, wild, man-eating cats roaming wild in the countryside, decimating their profits and killing their children. They have proved this by hunting many native species perceived as dangerous – wolves, bears, boar – to extinction, or by destroying their habitats.Comparison Why should Africa be different?Main conclusion

Which one of the following identifies a flaw in the above reasoning?

  1. AThe fact that other countries have failed to protect their wildlife heritage does not make it right.✓ “Europe wiped out its wolves and bears, so why shouldn’t Africa?” Others doing something wrong does not make it right.
  2. BFarmers in the UK are strong advocates of the culling of badgers to protect cattle from TB.✗ Another example of Europeans killing animals: it supports the comparison.
  3. CMany rural African communities are very poor and lions are a serious threat to their economic survival.✗ Backs up the argument’s point about the farmers’ losses.
  4. DWithout protection lions would soon become an endangered species in the wild.✗ A reason to protect lions, but an extra point, not a fault in the reasoning.
  5. ETourism has already suffered in Africa due to the scarcity of many large game animals.✗ Another reason for protection (tourism). It does not show the reasoning is faulty.
Tip A flaw answer names the faulty step. “They did it too” is a classic one.
12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoning
This essay is of a far higher standard than can be expected of undergraduates.Evidence / example Either the student is exceptionally intelligent and knowledgeable, or she has copied the essay from the internet.Reason Her previous essays have given no indication that she is more intelligent or knowledgeable than an average student.Evidence / example So she must be guilty of plagiarism.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AThis manager has an extravagant lifestyle with expensive holidays, which leads me to suspect that he has been stealing money from the company.✗ Jumps to one suspicion. No either/or and nothing ruled out.
  2. BMy partner has given me a lovely bouquet of flowers. He must be feeling guilty about something, because he only gives me flowers to celebrate special occasions or when he feels guilty, and this is not a special occasion.✓ Same shape: something surprising happens, only two explanations, one is ruled out, so it must be the other (guilt).
  3. CMy teenage daughter has cooked dinner and washed up without being asked. She must want something.✗ Jumps straight to one explanation. No second option is ruled out.
  4. DThe Prime Minister has admitted that a member of the Cabinet has been taking bribes. Either he has his own reasons for getting rid of the colleague or he knows that the evidence is too strong for him to be able to deny it.✗ Gives two explanations but rules neither out, so no conclusion.
  5. EThe standard of this essay is below what we have come to expect of you. Perhaps the topic was especially difficult. The only other possible explanation is that you were not trying hard enough.✗ Two explanations, but neither is ruled out. “Perhaps” is not a conclusion.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: surprising event (brilliant essay) only two explanations: X (very clever) or Y (copied) not X (earlier essays average) so Y (plagiarism).

BSurprising event (flowers) → only X (special occasion) or Y (guilt) → not X (“not a special occasion”) → so Y (guilty). ✓ Same shape, just in a different order.
ARich lifestyle → suspect theft. One guess, no either/or, nothing ruled out. ✗
CKind deed → must want something. One guess, no second option. ✗
DEither X or Y … and stops. Nothing ruled out, no conclusion. ✗
ESurprising event → X (hard topic) or Y (not trying), but neither is ruled out. ✗
Tip Look for all three parts: exactly two options, one ruled out, the other concluded. B hides the “only” rule in the middle.
13(Q23 in the original paper) Principle
It is both morally and economically wrong for the Government to give implicit approval to an employment system that lands the state with a huge bill to top up the incomes of the lowest earners.Main conclusion The holes in millions of pay cheques are being plugged by in-work support to the tune of £4 bn a year.Evidence / example But why aren’t those who are profiting from their workers paying up? Why is the Government having to subsidise businesses who don’t pay their employees enough to live on?Reason It is a question we need to answer and act on – fast.Intermediate conclusion The cost of living is rising but wages are not.Evidence / example In the rush for profit, and for high pay at the top, too many companies have forgotten the basic moral imperative that employees be paid enough to live on.Reason

Which one of the following is the best statement of the principle underlying the above argument?

  1. AThe Government is responsible for the employment system that leaves holes in millions of pay cheques.✗ A claim about who is to blame in this case, not a general rule.
  2. BIt is morally wrong of the Government to assist people on low incomes.✗ The argument objects to the state having to pay, not to helping the low paid at all.
  3. CHigh pay at the top end is responsible for low wages at the bottom end of the employment ladder.✗ A cause-and-effect claim the passage only hints at. It is not a principle.
  4. DAll pay should increase each year at the same rate as the cost of living.✗ Too strong. The argument asks for enough to live on, not for all pay to track the cost of living.
  5. EEvery worker should be paid at least a living wage by those who profit from their work.✓ The “basic moral imperative”: those profiting from workers should pay them enough to live on, so the state is not left to top it up.

Working: write the principle first

Businesses profit from workers but pay too little to live on the state tops it up wrong.
So the principle: whoever profits from someone’s work should pay them at least enough to live on.

EThose who profit pay at least a living wage. ✓ The rule itself.
ABlames the Government in this case. A fact claim, not a rule. ✗
BSays helping the low paid is wrong. The passage says the businesses should pay. ✗
CTop pay causes low pay. A causal claim, not a rule for action. ✗
DAll pay rising with prices. Much more than “enough to live on”. ✗
Tip The principle is often spelled out near the end: here “the basic moral imperative”.
14(Q27 in the original paper) Main conclusion
There is no point to re-enactments of historical battles.Main conclusion Despite the meticulous attention to getting the uniforms right and the cannon smoke,Opposing view the simulation of being there fails on every level:Intermediate conclusion there is no real danger of death and you know what the outcome will be.Reason It is merely an exercise in pageantry.Intermediate conclusion In history-as-lived, the participants at Gettysburg did not know that the Confederates were not going to win, while in a scripted re-enactment everyone knows their role and destiny from the outset.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ARe-enactments of historic battles are just colourful pageants.✗ An intermediate step (“merely an exercise in pageantry”) that supports “pointless”.
  2. BThe simulation of being there at a re-enacted historic event fails on all levels.✗ An intermediate step: the simulation fails, so there is no point.
  3. CRe-enactments of historic battles are pointless.✓ The opening sentence: “no point” = pointless. The failed simulation and “merely pageantry” are why.
  4. DNo insights into historic battles are gained through re-enactments.✗ “Insights” are never discussed. The passage is about the experience of being there.
  5. EPageantry is of no value in itself.✗ Never stated. At most it is taken for granted to get from “pageantry” to “pointless”.
Tip Here the conclusion comes first. Ask “why?” of each sentence: the one everything answers is the conclusion.
15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
Crazy as it seems, it looks as if lead poisoning could be the major cause of the rise and fall of violent crime.Intermediate conclusion Lead is so toxic that it is unsafe at any levelReason and in Europe it has been banned from many products, including paint and petrol, since the 1990s. Lead poisoning in infancy, even at very low levels, impairs the development of those parts of the brain that regulate behaviour and mood. The effect is stronger in boys than in girls. Lead poisoning is associated with attention deficit disorder, impulsiveness, aggression and, some think, psychopathy. Studies between cities, states and nations show that the rise and fall in violent crime follows, with a roughly 20-year lag, the rise and fall in the exposure of infants to trace quantities of lead. Virtually all studies agree there is a strong correlation.Evidence / example

Which one of the following could be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AEuropean countries could soon expect to see a fall in levels of violent crime.✓ Europe cut lead in the 1990s, and crime follows lead with a 20-year lag. So a fall in violent crime “could soon” be expected. Nicely cautious.
  2. BPossible causes of violent crime such as poor social conditions and drugs can now be discounted.✗ Too strong. A “major cause” still leaves room for other causes.
  3. CIf lead in petrol and paint is banned worldwide, violent crime will become almost unknown.✗ “Almost unknown” is far too strong. Lead is only one cause, and is in other products too.
  4. DCountries with less commitment to safety standards will be reluctant to ban lead from products.✗ Countries’ attitudes to banning lead are never discussed.
  5. EBoys who are exposed to lead in infancy are highly likely to go on to commit violent crimes in their early twenties.✗ Too strong. A link across whole populations does not make each boy “highly likely” to be violent.
Tip Combine two facts (1990s ban + 20-year lag) and keep the wording as cautious as the passage.
16(Q29 in the original paper) Assumption
The number one reason for people changing their jobs is to escape from ‘difficult people’, followed by seeking higher pay and promotion. In particular, many people cite a poor relationship with their boss as a reason for leaving. Typically, they’ll go to another job and find another difficult boss and the process continues.Evidence / example One of the areas that contributes to altercations with other people is the issue of unmet expectations. We easily see ourselves as being let down by the difficult person who fails to do what we expect them to. Very often these expectations are entirely in our own heads. We put the responsibility for knowing our expectations onto the difficult person and then blame them when they disappoint us.Reason If only people worked towards having honest conversations with difficult people, staff turnover might decrease.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AExpectations of other people are always realistic.✗ Contradicts the passage: our expectations are often “entirely in our own heads”.
  2. BDifficult people are aware that they are difficult.✗ Not needed. The fix is about us being honest, not about them knowing they are difficult.
  3. CPeople are not honest about their reasons for leaving a company.✓ The cure is “honest conversations”. That only helps if people are not honest now: they leave without saying what the real problem is.
  4. DStaff turnover is damaging for a company.✗ Not needed. The argument only says turnover might fall, not why that matters.
  5. EPeople cannot be trained to have honest conversations.✗ Training is never mentioned, and this would work against the suggestion.
Tip Negation test: “people ARE honest about why they leave”. Then honesty is already there and cannot be the cure.
17(Q33 in the original paper) Weaken
Some public institutions, such as the central bank and the constitutional court in many countries, are nominally independent but are actually captive to political interests.Intermediate conclusion Their leading officials are appointed by the government and these appointments are always the result of political power games. Moreover, it is fundamentally undemocratic to delegate too much power to people who have not been popularly elected.Reason Therefore, in order to ensure the real independence and the democratic nature of these institutions, the most prominent public officials should be elected in general elections.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AOnce appointed, the holders of independent public offices pursue strategies inconsistent with the political goals of those who appointed them.✓ If appointees go against the goals of those who appointed them, they are not “captive to political interests”. That removes a key reason for elections.
  2. BOpinion polls show that people are not interested in electing public officials leading independent institutions.✗ Lack of public interest does not touch the reasons about independence and democracy.
  3. CA politician vouches for the independence of a candidate to the head of the central bank.✗ One politician’s word proves nothing, and political involvement fits the argument.
  4. DA former judge of the constitutional court was recently jailed for electoral fraud.✗ One judge’s crime says nothing about whether appointed officials are captive.
  5. EHeads of central banks often have strong connections to political parties.✗ Party connections strengthen the claim that they are captive.
Tip Attack the reason: show that appointed officials really do act independently.
18(Q34 in the original paper) Flaw
When it comes to genius, the only possibilities are that it is something innate in people, or it is something to do with their upbringing. It is hard to imagine how simply bringing someone up in a certain way would be enough to turn them into a Mozart or a Shakespeare or an Einstein.Reason Mozart after all was writing complex music as a young child; it is difficult to explain how environment could have had such a significant influence by the age of five.Evidence / example It must therefore be the case that genius is something that is wholly innate.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt assumes that genius has to be evident at an early age.✗ Mozart is one example. The argument does not need every genius to show early.
  2. BIt is impossible to draw conclusions about questions of nature and nurture with such certainty.✗ A sweeping claim about the topic, not a statement of where this reasoning goes wrong.
  3. CJust because genius cannot be wholly explained by upbringing, this does not mean that upbringing is not an important factor.✓ The reasons show upbringing alone is not enough. The conclusion jumps to “wholly innate”, ignoring a mix of both.
  4. DThe argument does not specify exactly what is meant by ‘genius’.✗ A vague definition is not what breaks the step to “wholly innate”.
  5. EThe fact that something is hard to imagine does not mean that it is not true.✗ A real weakness, but even if upbringing alone cannot do it, “wholly innate” still does not follow. C is the bigger gap.
Tip “Not enough on its own” ≠ “plays no part”. Watch for “wholly” in a conclusion.
19(Q35 in the original paper) Main conclusion
The government’s decision to withdraw housing benefit from those living in council accommodation which has a spare bedroom has been a complete failure.Intermediate conclusion It has failed to bring in sufficient savings to the government and has brought hardship to those affected by the cut.Evidence / example The government should focus less on punishing certain people to make savings and more on implementing affordable universal childcare,Main conclusion which would allow many more people into the workplace and able to pay taxes.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AThe housing benefit cut affects vulnerable people.✗ Part of the evidence (hardship), and “vulnerable” is not the passage’s word.
  2. BThe government should implement a policy affecting more people.✗ Too vague. The argument names a specific policy: childcare.
  3. CThe housing benefit cut only tackles a few people.✗ Never stated. The cut failed because it saved too little, not because it hit few people.
  4. DThe government should target policy on providing universal childcare.✓ “The government should focus … more on implementing affordable universal childcare”. The failed cut is the reason to switch.
  5. EImplementing childcare would be a better way of generating money.✗ A reason (more people paying taxes), not the recommendation itself.
Tip “Should” sentences are often the conclusion. The “which would…” clause after it is the reason.

21(Q40 in the original paper) Assumption
Political legitimacy, the moral right to use state power, can only be derived from the consent of the governed.Reason So, in democracies that uphold this principle, regular elections are held to ensure that political power is legitimate.Intermediate conclusion Therefore, in these cases, the more voters participate in elections, the more legitimate the democratic political system is.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an assumption underlying the above argument?

  1. AElectoral turnout is always higher in democratic political systems.✗ No comparison with other systems is needed.
  2. BVoting at elections implies consent to the political system as a whole.✓ Legitimacy comes from consent. More voters only means more legitimacy if voting counts as consenting to the whole system.
  3. CThere is always a need for a government.✗ Not needed. The argument is about what makes power legitimate, not whether we need government.
  4. DThe more people who vote, the more the common interests are met.✗ “Common interests” are never part of the reasoning. Legitimacy rests on consent.
  5. ENon-democratic political systems rely on coercion as a basis of state power.✗ Non-democratic systems are not discussed, so nothing is assumed about them.

Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. AMany high street stores have been fined by the financial regulator for mis-selling financial products.✓ Fines for mis-selling show the worry is real: high street stores really have misled customers into financial products.
  2. BMany stores offer incentives to customers to encourage them to sign up for store cards.✗ Incentives are a sales tactic. They do not show staff misunderstand or mislead.
  3. CConsumers are supplied with full details of the terms and conditions for all financial products that they sign up for.✗ Full details help consumers decide for themselves: this weakens.
  4. DMany people do not understand the financial products they purchase yet still benefit from them.✗ People benefit even without understanding: this weakens.
  5. EMost people do not read the terms and conditions of products and services that they sign up to.✗ True of every product and every seller. It says nothing special about high street stores.

24(Q46 in the original paper) Parallel reasoning
In order for it to be possible to rain, clouds must be present.Reason The weather forecast is predicting clear skies,Evidence / example so there is no chance of rain.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AThe temperature in the fish tank must be at least 25°C for the fish to survive. The fish are all alive, therefore the temperature must be at least 25°C.✗ Starts from the result (fish alive) to show the condition is met. Valid, but the reverse direction.
  2. BSolar eclipses only occur at New Moon, when the Moon is between the Earth and the Sun. The new moon is not until next week, so there will be no Solar eclipse today.✓ Same shape: an eclipse needs a New Moon; there is no New Moon today; so no eclipse today.
  3. CA scab will only form when platelets in the blood have clotted. If you do not have any scabs, you do not have platelets in your blood.✗ Starts from no scab and concludes no platelets: runs it backwards (and “clotted” becomes “have”).
  4. DWhen outside temperatures fall below 0°C household pipes are at risk of bursting. So at a temperature of -5°C all your household pipes will burst.✗ Turns a risk into a certainty for all pipes. There is no missing condition.
  5. EDeciduous trees lose their leaves in the autumn. Autumn will be late this year, so trees will not lose their leaves until winter.✗ A timing prediction (late autumn, so late leaf fall). Not a missing condition ruling something out.

Working: necessary condition missing

Original: X (rain) needs Y (clouds) no Y (clear skies forecast) so no X (no chance of rain).

BX (eclipse) needs Y (New Moon) → no Y today (New Moon next week) → so no X today. ✓ Same shape.
AX (survive) needs Y (25°C) → X happened (fish alive) → so Y. Starts from the result. ✗
CX (scab) needs Y (clotting) → no X (no scabs) → so no Y (no platelets). Backwards, and invalid. ✗
DY (below 0°C) → risk of X (burst) → so X for certain, for all. Risk becomes certainty. ✗
EX (leaf fall) happens in Y (autumn) → Y late → X late. About timing, not a missing condition. ✗
Tip Valid pattern: X needs Y; no Y; so no X. Starting from the result (X happened) is a different shape, even when it is valid.

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  4. DModern crimes, such as internet hacking, should be the focus of police forces.✗ About police priorities, not about legalising anything.
