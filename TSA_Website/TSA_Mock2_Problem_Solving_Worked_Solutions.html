1(TSA 2015, Q30) League tablesAnswer D

Why it's herejudged the 14th-hardest of the 25. First work out how many games each team has left (they all play each other four times), then compare best possible finishes. Be careful with the tie-break: two teams can reach the leader's points total, but with different numbers of wins.

Towards the end of a sports season in which all teams in the league play each other four times1, the league positions are as follows:

PlayedWonDrawnLostPts
East Rovers1687131
West Athletic1473424
North Rangers1764722
South United17410322
Top Town1338217
Bottom Albion1536615

Three points are awarded for a win, 1 point per draw and 0 for a loss2. At the end of the season, if two teams have the same number of points, the league will be decided by the highest number of games won3.

How many of the competing teams could win4 the league when all the games have been completed?

  1. A1
  2. B2
  3. C3
  4. D4
  5. E5

Reading it properly

  • 16 teams: each plays 5 others × 4 = 20 games. Games left = 20 − played.
  • 2Best case: win every game left, +3 each.
  • 3Tie-break on wins. Count wins, not just points.
  • 4“Could” = is there any possible set of results where they finish top?

Working it out

  1. Best possible finish for each team (win every game left):

    TeamGames leftMost pointsWins then
    East Rovers431 + 12 = 4312
    West Athletic624 + 18 = 4213
    North Rangers322 + 9 = 319
    South United322 + 9 = 317
    Top Town717 + 21 = 3810
    Bottom Albion515 + 15 = 308

  2. Now the hurdle: East already has 31 points and 8 wins, and can’t drop below that. To win, a team must beat 31, or tie on 31 with more than 8 wins.

  3. East ✓ · West 42 ✓ · Top Town 38 ✓
    North 31 with 9 wins: ties East on points, wins the tie-break ✓
    South 31 with 7 wins: loses the tie-break ✗ · Bottom 30 ✗
    East, West, North, Top Town = 4 teams
    Check Best case for North: North wins its last 3, East loses all 4, and the others don’t pass 31. Nothing in the table stops that.
D4 teams: East, West, North and Top Town.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 1 assumes the leaders have already won it. They can still be caught.
  • B✗ 2 only counts the top two by points now and ignores Top Town’s 7 games in hand.
  • C✗ 3 misses North: it can only tie East on 31 points, but it would have more wins (9 against 8).
  • E✗ 5 includes South, which can also reach 31, but with only 7 wins it loses the tie-break to East.
Tip East is stuck on at least 31 points and 8 wins. Max points: W 42, T 38, N 31 (9 wins), S 31 (7 wins), B 30. Count E, W, T, N = 4.

2(TSA 2012, Q32) Shapes & tilingAnswer A

Why it's herejudged the 20th-hardest of the 25. One wrong piece passes both the area check and the angle check yet still cannot tile the shape, while the right piece only works in an unexpected arrangement, with the pieces turned and flipped different ways.

The trapezium below may be cut into four identical pieces1.

Which of the following pieces can be used four times (flips and rotations are allowed2) to make up the trapezium?

  1. Apiece A
  2. Bpiece B
  3. Cpiece C
  4. Dpiece D
  5. Epiece E

Reading it properly

  • 1Each piece must be exactly a quarter of the trapezium’s area, and the four must fit with no gaps.
  • 2You may turn pieces over, so mirror images are fine.

Working it out

  1. Put the trapezium on squared paper. Its height = its top edge, and the bottom is twice the top. Using 8 squares for the height:

    top 8, bottom 16, height 8
    area = (8 + 16) 2 × 8 = 96 squares
    each piece must be 96 4 = 24 squares

  2. Piece A is as tall as the trapezium (8), with a top of 1 and a bottom of 5, and its slope matches the trapezium’s sides:

    A: (1 + 5) 2 × 8 = 24 squares ✓

    Now fit four of them in:

  3. Quick checks on the others:

    • C is half of an 8 × 8 square, 32 squares: too big. (Three C’s make the trapezium, not four.)
    • D is about 29 squares: too big.
    • E is 6 × 4 = 24 squares, the right size, but its sides are 6 and about 4.5 long. The trapezium’s top edge is 8, and no mix of 6s and 4.5s makes exactly 8, so there would be a gap.
    • B has sides of about 5, 9 and 9: again nothing adds up to the top edge of 8.
    answer = A
AA: one in each bottom corner and two (one upside down) filling the middle rectangle.

Why not the others?

  • B✗ B’s sides (about 5, 9 and 9) can’t add up to the top edge of 8, so four B’s leave gaps. It is also a little too small.
  • C✗ C has area 32, a third of the trapezium: three C’s make it, not four.
  • D✗ D is too big (about 29 squares instead of 24).
  • E✗ E is the right size, but its sides (6 and about 4.5) can’t make the top edge of 8 exactly.
Tip Try A first: two in the corners, the middle is a rectangle that two A’s (one upside down) fill. Done.

3(TSA 2015, Q38) Reflections & clocksAnswer E

Why it's herejudged the 6th-hardest of the 25. Last night's clock was a reflection too, so the time you set your watch by was wrong, and mirroring a digital display both reverses the order of the digits and flips each digit (some digits turn into others). Several options differ by a single digit, so every step has to be exact.

I stayed in a hotel last night. My room was at the front of the building. At one point I looked out of the window and saw:

on an illuminated digital clock on the other side of the road. I took the opportunity to reset my watch1, which had stopped sometime earlier.

This morning, when I looked out, I realised that what I could see across the road was a reflection in the building opposite of a clock outside the hotel2. According to my watch (which I now knew I had reset incorrectly last night3) the time was supposedly nine minutes past ten4.

What did I see5 across the road when I looked out this morning?

  1. Adisplay A
  2. Bdisplay B
  3. Cdisplay C
  4. Ddisplay D
  5. Edisplay E

Reading it properly

  • 1He set his watch to 22:05, exactly what he saw. But that was a mirror image!
  • 2A reflection reverses left and right: the digit order flips and every digit is drawn back to front.
  • 3So the watch is wrong by a fixed amount. Find it from last night.
  • 4Watch says 10:09.
  • 5He still sees the reflection, so the answer is a mirror image.

Working it out

  1. Picture the street:

  2. Last night: 22:05 was a reflection. Flip it back: reverse the order and flip each digit (in a mirror a 2 looks like a 5 and a 5 like a 2):

    seen 22:05 real clock 20:55
    watch set to 22:05 watch is 1 h 10 min fast

  3. This morning:

    watch 10:09 − 1 h 10 min = real time 08:59

    Now mirror 08:59: the order becomes 9, 5, 8, 0 and each digit flips (9 back to front, 5 becomes a 2-shape, 8 and 0 unchanged).

    that is display E
EDisplay E: the mirror image of 08:59.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ A flips the 9 but forgets to flip the 5 (in a mirror it looks like a 2). Read back, A says 08:29.
  • B✗ Read back in a mirror, B says 09:02, not 08:59.
  • C✗ Read back, C says 09:29: wrong hour, and the 5 is not flipped.
  • D✗ Read back, D says 09:32.
Tip Mirror 22:05 → real 20:55, so the watch is 70 min fast. 10:09 − 1:10 = 08:59. Mirror 08:59: reversed order, 5 becomes a 2-shape: E.

4(TSA 2013, Q43) Timetables & speedAnswer C

Why it's herejudged the 23rd-hardest of the 25. You must read the right distance from the triangular chart, separate the normal stop at Uble from the extra delay, and then apply the faster speed and shorter stops for the rest of the journey.

There is a half-hourly train service that operates along the Rowbina Valley, between Erd and Teovil. Leaving Erd at 10 and 40 minutes past each hour, trains stop at Aregon, Lowley, Ergen, Uble and Gidion before arriving at Teovil 51 minutes after departure1 from Erd. In the opposite direction, trains leave Teovil at 20 and 50 minutes past each hour, arriving at Erd 51 minutes later.

The trains normally average a speed of 60 km/h, but this rises to 80 km/h if they are running late2. They stop for three minutes at each station en route, but this is reduced to two minutes when running late3.

Yesterday the 10.20 am train from Teovil arrived at Uble on time4, but because of a technical fault, remained at Uble for 22 minutes5. There were no further delays.

How many minutes late was yesterday's 10.20 am train from Teovil when it arrived at Erd6?

  1. A5 mins
  2. B8 mins
  3. C10 mins
  4. D13 mins
  5. E15 mins

Reading it properly

  • 1Check: 36 km at 60 km/h = 36 min (1 km a minute!), plus 5 stops × 3 = 15 min. 36 + 15 = 51 ✓
  • 280 km/h once late: 3 km takes 2¼ min instead of 3.
  • 3And each later stop is 1 minute shorter.
  • 4No delay at all before Uble.
  • 5A 22-minute stop instead of the usual 3: it leaves 19 minutes late.
  • 6Late leaving Uble, minus the time it claws back on the way to Erd.

Working it out

  1. Normal times (60 km/h = 1 km per minute). Teovil → Uble is 7 + 5 = 12 km:

    10.20 + 12 min running + 3 min at Gidion = arrives Uble 10.35
    normally leaves Uble 10.38 and reaches Erd 10.20 + 51 = 11.11

  2. Yesterday: it sat at Uble for 22 minutes.

    leaves Uble 10.35 + 22 = 10.57 19 min late
    Uble → Erd = 24 km at 80 km/h = 24 80 × 60 = 18 min
    stops at Ergen, Lowley, Aregon: 3 × 2 = 6 min
    arrives 10.57 + 18 + 6 = 11.21
  3. 11.21 − 11.11 = 10 minutes late
    Check Catch-up = 6 min on speed (24 → 18) + 3 min on stops (9 → 6) = 9 min. 19 − 9 = 10.
C10 minutes late: it left Uble 19 minutes late and clawed back 9 minutes.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 5 uses the faster speed and shorter stops for the whole route from Teovil (saving 14). But it ran normally until Uble.
  • B✗ 8 would need 11 minutes of catch-up. The last 24 km can only give 9 (6 from speed, 3 from stops).
  • D✗ 13 treats all 22 minutes as delay (forgetting the normal 3-minute stop), or forgets the shorter stops (19 − 6).
  • E✗ 15 would mean only 4 minutes clawed back, but the faster running alone saves 6.
Tip Leaves Uble 19 late. Last 24 km: normally 24 + 9 = 33 min, late 18 + 6 = 24 min, saving 9. 19 − 9 = 10.

The table below shows the medal count by the top five teams in a recent Mini Olympics.

teamsgold medalssilver medalsbronze medals
Amber876
Emerald5106
Garnet6610
Jade655
Sapphire1189

Each team calculated the proportion of their medals that were gold1.

Which one of the following charts, with appropriate labelling2, shows this proportion3 for each of the five teams?

  1. Achart A
  2. Bchart B
  3. Cchart C
  4. Dchart D
  5. Echart E

Reading it properly

  • 1Proportion = gold (gold + silver + bronze) for that team.
  • 2No numbers on the axes, so only the shape matters: the bar heights must be in the right proportion (from 0).
  • 3Work out the five fractions, then compare heights.

    Garnet 6 + 6 + 10 = 22: 6/22 ≈ 0.27

  3. Run through the charts:

    A: Garnet shorter than Emerald ✗
    B: these are the gold counts 8, 5, 6, 6, 11 ✗
    C: these are the total medals 21, 21, 22, 16, 28 ✗

  • B✗ Shows the number of golds (8, 5, 6, 6, 11), not the proportion. Sapphire towers over the rest.
  • C✗ Shows each team’s total number of medals (21, 21, 22, 16, 28).
  • E✗ Right order, but Emerald’s bar is less than half of Sapphire’s; it should be about 60% of it. The heights only fit if the axis started at about 11%, not 0.

A cube is labelled with six letters and, when viewed in two positions1, looks like this:

Which one of the following nets could be folded2 to make the above cube?

  1. Anet A
  2. Bnet B
  3. Cnet C
  4. Dnet D
  5. Enet E

Reading it properly

  • 1The same cube twice. A is in both views, so build everything around A.
  • 2Right faces and letters pointing the right way.

Working it out

  1. View 1: B sits on top of A, and F is on A’s right. Everything is the right way up.

    View 2: A is upside down. Spin this view half a turn so that A stands up again:

    • D, on top, drops below A and turns upside down.
    • E, on the right, swings to A’s left and becomes the right way up, with its arms pointing at A.

  2. Now draw A with its four neighbours:

    So opposite pairs are A–C, B–D, E–F. Every net gets these pairs right, so it all comes down to which way the letters point. Four checks:

    • ① A’s head touches B.
    • ② F is on A’s right; E is on A’s left with its arms pointing at A.
    • ③ D is under A’s feet, upside down.
    • ④ E and F stand up on the sides, so their tops touch B.

  3. Test the nets:

    A: E and F stand upright beside B, so their tops point up the page, not at B ✗ (④)
    B: turn it so A is upright: E is now on A’s right, F on its left. Swapped ✗ (②)
    C: E’s arms point at C, away from A ✗ (②)
    E: A is above B with its feet on B ✗ (①)
    D: B on A’s head, D upside down under A, tops of E and F point at B ✓ D
    Tip Moving a square round a corner of a net turns it a quarter turn. That is why E and F lie on their sides in net D but stand upright in the map.
DNet D: every letter touches the right neighbours and points the right way.

  • A✗ E and F sit beside B standing upright, so their tops point up the page, not at B. On the cube their tops touch B, so here they would end up lying on their sides.
  • B✗ With A turned upright, E is on A’s right and F on A’s left. View 1 shows F on A’s right, so this is the mirror image.
  • C✗ E’s arms point at C, away from A. View 2 shows E’s arms pointing at A.
  • E✗ A is above B with A’s feet touching B. On the cube B sits on A’s head.

7(TSA 2015, Q44) Dice & 3DAnswer D

Why it's herejudged the 8th-hardest of the 25. Every option passes the opposite-faces test and has the right handedness, so the only thing that separates them is which way one face's spots run compared with its neighbours. It is easy to reject a correct die because of how a symmetrical face is turned, which the original views never fix.

The die shown below is a conventional die, i.e. the total number of spots on any two opposite faces is always seven1.

Which one of the following could not2 be the appearance of the die shown above after a subsequent throw3?

  1. Adie A
  2. Bdie B
  3. Cdie C
  4. Ddie D
  5. Edie E

Reading it properly

  • 1Opposite pairs: 1–6, 2–5, 3–4. You never see both of a pair at once.
  • 2Four dice are the same die turned round. Find the one that can’t be.
  • 3Same die, any way up.

Working it out

  1. Read each option as top / left / right:

    A 2, 1, 4 · B 3, 2, 6 · C 4, 1, 5 · D 5, 3, 6 · E 6, 2, 4

    None shows an opposite pair (1–6, 2–5, 3–4), so that quick test rules nothing out. Look for a finer clue.

  2. The 1 and 5 look the same however you turn them. The 3 doesn’t: its spots make a diagonal line that points at two corners. On the given die the line runs into the top corner, where 3 meets 1 and 5. Its other end is the hidden corner where 3 meets 2 and 6.

    rule: the line of 3 always joins the 1–5 corner and the 2–6 corner

  3. Only B and D show a 3. Check where the line points:

    B: 3 on top, line runs to the front corner where it meets 2 and 6 ✓
    D: 3 on the left, line runs to the top corner where it meets 5 and 6 ✗ D
    Check A, C and E show no 3, and each is the die tipped over. For example C: roll the die so the 1 tips onto the left face; the 4 comes up on top and the 5 stays on the right.
DDie D: its line of three spots points at the corner shared with 5 and 6, which can’t happen on this die.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ A (2, 1, 4) is possible: it is the die tipped over twice, and with no 3 showing there is nothing to clash.
  • B✗ B is possible: the line of 3 runs to the 2–6 corner, just like the hidden end of the line on the original.
  • C✗ C (4, 1, 5) is the die rolled once so the 1 goes to the left face and 4 comes on top.
  • E✗ E (6, 2, 4) is possible: no opposite pair and no 3 to check, and it is the die tipped over again.
Tip Opposites don’t help. The 3’s diagonal must join the 1–5 and 2–6 corners. In D it hits the corner with 5 and 6: impossible.

The joined triangles are then folded and joined to make a pyramid2.

Which of the following could not be a view from above3?

  1. Aview A
  2. Bview B
  3. Cview C
  4. Dview D
  5. Eview E

  • 1The coloured side ends up outside, so you see the colours the right way round, never a mirror image.
  • 2Four triangles make a triangular pyramid: every corner has three faces meeting at it.
  • 3From above you see one corner and the three faces around it. Four options are real corners; one is impossible.

Working it out

  1. The pyramid has 4 corners. Three are the points in the middle of each side of the net, where three triangles already meet. The fourth is where the three outer tips come together.

  2. Folding doesn’t flip anything, so going clockwise round a corner you meet the faces in the same order as on the net:

  3. Read each option clockwise (start anywhere):

    A: G → Y → R = Y → R → G ✓ right middle
    B: R → Y → B ✓ bottom middle
    C: B → G → R = G → R → B ✓ left middle
    D: G → B → Y ✓ the tips
    E: R → B → Y ✗ bottom middle backwards
    E is a mirror image, so it can’t be seen E

EView E: it shows R, B, Y clockwise, the mirror image of the real corner (R, Y, B).

  • C✗ Possible: the corner at the left-hand middle point of the net (G, R, B clockwise).
  • D✗ Possible: the corner where the three tips meet (G, B, Y clockwise), the top when it stands on R.

10(TSA 2012, Q26) Folding & symmetryAnswer A

Why it's herejudged the 7th-hardest of the 25. You must reflect each hole across the fold before you can see what shape the opened holes make. The option that looks as if it obviously gives the most corners gives fewer, while one that looks impossible works because the holes are offset.

A sheet of paper has two holes1 cut from it. When it is folded in half2 the hole shown in the diagram below is seen.

Which one of the following could not3 be the unfolded sheet of paper? (The dotted line indicates where it was folded.)

  1. Asheet A
  2. Bsheet B
  3. Csheet C
  4. Dsheet D
  5. Esheet E

Reading it properly

  • 1One hole in each half of the sheet.
  • 2Folding flips one half over onto the other: the left hole becomes its mirror image.
  • 3You can only see through where both layers have a hole, so the octagon is the overlap.

Working it out

  1. Folding along the dotted line flips the left half over onto the right. Light only gets through where the flipped left hole and the right hole overlap. That overlap must be the octagon.

  2. Test the overlaps:

    • A: the left hole has its top-left and bottom-right corners cut off. Flipped, those become top-right and bottom-left, exactly like the right hole. The overlap is that same 6-sided shape, not an octagon ✗
    • B: a downward arrow over an upward arrow: the box parts and the points trim each other into an octagon ✓
    • C: square over diamond: the diamond trims the square’s corners into an octagon ✓
    • D: tall hexagon over wide hexagon: each trims the other’s points, an octagon ✓
    • E: flipped, the left arrow points left, so you get a left arrow over a right arrow, an octagon (like B on its side) ✓
    cannot be the sheet: A
AA: when folded, its two holes line up exactly, so the hole you see has only 6 sides.

Why not the others?

  • B✗ B works: the down-arrow and up-arrow overlap in an octagon.
  • C✗ C works: a square and a diamond overlap in an octagon.
