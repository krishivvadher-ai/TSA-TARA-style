1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusion
Leaders of enemy countries are often described by our own political leaders and by the media as “mad”.Context But some of the features that we associate with madness can be useful assets in a political leader.Intermediate conclusion Self-absorption, obsession, addiction to risk-taking and frenzied creativity all apply to some leaders agreed to have been “great”, as well as to those described as “mad”.Evidence / example People without some of those characteristics would be unlikely either to seek power or to be successful.Reason The label “mad” is cynically used as a way of dehumanising and discrediting leaders of countries with whom we are in dispute.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above passage?

  1. APeople who seek power do so for their personal aggrandisement.✗ Personal aggrandisement is never mentioned.
  2. BThere is agreement on which leaders are “great” or “mad”.✗ A detail from the evidence, and it overstates it: agreement is only mentioned for some “great” leaders.
  3. CA definition of a successful leader is one who can obtain and hold power.✗ The passage never defines a successful leader.
  4. DPolitical leaders have personality traits not normally found in the general population.✗ No comparison with the general population is made. The point is that “great” and “mad” leaders share traits.
  5. EThe use of the word “mad” is a propaganda tool rather than a genuine description.✓ The last sentence: “mad” is a cynical label to discredit enemies, not a real description. The shared traits of “great” and “mad” leaders are the reason.
Tip The “But” turns away from the media’s view. Ask what all the evidence builds up to: the last sentence.
2(Q3 in the original paper) Flaw
The decline in voter turnout at elections is chiefly explained by the decline in voting by younger people. Older people are much more likely to vote.Evidence / example If this trend continues, we will reach a point at which no government can be said to have a mandate to govern as the number of voters will be too small to indicate the wishes of the majority.Intermediate conclusion We need, therefore, to find an alternative to conventional elections so that the wishes of the people can be heard.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AThere may be problems in using alternatives such as internet and postal voting.✗ A practical snag with solutions the argument never names. It does not touch the reasoning.
  2. BYoung people may be more likely to join a pressure group than to vote in an election.✗ If anything this backs up finding other ways to hear young people. It is not a flaw.
  3. CAll European countries have seen a decline in voter turnout.✗ Falling turnout everywhere supports the worry rather than exposing a flaw.
  4. DElections are used in all democratic countries as a way of choosing a government.✗ That elections are used everywhere is just habit. It does not show the reasoning is wrong.
  5. EPeople who are young now may become more likely to vote as they get older.✓ It assumes today’s young non-voters stay non-voters. If they start voting as they age, like older people now, the “trend” may not continue.
Tip “Older people are much more likely to vote” is the clue: young people turn into older people.
3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
In today's 24/7 media environment, public figures have the ability to influence the behaviour of millions of young people, for better and for worse.Reason There are celebrities who don’t give in to social pressures and are in the news because of good deeds which have a positive influence on young people, such as giving to charity. However, there are others who glamorise destructive behaviour such as drinking, smoking, swearing and sexual promiscuity, giving young people the impression that this is socially acceptable or even desirable.Evidence / example Although many celebrities maintain that they do not wish to be regarded as role models,Opposing view it seems that this attitude is not only unrealistic but also irresponsible,Intermediate conclusion as their behaviour will undoubtedly be imitated.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AWe are living in an age of celebrity culture.✗ True as background, but it is not where the reasoning leads.
  2. BCelebrities should act responsibly because they are role models whether they like it or not.✓ Their behaviour “will undoubtedly be imitated”, so denying it is unrealistic (they are role models anyway) and irresponsible (so they should act responsibly).
  3. CLess media coverage of celebrities would stop young people imitating their role models.✗ “Would stop” is too strong, and less coverage is never suggested.
  4. DParents should monitor what their children are being exposed to in the media.✗ Parents are never mentioned.
  5. ECelebrities should not be regarded as role models.✗ This is the celebrities’ own view, which the passage calls unrealistic.
Tip The last sentence nearly gives it away: turn “unrealistic and irresponsible” into what celebrities should do.
4(Q5 in the original paper) Assumption
Over the years, many owners of businesses have appointed their sons to run the business when they retired or died.Context But this has been unfair to other workers in the company, some of whom were probably more talented and experienced than the boss’s son. It is highly unlikely that the son of the previous boss should happen to be the best candidate to take his place.Reason The practice of favouring your own family when making senior appointments in industry and commerce should therefore be made illegal.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AA talent for business often runs in families.✗ This would weaken the argument: the son might then be the best.
  2. BThe best candidate should always be appointed to be head of a company.✓ The son is “unlikely to be the best candidate”, so it is “unfair”. That only works if the best candidate is the one who should get the job.
  3. CFavouring your own family when making appointments is recognised as a corrupt practice.✗ The argument rests on unfairness, not on what people recognise as corrupt.
  4. DThere is no substitute for experience in acquiring the skills necessary to succeed in business.✗ Experience is one point (talent is another). The argument does not need it to be irreplaceable.
  5. EOwners of businesses should be free to make their own decisions about who should take over from them.✗ The opposite of the conclusion, which wants a law to stop it.
Tip Negation test: “the best candidate need NOT be appointed”. Then passing over better workers is not unfair, and the argument falls.
5(Q9 in the original paper) Weaken
In the UK, most people open a bank account in their teenage years.Context There is then a tendency not to change banks: eleven per cent of bank customers are still with the bank their parents set them up with when they were a child and haven’t changed it since.Evidence / example This strongly suggests that,Main conclusion contrary to what most commentators say,Opposing view people are not unhappy with the service that their banks provide.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ANearly two-thirds of UK bank customers have changed banks and two per cent have switched over five times.✓ Most customers (two-thirds) have switched, some many times. So there is no real “tendency not to change”, and all that switching hints at unhappiness.
  2. BBank customers say that they feel that all banks provide the same level of service.✗ If all banks are the same, they could be equally good. It does not show anyone is unhappy.
  3. CNearly two-thirds of customers say they are happy with their bank’s service.✗ Happy customers strengthen the conclusion.
  4. DBank services change as people get older and their needs change.✗ Explains why services change, not whether people are happy with them.
  5. EIt is not difficult to change banks.✗ If switching is easy, staying looks like a free choice, which strengthens it.
Tip Only 11 per cent stayed put. Look for the option that shows most people do switch.
6(Q10 in the original paper) Flaw
The UK Department of Education is encouraging pupils aged 14 to 18 to learn poetry by heart in order to recite it at a new national competition.Context This government initiative is designed to boost the confidence of young people and increase their awareness of poetry.Reason By taking part in this initiative and by reciting poetry, young people will improve their appreciation of poetry.Main conclusion

Which one of the following identifies a flaw in the above argument?

  1. AYoung people are already aware of poetry.✗ Even if true, it does not attack the jump from awareness to appreciation.
  2. BYou don’t have to recite poetry to appreciate it.✗ The argument never says reciting is needed to appreciate poetry, only that it will help.
  3. CAwareness is not the same as appreciation.✓ The aim is more awareness, but the conclusion claims more appreciation. Knowing about poems is not the same as valuing them.
  4. DThe young people who are willing to recite poetry are already confident.✗ About confidence, which is not what the conclusion claims.
  5. ELearning something in order to recite it is not the same as being creative yourself.✗ Being creative is never part of the argument.
Tip Compare the key word in the reason with the one in the conclusion. A swapped word (awareness → appreciation) is a classic flaw.
7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusion
Health clubs are booming.Context It is claimed that this must be a positive sign about modern society, in that people now appreciate that a healthy body is needed in order to sustain a healthy mind.Opposing view But that claim is mistakenMain conclusion because what those optimistic conclusions fail to appreciate is people’s ability to deceive themselves. Surely if someone is really keen to stay fit they go jogging rather than join a health club?Reason The health club phenomenon is just an example of how easily people can be swayed by marketing.Intermediate conclusion They pay to jog on the spot when they could jog around the park for free. The vast majority of members barely use their health clubs and feel they are achieving something just by paying the fees.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AHealth clubs deceive people into joining them.✗ Close to a reason (marketing), but the passage says people deceive themselves.
  2. BMost people who join health clubs are not getting healthy.✗ This is the evidence: members barely use their clubs.
  3. CPeople do not really appreciate that they must keep their bodies healthy.✗ Never claimed. The point is that people fool themselves, not that they don’t care.
  4. DThe success of health clubs is not a reliable sign of an improvement in people’s health.✓ “That claim is mistaken” means booming health clubs are not a good sign that people are healthier. D spells that out.
  5. EWe should judge the nation’s health by looking at the sales of jogging shoes not health club membership.✗ Jogging shoes are never mentioned.
Tip “It is claimed… But that claim is mistaken”: the conclusion is the rejection, so rewrite it in full.
8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
Increasing attention is being paid to the environmental consequences of the plastic ‘single-use’ shopping bags provided to customers by supermarkets and other retailers.Context Not only does the production of these bags represent a wasteful use of natural resources, but there are also problems surrounding their disposal.Reason For instance, the bags often end up as litter, and in the marine environment, they kill many animals that become entangled in them or mistake them for food.Evidence / example Numerous countries and individual cities have banned these bags,Context but experts on the environmental impacts of products warn that no perfect alternative exists.Reason Whether using fabric or heavier plastic, manufacturing bags that are stronger and thus more reusable requires significantly greater quantities of natural resources than the vilified ‘single-use’ ones.Evidence / example

Which one of the following is a conclusion that can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AEncouraging consumers to change their behaviour is not an effective solution to environmental problems.✗ Changing consumer behaviour is never discussed. The passage talks about bans.
  2. BProduct bans are never an effective solution to environmental problems.✗ “Never” is far too strong. The passage only says there is no perfect alternative.
  3. CThe solution to environmental problems lies in limiting human population growth.✗ Population growth is never mentioned.
  4. DRecycled paper bags should be used instead of plastic bags.✗ Paper bags are never mentioned, and the passage says no alternative is perfect.
  5. EWhen comparing two products from an environmental standpoint, it is important to consider their manufacturing processes, length of use, and disposal.✓ The passage weighs production (resources), disposal (litter, sea life) and reuse (stronger bags last longer but cost more to make). All three matter in a fair comparison.
Tip A drawn conclusion sums up the whole passage without new topics or strong words like “never”.
9(Q16 in the original paper) Assumption
Several cities around the world have introduced congestion charging schemes. Drivers are expected to pay a fee in order to drive inside the charging zone. In recent years, some schemes have offered discounts or entirely waived the charge for vehicles that cause less pollution.Context This is, however, an illogical and unfair policy that should not continue.Main conclusion Congestion is unaffected by emissions, and those cars that have low emissions still contribute to congestion. Those who cannot afford to buy a low-emission car are still expected to pay the charge even if the roads they drive on remain congested.Reason

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AThe principal purpose of a congestion charge is to reduce congestion.✓ Discounts for clean cars are only “illogical” if the charge is there to cut congestion. If its main aim were cutting pollution, the discounts would make sense.
  2. BIt is not important to try to reduce car emissions in cities.✗ Too strong. The argument only needs the charge to be about congestion, not that emissions don’t matter.
  3. CAll drivers would buy low-emission vehicles if they could afford to do so.✗ “All drivers” is too strong. The fairness point only needs some who can’t afford one.
  4. DCongestion charging has not had any positive effects where it has been used.✗ The argument attacks the discounts, not congestion charging itself.
  5. EIf everyone drove low-emission cars, nobody would have to pay the charges.✗ Not needed. The point is that clean cars still cause congestion.
Tip Negation test: “the main purpose is NOT congestion”. Then the discounts are logical and the argument falls.
10(Q17 in the original paper) Strengthen
We live in an information-rich world where communication is quicker and easier than ever before.Context We don’t have to wait for journalists and photographers to report news to us. Anyone can talk about or take photographs of significant events and share them immediately.Evidence / example Traditional ways of reporting meant that news could be effectively censored, but this is no longer the case. Similarly, incorrect information cannot be removed from the public domain without great difficulty.Reason Governments and the traditional media will therefore need to find new ways to cope with these challenges and adapt to new technology.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. ASome governments have successfully restricted the information available on the internet in their countries.✗ If governments can still restrict the internet, the old controls work: this weakens.
  2. BJournalists occasionally film material on their mobile phones.✗ A little adapting by journalists. It does not make the need for new ways any stronger.
  3. CInformation can be posted anonymously on the internet, thus avoiding possible prosecution.✓ Anonymous posting dodges prosecution, so the old tools (censor, prosecute) no longer work. That backs up the need for new ways to cope.
  4. DLegal proceedings have been brought against those publishing false and incriminating material on the internet.✗ Legal action works against false material, so the old methods cope: this weakens.
  5. EIn the past, individuals often took photographs that they then sold to the press.✗ About the past, so it suggests the situation is not so new.
Tip Strengthen: find the option that shows the old ways really can’t cope.
11(Q21 in the original paper) Flaw
Anyone who lends money to anyone else without a licence is committing an offence under UK law.Context Some unlicensed money lenders charge excessive rates of interest, and many use threats and violence to enforce payment.Evidence / example It is important to protect poor and vulnerable people from being exploited in this way.Reason So anyone convicted of this offence should be sent to prison.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt appeals to the reader’s emotions by describing some people as ‘poor and vulnerable’.✗ “Poor and vulnerable” is a fair description, not the faulty step.
  2. BIt draws a conclusion about all unlicensed money lenders on the basis of evidence about some of them.✓ Some charge too much and many use threats, yet anyone convicted goes to prison, even a fair lender who just lacks a licence.
  3. CIt makes a personal attack on the unlicensed money lenders.✗ It criticises what some lenders do. There is no personal attack.
  4. DIt restricts the options between licensed and unlicensed money lenders.✗ It never sets licensed against unlicensed lenders as the only options.
  5. EIt wrongly assumes that licensed money lenders do not charge excessive rates of interest.✗ Licensed lenders are never discussed, so nothing is assumed about them.
Tip Watch the quantity words: “some” and “many” in the reasons, “anyone” in the conclusion.
12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoning
Either young people are getting cleverer each year or examinations are becoming easier for students to pass.Reason Since there is very little evidence from IQ testing and other supposed benchmarks that young people are more intelligent now than in the past,Evidence / example we must conclude that the examination system is easier for students today than it was in the past.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AFarmers must choose between using organic or non-organic fertilisers on their land. Organic fertilisers can produce a lower crop yield and farmers may find it difficult to change to organic methods if they have been using non-organic methods for some time. Farmers should therefore consider the longer-term impacts of their decisions.✗ Two choices, but it lists drawbacks and ends with “consider the longer term”. Nothing is ruled out.
  2. BEvery year millions of holidaymakers decide whether to holiday in the UK or abroad. Since most of us like to have a degree of predictability in terms of holiday weather, we should not be surprised that, given the choice, most people would holiday abroad.✗ Two choices, but it picks one because of a reason for it, not by ruling out the other.
  3. CResearchers have looked at the communication between different animals and found that meerkats have a series of alerts and calls that fall into two categories, greetings and alarms. Since we can be sure that this meerkat is not calling as a greeting, we must accept that he is attempting to raise the alarm.✓ Same shape: only two options (greeting or alarm), one is ruled out, so it must be the other.
  4. DWood burning stoves are a better way to heat your home than an open fire. Wood burning stoves are highly efficient, whereas an open fire loses a great deal of heat up the chimney. More people should therefore install wood burning stoves in their homes.✗ Compares two options and recommends the better one. No elimination.
  5. EWhen allocating emergency appointments, receptionists prioritise children and the elderly above other patients. If you are neither a child nor elderly, you may have to wait longer to see a doctor.✗ A priority rule and what happens if you fall outside it. Not either/or.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: Either X (cleverer) or Y (easier exams) not X (little IQ evidence) so Y.

CEither X (greeting) or Y (alarm) → not X (“not calling as a greeting”) → so Y (alarm). ✓ Same shape.
AOrganic or not → drawbacks of organic → “consider the longer-term impacts”. No option ruled out. ✗
BUK or abroad → people like predictable weather → most go abroad. Picks Y for its own sake. ✗
DStove beats open fire → more people should install stoves. A comparison, not either/or. ✗
EChildren and elderly first → if you are neither, you may wait. A rule applied. ✗
Tip Look for both parts: exactly two options, then “not this one, so that one”.
13(Q23 in the original paper) Principle
A huge number of people have taken up cycling in recent years, and this is a welcome development.Context But if cycling helmets were made compulsory, it would deter people from cycling. There is risk in everything. And there is also such a thing as personal responsibility. It is not the role of the government to stop people from harming themselves.Reason Forcing cyclists to wear helmets is undue state interference in personal choices.Main conclusion

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AAlcoholics should not receive publicly funded treatment for their addiction because it is too expensive.✗ About cost, not about personal choice.
  2. BRecreational drugs should be legalised.✓ Principle: the state should not stop people making choices that risk harming only themselves. Legalising recreational drugs applies exactly that.
  3. CProfessional cyclists should promote the wearing of helmets.✗ Same topic (cyclists and helmets), but it is about encouraging, not state force.
  4. DThe government should combat cyberbullying by passing appropriate legislation.✗ The state stepping in to stop harm to others: the opposite direction.
  5. EIt is right for parents to be fined if their children are absent from school without permission.✗ The state punishing people over choices: the opposite of the principle.

Working: write the principle first

Helmets protect only the rider risk is part of life + personal responsibility the state should not force them.
So the principle: the government should not stop people from risking harm to themselves.

BRecreational drugs mainly risk the user → leave the choice to them → legalise. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
ARefuses help because it costs too much: a money reason. ✗
CPromotes helmets: no state, no choice taken away. ✗
DA law to stop people harming others. ✗
EThe state fining parents: more interference, not less. ✗
Tip Write the rule in general words (no bikes, no helmets), then look for it in a new setting.
14(Q27 in the original paper) Main conclusion
The Human Rights Act (HRA) should apply to soldiers in the UK and at their overseas bases but not to those fighting in the heat of battle.Main conclusion The concern is that the HRA could damage the effectiveness of the armed forces.Intermediate conclusion Commanders making split-second decisions in battle may be paralysed by fear that those decisions will become the subject of legal actions.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AThe HRA should apply to British soldiers away from battle situations but not in them.✓ The first sentence is the claim, and A rewords it: yes away from battle, no in battle. The rest explains why.
  2. BThe HRA could be a distraction to commanders in the heat of battle.✗ A reason (commanders paralysed by fear), not the conclusion.
  3. CThe HRA should not be applied to the armed forces.✗ Too broad. The argument wants the HRA to apply to soldiers in the UK and at bases.
  4. DThe need to make split-second decisions in battle is incompatible with the HRA.✗ “Incompatible” overstates a reason.
  5. EThe introduction of the HRA would undermine the efficiency of the armed forces.✗ A reason (“could damage the effectiveness”), made stronger with “would”.
Tip Here the conclusion comes first. “The concern is…” signals the reasons.
15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
Increased wealth in many regions and an ever-growing population have produced an unprecedented demand for fish.Context This increased demand is occurring at a time when over 70% of the world’s marine fish stocks are now overfished or already fished at full capacity.Evidence / example One possible solution is the continued expansion of aquaculture, a long-standing means of farming fish for human consumption. Controls must be put into place, however, to manage the potential environmental risks associated with fish farming, such as water pollution and transmission of disease to adjacent wild fish populations. Moreover, the aquaculture sector has yet to devise effective methods for rearing some commercially popular predator species, such as tuna, without having to feed them wild-caught fish, which contributes to the pressure on our oceans.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AWe should encourage people to switch from consuming marine fish species to consuming freshwater fish species, such as those living in lakes and rivers.✗ Freshwater fish are never mentioned.
  2. BNothing can be done to make fishing in the oceans less damaging to the state of fish stocks.✗ “Nothing can be done” is too strong. Fish farming is offered as a possible solution.
  3. CIn the long term, people may have to adopt a vegetarian diet due to the environmental problems associated with the production of all kinds of fish and meat products.✗ Goes far beyond the passage: meat and vegetarian diets are never discussed.
  4. DAt present, fish farms should avoid trying to rear predator fish, such as tuna.✓ Farming tuna means feeding them wild-caught fish, which adds to the pressure on the oceans: the very problem fish farming is meant to ease. So, for now, avoid it.
  5. EThe farming of only some kinds of fish is likely to result in environmental damage.✗ Pollution and disease are risks of fish farming in general, not of only some kinds.
Tip Look for the option that follows from the last sentence without adding anything new.
16(Q29 in the original paper) Assumption
A daily dose of chocolate could be the key to preventing Alzheimer’s disease, according to recent research.Main conclusion Blood flow to the brain was boosted and memory improved among volunteers who drank two cups of hot chocolate a day over a thirty day period. They were tested for memory and thinking skills, and ultrasound measured the blood flow to the brain. Participants showed an average 8.3% improvement in blood flow and memory scores improved, with recall times falling from 167 to 116 seconds on average.Evidence / example The study suggests that drinking hot chocolate regularly could help keep the brain healthy.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following is an assumption underlying the above argument?

  1. AEating chocolate would also have the same effect.✗ Not needed. The dose can be a drink, as in the study.
  2. BThe sample size may have been too small to be significant.✗ A doubt about the study, which would weaken it. An assumption supports the argument.
  3. CThe memory improvement would be permanent.✗ Not needed. A daily dose keeps the effect going, so it need not be permanent.
  4. DIf regular amounts of chocolate are consumed over time, other health problems could arise.✗ Side effects are a separate issue. The argument does not rely on them.
  5. EProblems with general brain functioning are linked to the development of Alzheimer’s disease.✓ The study shows better memory and blood flow, but the conclusion is about Alzheimer’s. The bridge is that general brain health is linked to Alzheimer’s.
Tip Find the gap between the evidence (memory, blood flow) and the conclusion (Alzheimer’s).
17(Q33 in the original paper) Strengthen
Couples who have more than two children are being ‘irresponsible’ by creating an unbearable burden on the environment, the government’s Sustainable Development adviser has warned. The adviser says that curbing population growth through contraception must be at the heart of policies to fight global warming.Reason Political leaders and ‘green’ campaigners should stop dodging the issue of environmental harm caused by an expanding population.Intermediate conclusion Each baby born in the UK will, during his or her lifetime, burn carbon roughly equivalent to 2½ acres of woodland – an area the size of Trafalgar Square. The UK population, now 62 million, will exceed 70 million by 2028. On average, women born outside the UK have 2.5 children compared with 1.7 for those born in the UK. The global population of 7 billion is expected to rise to 9.2 billion by 2050.Evidence / example Governments of both developed and developing nations must act to limit family size if they are serious about reducing energy consumption.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. ALarge families are the norm in many developing nations, often for good economic reasons.✗ Good economic reasons for large families are a reason not to limit them: this weakens.
  2. BMethods of reducing carbon footprint by other means are being developed.✗ Other ways to cut carbon make limiting families less necessary: this weakens.
  3. CMany babies are born to men and women who did not intend or wish to become parents.✓ If many babies are unplanned, contraception (the adviser’s method) could limit family size without forcing anyone. So the policy can work.
  4. DGovernments of developed nations will meet fierce resistance to plans to limit family size.✗ Fierce resistance makes the plan harder: this weakens.
  5. EMany men and women are unable to have children.✗ People who cannot have children do not affect the case for limiting family size.
Tip To strengthen a “must act” argument, show that the action would actually work.
18(Q34 in the original paper) Flaw
Television has had an adverse effect on family communication.Intermediate conclusion Why? Because instead of talking together, as they did in the past, families now tend to sit round the small, or not-so-small, screen. They watch and listen rather than discuss. They are passive rather than active. Instead of participating, they are content to be entertained.Reason Consequently, television dampens people’s ability to think for themselves.Main conclusion

Which one of the following identifies a flaw in the argument above?

  1. AThe initial assertion is contradicted by the conclusion.✗ Both claims are negative about TV, so they do not contradict each other.
  2. BThe reasons are not relevant to the stated conclusion.✓ The reasons are all about families not talking. The conclusion jumps to people not thinking for themselves. Watching quietly does not show that.
  3. CIt makes a generalisation on the basis of a particular example.✗ No single example is used. It talks about families in general.
  4. DIt assumes that what was right in the past is right for the present.✗ It mentions the past, but the faulty step is the jump from talking to thinking.
  5. EThe argument is circular in that it assumes what it sets out to conclude.✗ The conclusion (thinking) is not hidden in the reasons (talking), so it is not circular.
Tip Check the reasons and the conclusion are about the same thing: here, talking versus thinking.
19(Q35 in the original paper) Main conclusion
We need to change science lessons so that they give children a correct view of modern science.Main conclusion Children are often taught in science lessons that matter can exist in three different states – solid, liquid and gas.Context However, developments in science suggest that this is far too simple a picture.Intermediate conclusion Increased knowledge of the universe has shown that many other states of matter exist, for example, plasma and liquid crystal. Plasma is thought to be the state of 99% of matter in the universe,Evidence / example so the traditional states of matter are in a very small minority. So the traditional view is giving children a false, indeed unscientific, view of the universe.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ASchool science has failed to keep up with modern scientific developments.✗ Implied along the way, but it is a step towards the call for change.
  2. BSchool science gives an over-simplistic view of the nature of matter.✗ An intermediate conclusion (“far too simple a picture”).
  3. CSchool science gives children an incorrect view of the universe.✗ The last sentence: an intermediate conclusion that supports the need to change.
  4. DSchool science suggests matter is made up of three different states.✗ Background: what is taught now.
  5. ESchool science should be updated so that it reflects modern science.✓ The first sentence (“We need to change science lessons”) is the call to action, and E rewords it. Everything after supports it.
Tip A “We need to…” sentence at the start can be the main conclusion, with the “So…” sentences supporting it.
20(Q39 in the original paper) Draw a conclusion
A pioneering scheme started two years ago in Cambridgeshire assigns an individual caseworker to the most vulnerable homeless people. The caseworker helps the homeless person navigate through the health, justice and housing systems until his or her life improves.Context It has already shown that an average of £1000 a month per homeless person has been saved – money that would otherwise have been spent on providing emergency support.Evidence / example

Which one of the following is a conclusion that can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AIndividual case workers should be assigned to homeless people.✓ The scheme saves about £1000 a month per person, so the sensible conclusion is to assign caseworkers to homeless people.
  2. BVulnerable homeless people are costing too much.✗ “Too much” is a judgement the passage never makes.
  3. CSome systems are hard for homeless people to deal with.✗ Hinted at as background, but it is not what the evidence (the savings) leads to.
  4. DHomeless people rely on emergency support at present.✗ Background, and too general: the passage only covers the most vulnerable on the scheme.
  5. EThe lives of homeless people are improved when they can easily access support.✗ No evidence about lives improving is given, only money saved. “Easily access” is new.
Tip Ask what the evidence is evidence for. Money saved supports using the scheme.
21(Q40 in the original paper) Assumption
Government advice on alcohol drinking limits is based on studies carried out twenty-five years ago. It has often been expressed in terms of a maximum number of units per day.Context But advising people about a daily rate implies that drinking alcohol every day is acceptable,Reason when up-to-date research suggests that the body needs more ‘recovery time’ than one day.Evidence / example Health promotion advice should avoid being so simple that it inadvertently encourages unhealthy behaviour.Intermediate conclusion Better to have more complex messages than simple messages which promote unhealthy behaviour.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an assumption underlying the above argument?

  1. AThe research carried out twenty-five years ago is no longer valid.✗ Not needed. The problem is how the advice is worded (per day), not that the old research is wrong.
  2. BDrinking alcohol every day is common practice.✗ Not needed. A daily limit sends the wrong message even if daily drinking is rare.
  3. CPeople always follow the advice of health promotion messages.✗ “Always” is too strong. The argument only needs messages to have some effect.
  4. DThe government should regularly revise its advice on all health issues.✗ “All health issues” goes far beyond an argument about alcohol advice.
  5. EComplex health promotion messages will be read and understood.✓ Complex messages are only “better” if people actually read and understand them. If not, they help nobody.
Tip Negation test: “complex messages will NOT be read or understood”. Then they are not better, so E is assumed.
22(Q41 in the original paper) Weaken
Studies of fossilised remains indicate that, every 20 to 30 million years, life on Earth experiences a sudden and mass extinction of all or most of its dominant species. Yet, in each case, within some 5 to 10 million years the diversity of species on the planet is as great, if not greater, than it had been previously,Evidence / example since the demise of the dominant species allows more minority species to flourish.Reason Whatever disasters we may wreak on the planet, life in the long term will not only survive but actually increase in richness and diversity.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AThe consequences of a global nuclear holocaust are not known.✓ The evidence is about past natural disasters. “Whatever disasters we may wreak” includes nuclear war, whose effects are unknown, so the past may be no guide.
  2. BIt can be difficult to draw precise conclusions about dates from fossil records.✗ Exact dates don’t matter. The pattern of recovery would still stand.
  3. CHuman technology will soon be advanced enough to prevent most natural catastrophes.✗ Stopping natural disasters says nothing about recovering from the ones we cause.
  4. DSome species are able to withstand the most extreme forms of environmental change.✗ Tough species surviving strengthens the idea that life goes on.
  5. ESome species have died out due to dramatic changes in climate.✗ Species dying out fits the extinctions in the passage. It doesn’t touch the recovery.
Tip Spot the jump: natural extinctions in the past → any disaster we might cause.
23(Q45 in the original paper) Main conclusion
In the 1990s, there were two lines of argument against buying properties to let them out instead of living in them yourselves. One was the moral argument that you were driving the boom for your own profit and making houses unaffordable for everyone else; the other was the practical argument that houses were a risky investment.Context Despite these objections, the buy-to-let market continued to grow and now, twenty years later, the result is that the housing market is totally inaccessible to most of a generation. Rents in London have gone up eight times faster than wages.Evidence / example Plainly, no social good will come of this.Main conclusion Rents will continue to rise, and people who live in London will see their finances become more and more precarious.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ABuy-to-let has made housing unaffordable in London.✗ A reworded piece of evidence (the result so far), so it is a reason.
  2. BRent increases have exceeded wage increases in London.✗ Evidence: rents up eight times faster than wages.
  3. CNo social good will come of buy-to-let.✓ “Plainly, no social good will come of this”. The facts before it and the predictions after it both support it.
  4. DRents in London will continue to rise.✗ A prediction used as a reason.
  5. EInvesting in buy-to-let is immoral.✗ This reports the 1990s moral argument. The author never concludes it.
Tip “Plainly” often flags the conclusion, even in the middle of a passage.
24(Q46 in the original paper) Parallel reasoning
When either freedom of speech or freedom of assembly is present in society, democratic government inevitably results. If you live under a dictatorship,Reason you must therefore lack both of these key liberties.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the kind of reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AIf a house lacks both window locks and a burglar alarm, the insurance application will inevitably be turned down. Since your claim has been rejected, your house must have neither window locks nor burglar alarm.✗ Runs the rule backwards: “if neither, then C; C happened; so neither”.
  2. BIf heat or light is emitted by a body, then its energy decreases. It follows that, if a body’s energy remains constant, it has emitted neither heat nor light.✓ Same shape: if A or B, then C; not C; so neither A nor B. (Energy staying constant = no decrease.)
  3. CAnybody over 21 who holds a university degree or equivalent is eligible for selection. Since Greg is 19 and still an undergraduate, he cannot be eligible.✗ Uses “and” (over 21 and a degree) and denies the condition, not the result.
  4. DEither the fans are very loyal or they don’t mind the team losing week after week. No fans like to watch their team being beaten, so they must be very loyal.✗ An either/or with one option ruled out: a different shape.
  5. EWith water and sunlight this plant will thrive, since both water and sunlight are necessary for a plant's survival.✗ Treats necessary conditions as enough to make the plant thrive.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if A or B (free speech or free assembly) C (democracy). Not C (dictatorship) so neither A nor B.

BIf A or B (heat or light emitted) → C (energy decreases). Not C (energy constant) → so neither A nor B. ✓ Same shape.
AIf no A and no B (locks, alarm) → C (turned down). C happened → so no A and no B. Starts from the result. ✗
CIf A and B (over 21, degree) → C (eligible). Not A, not B → so not C. Denies the start, not the end. ✗
DEither A or B; not B → so A. Either/or elimination. ✗
EA and B are necessary → with A and B it will thrive. Necessary treated as enough. ✗
Tip Valid pattern: if P then Q; not Q; so not P. Here P is “A or B”, so “not P” means “neither A nor B”.
25(Q47 in the original paper) Principle
In a situation where every course of action is immoral, there still remains the ability to act for the greatest good for the greatest number.Reason Therefore, the outsiders ought to be eliminated at the least possible cost and with the least possible delay. I am sorry to arrive at that conclusion, but it is the right step.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle contained in the above argument?

  1. AThe swiftest and cheapest option is often the least immoral action.✗ Takes the “least cost, least delay” detail, which is how to do it, not the rule for choosing.
  2. BThe action that harms the least number of people is the best possible action.✓ “The greatest good for the greatest number” means picking the option that harms the fewest people. B states that as a general rule.
  3. CWhere all options are immoral, it is necessary to consider cost and speed.✗ Cost and speed again. The deciding rule is the greatest good, not cost or speed.
  4. DAn action cannot be considered totally immoral if it is the right action.✗ The argument says every option is immoral, yet one is still the right step. It never says the right step is less immoral.
  5. EAn action cannot be considered totally immoral if it is done for the greater good.✗ Same mistake as D. The argument accepts the action is immoral even though it serves the greater good.

Working: write the principle first

Every option is immoral still choose the greatest good for the greatest number so eliminate the outsiders (quickly and cheaply).
So the principle: choose the action that does the most good, and least harm, for the most people.

BFewest people harmed = best action. ✓ The same rule.
AFastest and cheapest = least immoral. Cost and speed are only how it is carried out. ✗
CAll immoral → think about cost and speed. Right situation, wrong deciding rule. ✗
DRight action → not totally immoral. The argument still calls every option immoral. ✗
EGreater good → not totally immoral. Mixes up “best choice” with “not immoral”. ✗
Tip Separate the rule for choosing (greatest good) from the details of doing it (cost, delay).
