1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer D
If people go to a foreign country, they should try to learn at least some of the language of that countryMain conclusion because,Reason whilst it is difficult to pick up a good command of a foreign language in a short time,Opposing view learning just a little of a foreign language helps you to find out more about the country itself, its people's customs and mannerisms. As well as this, it means that you can function more easily by being able to ask for directions or just by being able to order what you want at a restaurant, which is much less embarrassing than pointing and arm-waving.Reason

Which one of the following is an expression of the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AIt is easier to function in a country if you have learnt the language of that country.✗ A reason (functioning more easily), not the conclusion.
  2. BYou will find out more about a country if you learn the language of that country.✗ Another reason (finding out more about the country).
  3. CIt is difficult to learn a foreign language in a short time.✗ A point the writer admits on the way (“whilst it is difficult…”).
  4. DWhen people visit a foreign country they should try to learn some of the language.✓ The “should” comes first. Everything after “because” is a reason for it: finding out about the country and getting by more easily.
  5. ESpeaking a foreign language is easier than having to use sign language.✗ The passage says “less embarrassing” than arm-waving, not easier, and that is only a detail inside a reason.
Tip “Because” straight after a claim shows the claim is the conclusion.

2(Q3 in the original paper) FlawAnswer E
When the government issued its guidelines to the public to improve health by eating five portions of fruit and vegetables per day,Context it underestimated the possible negative consequences for farmers and the landscape.Intermediate conclusion The reduction in the demand for meat and dairy products would impact on farmers' livelihoods from raising cattle, and without cattle grazing, the countryside would become overgrown with weeds and gorse.Reason The government should stop telling people what to eat!Main conclusion

Which of the following expresses the flaw in the argument?

  1. AFarmers' profits and the landscape are not necessarily less important than public health.✗ This backs the argument up (farmers matter too). It does not expose a weak step.
  2. BEating five portions of fruit and vegetables per day is not necessarily beneficial to health.✗ The argument never relies on the guidelines being healthy. Its complaint is about farmers.
  3. CThe consumption of meat and dairy products is not necessarily bad for one's health.✗ The guidelines never say meat and dairy are bad, so this misses the reasoning.
  4. DLivestock and dairy farmers could not necessarily switch to production of fruit and vegetables.✗ If farmers could not switch, the harm to them is worse. That supports the argument.
  5. EThe increased consumption of fruit and vegetables will not necessarily result in the decreased consumption of meat and dairy products.✓ The argument jumps from “eat more fruit and veg” to “eat less meat and dairy”. People could simply add the five portions to what they already eat.
Tip Look for the hidden step between cause and effect: more of one food need not mean less of another.

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
One in ten adults in the UK has had a body piercing somewhere other than the ear lobe. 28% of these experienced complications and 1% were admitted to hospital, according to a survey of 10,000 adults.Evidence / example Body piercing is three times more common in women than men. Navel piercings are most common amongst women, whereas men are more likely to have a nipple piercing.Context Swelling, infection and bleeding are common side effects and tongue piercings are the most risky. Serious complications resulting in hospital admission mostly occur with piercings performed by non specialists rather than those carried out in a tattoo parlour or specialist piercing shop. In other countries, people have been infected with hepatitis B and C and HIV.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can reliably be concluded from the information above?

  1. AGood piercers give their clients advice about how to care for a piercing and minimise the risk of infection.✗ Aftercare advice is never mentioned.
  2. BNo one should ever get a body piercing because the risk outweighs the benefit.✗ “No one should ever” is far too strong, and benefits are never weighed. Most people (72%) had no complications.
  3. CNothing can reliably be concluded because the survey sample was too small to be representative.✗ 10,000 adults is a large survey. Nothing suggests it is too small.
  4. DPeople who want a body piercing would be well advised to go to a reputable piercer.✓ Serious complications mostly come from non-specialists, not tattoo parlours or specialist shops. So going to a reputable piercer is sensible advice.
  5. EThe Government should regulate body piercers more strictly to reduce complications.✗ Government regulation is never discussed, so it cannot be concluded.
Tip “Reliably concluded” means the modest option that one sentence of the passage clearly backs.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer D
In the UK 45% of the workforce is female, but the percentage of women in senior positions, both in the professions and in business, is considerably lower.Evidence / example This is unfair to women, and also bad for the economy, in that those who are promoted to senior positions may not be the most able.Intermediate conclusion Women may be discouraged from seeking promotion because employers require senior employees to work long and inflexible hours. Women may also be perceived as being unwilling to subordinate the demands of family to the demands of the job.Reason So employers must be urged to offer flexibility in working hours, and to seek to move away from the UK's culture of long working hours.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an assumption underlying the above argument?

  1. AMost women are not interested in seeking promotion to senior positions.✗ This would weaken it: the low numbers would then be by choice, not unfair.
  2. BMen would not object to the promotion of a higher percentage of women.✗ What men think is never part of the reasoning.
  3. CWomen would be better leaders than men if they were promoted to senior positions.✗ “Better” is too strong. The argument only needs women to be as able, not more able.
  4. DWomen are no less likely than men to have the abilities required for working in senior positions.✓ The low numbers are only “unfair” and only mean the promoted “may not be the most able” if women are just as capable as men.
  5. EWorking hours would be more flexible if employers promoted more women to senior positions.✗ Backwards: the argument says flexible hours would help women get promoted, not the other way round.
Tip Negation test: “women ARE less likely to have the abilities”. Then the gap is fair and the argument falls.

5(Q9 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
In some countries hunting clubs can arrange for members to hunt and kill just about anything that moves around on land. Despite the high costs of these hunts the clubs have grown in popularity as rich businessmen look for new ways to entertain their clients.Context Rare and endangered species of bears are often the target of the huntsReason and so there is a risk that these species will become extinct.Intermediate conclusion The growth of these hunting clubs should be opposed.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AOther popular activities such as motor sports also do considerable damage to the environment, but are not opposed.✗ Other harmful activities being allowed does not make this one acceptable.
  2. BThe high costs of the hunts mean that only a small number of people are taking part in this activity.✗ Even a few hunters can harm a rare species, and the clubs are still growing.
  3. CThe population of endangered bears has not changed significantly since the hunting clubs were formed.✓ The case rests on a risk of extinction. If bear numbers have not changed since the clubs began, that risk looks much smaller.
  4. DThere are plenty of alternative ways for the rich businessmen to entertain their clients.✗ If businessmen have other options, opposing the clubs costs little. That supports the argument.
  5. EMany hunters kill animals other than their target during the course of a hunt.✗ Killing other animals too adds more harm. That strengthens it.
Tip Find the reason the argument leans on (risk of extinction) and attack it head on.

6(Q10 in the original paper) FlawAnswer E
The battery life of laptop computers means that they cannot be used for the duration of a long haul flight without needing to be recharged from an onboard power point. Not all aeroplanes have this facility.Reason Therefore, laptop computers should not be allowed as carry on items for long haul flights on planes that do not have onboard power points, regardless of whether they are intended for personal or business use,Main conclusion as they will be of limited use to their owners and take up a disproportionate amount of space in the cabin.Reason

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. ASome companies expect their employees to work on long haul flights.✗ Employers’ wishes do not make the battery last longer, and the argument already says “regardless of… business use”.
  2. BThere is no chance of a laptop becoming damaged if it is stored in a suitcase in the hold.✗ Safety in the hold is not part of the reasoning (and would only support putting laptops there).
  3. CA laptop may run out of battery on a short haul flight, too.✗ Short-haul flights are not what the argument is about.
  4. DThe passenger cannot choose whether or not they book a flight with an onboard power point.✗ Whether passengers can choose their flight does not change how useful the laptop is.
  5. EPassengers could make some use of their laptop even if the battery does run out.✓ It treats “cannot last the whole flight” as “not worth having”. A laptop can still be used for hours before the battery runs out.
Tip Watch for an all-or-nothing jump: “not for the whole flight” becomes “of limited use”.

7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer A
How long will it be before we realise that force feeding our children with junk TV is just as bad as allowing them to eat nothing but junk food?Comparison Children are impressionable, we know that, so why do parents allow them to gain their insights into life through a TV screen?Reason I've heard so many 'concerned' mothers and fathers complaining about playground violence, petty crime and early sexual awareness in their children, yet they allow them to watch all sorts of rubbish on TV.Evidence / example Rather than blame outside influences over which they have no control, they should recognise they have answers closer to home - they're called common sense and a remote control.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ASome parents should do more to regulate what their children watch on TV.✓ The last sentence tells parents the answer is in their hands: common sense and the remote. In plain words, they should control what their children watch.
  2. BSome parents are not concerned by what their children watch on TV.✗ At most a hint about these parents. The point of the argument is what they should do.
  3. CSome parents are not genuinely concerned about the way their children behave.✗ The scare quotes round ‘concerned’ hint at this, but it is a side jab, not the conclusion.
  4. DSome parents do not have any influence over the way their children behave.✗ The opposite: the writer says parents have the answer “closer to home”.
  5. ESome parents do not believe that what their children watch on TV is causing them to behave badly.✗ Maybe true of these parents, but it describes them. The conclusion tells them what to do.
Tip “They should…” near the end is the recommendation. The rhetorical questions and the example lead up to it.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer C
When serious accidents such as train and plane crashes occur there are demands to identify and punish those responsible. Companies found guilty of corporate killing (“corporate killing” means the company as a whole is held responsible for deaths) in these accidents could be given huge financial penalties.Context But the point of punishment should be to ensure that safety systems are excellent and safety procedures are carefully followed. Financial penalties for the company do not have a great impact on individuals within the company, hence they do not make them feel a sharp sense of personal responsibility for safety. If the law allowed for jail sentences for the directors of these companies, this would provide the incentive to ensure the best safety systems and procedures.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AFinancial penalties should be imposed on companies found guilty of corporate killing.✗ The passage says fines don’t work well, so it would not recommend them.
  2. BTrain drivers and airline pilots should not be held responsible for serious accidents.✗ Drivers and pilots are never discussed.
  3. CThe law should allow for jail sentences for directors of companies which are found guilty of corporate killing.✓ Punishment should drive safety; fines don’t make individuals feel responsible; jail for directors would. So the law should allow jail sentences.
  4. DThe point of punishing directors of companies is to achieve justice for victims of serious train and plane crashes.✗ The opposite: the passage says the point of punishment is safety, not justice.
  5. ESafety systems and procedures are not the direct responsibility of directors of transport companies.✗ It goes against the passage, which wants directors to feel personally responsible for safety.
Tip “If the law allowed X, this would give the incentive we want” points straight to “the law should allow X”.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer B
'People who see America as 'The Great Satan' often forget how, whatever the nation's faults, it is truly heaving with free debate.Main conclusion After his re-election in 2004 the public and media began turning against George W Bush with a ferocity which matched only their bewildering support for him previously. Every day newspapers revealed polls that were more damaging for the president. The bookshops were packed with anti-administration best-sellers. At number one was Fiasco by Thomas E Ricks about, obviously, the war in Iraq. Nor are the shops just small, political outlets, you could find: 100 Ways America Is Screwing Up The World; Losing Our Democracy; How GWB Misleads America, and dozens of similar titles, even in major chain stores.'Evidence / example

The above argument rests on the implicit assumption that:

  1. Aif free debate is permitted, anti-administration feelings are bound to be expressed.✗ Backwards: the argument goes from anti-government feeling to free debate, not the other way.
  2. Bif popular opinion and the media turn against the administration, considerable free debate must be taking place.✓ All the evidence shows the public and media turning against Bush. That only proves “free debate” if turning against the government means free debate is going on.
  3. Cpolls are an accurate indicator of popular opinion.✗ Not needed: publishing damaging polls is itself a sign of free debate, accurate or not.
  4. Dby rights George W Bush should not have been re-elected in 2004.✗ “Bewildering” is a jibe, but the argument needs no view on whether he deserved to win.
  5. Ethe Bush administration, for all its faults, has encouraged free debate by the public and the media.✗ The argument says debate exists, not that the government encouraged it.
Tip Bridge the gap between the evidence (criticism of Bush) and the conclusion (free debate).

10(Q17 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
In European countries the incidence of cancer is higher than in non-European developing countries.Evidence / example This difference in cancer rates cannot be due to a greater genetic susceptibility to cancer amongst Europeans,Intermediate conclusion because statistics show that Europeans who migrate to developing countries decrease their chances of getting cancer and that those from developing countries who migrate to Europe increase their chances of getting cancer.Evidence / example Since there are major differences in diet between Europe and developing countries,Reason it is reasonable to conclude that the higher cancer rates in Europe are due to certain cancer-causing foods.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. ADiet is not a major cause of cancer in developing countries.✗ Fits the argument: their diet is less harmful, so their cancer rates are lower.
  2. BSouthern European countries have far lower cancer rates than countries in Northern Europe.✗ Differences inside Europe could also come from diet (northern and southern diets differ).
  3. CStress is a contributory cause of cancer and Europeans experience higher than average stress in their daily lives.✓ Stress is another non-genetic difference between Europe and developing countries. It could explain the cancer gap instead of food.
  4. DDiet is a contributory cause of many other health problems besides cancer.✗ Other health problems tell us nothing about the cause of cancer.
  5. EPeople who migrate from developing countries to Europe are relatively young and healthy.✗ Young, healthy migrants getting more cancer makes the change more striking. No other cause is offered.
Tip It rules out genes and jumps to diet. Weaken it by naming another difference that could be the cause.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
The problem of insect pests on crops is a difficult one and is often solved by spraying crops with pesticides.Context However, there are many other insects which survive by feeding on the pests that we are trying to kill. The pesticides also kill these insects.Reason It would be better to stop wasting money on pesticides and let other insects control the problem of pests for us.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the argument above?

  1. AIt ignores the fact that pesticides may be more effective at controlling the problem than these other insects.✓ It wants to swap pesticides for the pests’ natural enemies without showing those insects control the pests as well as pesticides do.
  2. BIt fails to consider the other problems that are associated with pesticides.✗ Other problems with pesticides would strengthen the case against them.
  3. CIt fails to establish that there is a positive benefit offered by allowing the insects to control the problem rather than using pesticides.✗ It does name a benefit: no more money “wasted” on pesticides.
  4. DIt does not establish that there are any problems for the crops presented by these other insects.✗ An argument does not need to show problems caused by its own solution.
  5. EIt offers no explanation to justify the statement that pests on crops present a problem.✗ That pests are a problem is common ground. It needs no proof.
Tip A “switch to this instead” argument needs the new option to work at least as well. Check whether that was shown.

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
There was a time when the economic situation demanded that the Government either had to support the currency directly in the foreign exchange markets, or else it had to raise interest rates. However, it could not raise interest rates because that would have been political suicide.Reason The Government therefore had to support the pound, costly though it turned out to be.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the kind of reasoning used in the passage above?

  1. AIf he had followed the doctor's advice and stayed at home he would have recovered completely by now. However, he couldn't take the time off, so he is still unwell.✗ “If X, then Y; not X; so not Y.” There is no choice between two options.
  2. BYou either have an allergy or you have picked up a virus. If it is a virus there is no treatment that will help, so you may as well treat it as an allergy.✗ Two options, but neither is ruled out. It just acts as if it were an allergy.
  3. CShe needed a painkiller and we had either paracetemol or aspirin. But, given that she was allergic to aspirin, she had to take the paracetomol.✓ Same shape: only two options, one is ruled out, so the other one had to be taken.
  4. DThis treatment is available on a doctor's prescription or over the counter at a pharmacy. However, it is cheaper on prescription, so I need to see my GP.✗ Both options are still open; one is picked because it is cheaper.
  5. EThere are two doctors at the clinic, a male and a female. The female is always pleasant but the man can be disagreeable, so I hope I see the woman.✗ Both doctors are possible; it only hopes for the nicer one.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: must do X (support the pound) or Y (raise interest rates) can’t do Y (political suicide) so had to do X.

CEither X (paracetemol) or Y (aspirin) → can’t take Y (allergic) → so had to take X. ✓ Same shape.
AIf X (stayed home) then Y (recovered) → not X → so not Y. No either/or. ✗
BEither X (allergy) or Y (virus) → if Y nothing helps → act as if X. Y is never ruled out. ✗
DX (prescription) or Y (pharmacy) → X is cheaper → choose X. A preference, not “had to”. ✗
EX (woman) or Y (man) → X is nicer → hope for X. A hope, not “had to”. ✗
Tip Look for both parts: exactly two options, then one made impossible. Cheaper or nicer is a preference, not ruling one out.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer E
There is a trend in climate discussions to devalue individual solutions such as wind energy, alternative fuels or investment in public transport, because each one by itself is not going to solve the climate crisis.Opposing view There is not, however, and will never be, any single solution to climate change. When there is no single solution to a problem, attempts to improve the situation should not be dismissed on the grounds that they do not work fully.Reason We should get past the bickering and move towards promoting any single initiative which will have any positive effect on climate change, however small.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle underlying the argument?

  1. ASchools should revert to traditional methods of teaching reading as new initiatives have failed to raise standards.✗ Drops new methods because they did not work: close to the attitude the passage rejects.
  2. BScientists, not governments, should be responsible for finding solutions to environmental problems.✗ About who should find solutions, not about partial solutions.
  3. CWorld peace can only be guaranteed by world government.✗ Claims one single solution exists: the opposite of the passage.
  4. DTackling the tide of obesity demands a national campaign to increase the amount of exercise people take even though this may damage their joints.✗ About accepting a side effect for a benefit, not about partial solutions.
  5. ECurfews should not be rejected on the grounds that on their own they will not solve the problem of anti-social behaviour.✓ Principle: when there is no single fix, don’t dismiss a measure because it can’t solve the whole problem alone. E applies this to curfews.

Working: write the principle first

No single solution exists so a measure should not be rejected just because it doesn’t solve the problem on its own back anything that helps, however small.
So the principle: don’t dismiss a partial solution for being partial.

ECurfews won’t solve anti-social behaviour on their own → don’t reject them for that. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
ANew methods failed → go back to the old ones. Rejecting, not keeping, what falls short. ✗
BWho should solve problems (scientists, not governments). ✗
COne single solution (world government): the opposite idea. ✗
DDo it even though it may cause harm. Side effects, not partial solutions. ✗
Tip The passage spells the principle out in its general sentence (“When there is no single solution…”). Find the option that copies it.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
Everyone is exposed to low background levels of asbestos and other mineral fibres in the environment, without any evident risk to health.Reason There is absolutely no reason to be concerned about it.Intermediate conclusion Exposure to asbestos fibres in homes and other buildings where asbestos is present and in good condition is not normally significantly different from the background exposure.Reason

Which one of the following is a conclusion which can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AAsbestos is harmless whether you encounter it in the home or in the environment.✗ Too broad: the passage only covers asbestos in good condition, and only “normally”.
  2. BThere is a serious risk to health if asbestos found in the home is in bad condition.✗ Asbestos in bad condition is never discussed.
  3. CThe dangers of using asbestos as a building material have been greatly exaggerated.✗ Using asbestos as a building material is never discussed.
  4. DThere is normally no reason to be concerned about asbestos in the home, if it is in good condition.✓ Background asbestos is no cause for concern, and good-condition asbestos at home is normally no different. So it is normally no cause for concern either.
  5. ERemoving asbestos from a building where it has been used is much more of a health hazard than leaving it in place.✗ Removing asbestos is never discussed.
Tip Keep the qualifiers: “in good condition” and “normally” must carry through into the conclusion.

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer C
Many drivers deliberately break traffic laws, both because they are convinced that there is little chance of getting caught and because, even if they are caught, the penalties do not act as a sufficient deterrent.Reason For example, people who would never think of stealing money even when they needed it, think nothing of routinely exceeding a 30mph limit even in a street where children are playing.Evidence / example It is clear, then, that a substantial reduction in road accidents can be achieved only by catching more motorists who break the law and by increasing the penalties for such law-breakers.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an assumption of the above argument?

  1. AThe number of road accidents is increasing because motorists are ignoring the traffic laws.✗ The argument is about reducing accidents. It does not need them to be rising.
  2. BDrivers who have been convicted of a traffic offence think nothing of continuing to break the law.✗ Repeat offending by convicted drivers is never part of the reasoning.
  3. CPeople who break the traffic laws are a significant cause of road accidents.✓ The reasons are about law-breaking; the conclusion is about accidents. Catching law-breakers can only cut accidents if they cause a significant share of them.
  4. DIf the penalties for stealing were less severe, people would think nothing of stealing money when they needed it.✗ The stealing example is about attitudes. Nothing is assumed about penalties for theft.
  5. EIf the penalties for traffic offences were increased, drivers would not break the law so frequently.✗ The conclusion says both steps are needed (“only by”), not that tougher penalties alone would work.
Tip Spot the switch of topic: law-breaking in the reasons, accidents in the conclusion. The assumption links them.

17(Q33 in the original paper) StrengthenAnswer A
Researchers studying the effects of diet on health sometimes use 'randomised intervention trials'. One group - the intervention group - is asked to change some aspect of diet, for example eat less fat or more fruit, while the other group - the control group - is told to eat as normal. At the end of the trial the number of cases of, say, colon cancer, in the two groups can be compared.Context Such results could be misleading,Main conclusion because as the study progresses, the 'intervention' subjects become less conscientious about sticking to their supposedly healthy diet, so the longer the study goes on, the less the diets of the two groups differ. Yet to get results that are statistically significant, the study must go on as long as possible.Reason

Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. A'Control' subjects often change their diet in response to health information in the media.✓ If the control group changes its diet too, the two groups’ diets end up even more alike. That makes the results even more likely to mislead.
  2. BDiets that are low in fat and high in fruit are known to be beneficial to health.✗ Whether the diets are healthy does not affect whether the trial can measure it.
  3. C'Intervention' subjects know that the changes in diet could benefit their health.✗ Knowing the benefits should help them stick to the diet. That weakens.
  4. DSome people have an inherited tendency to suffer from colon cancer.✗ Random groups share out inherited risk evenly. It does not touch the diets drifting together.
  5. EStatisticians who interpret the results take account of the effects of exercise and smoking.✗ Allowing for other factors makes results more reliable, if anything.
Tip The reason is “the two groups’ diets become alike”. Find another way that could happen.

18(Q34 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
When police raided a London nightclub known for selling illegal drugs, nine people were arrested. However, everybody was led outside, searched and photographed, whether or not drugs were found in their possession.Evidence / example Visiting a nightclub is not yet a crime,Reason so it is a troubling breach of civil liberties that the police should keep a record of these people.Intermediate conclusion Many of us are more comfortable with dealers operating in dark corners of nightclubs, rather than selling crack openly on busy daytime streets.Reason Therefore, we should simply accept that drugs will be sold in nightclubs and hope they can be contained there.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses a flaw in the argument?

  1. AIt assumes that the only alternative to excessive use of police powers and open drug dealing is acceptance of nightclubs selling drugs.✓ It rules out heavy-handed raids and open street dealing, then says we must accept nightclub dealing. Other options, such as better-targeted policing, are ignored.
  2. BIt does not follow that the police abused people’s civil rights by photographing them.✗ The argument gives a reason for this (visiting a club is not a crime), and it is a side step, not the leap to the conclusion.
  3. CIt uses one example of a nightclub where drugs are sold to draw the conclusion that nightclubs sell drugs.✗ It never concludes that nightclubs sell drugs. It says we should accept that they do.
  4. DIt uses extreme and illogical steps to move from one nightclub raid to the unlikely prospect of crack being sold openly on the streets.✗ It never says the raid will lead to crack on the streets. It only says which many people prefer.
  5. EJust because some people were not in possession of drugs does not mean that they were innocent of taking drugs.✗ Whether people took drugs is not the point. The argument is about keeping records of them.
Tip “We should simply accept…” after knocking out two bad options is a sign of a false choice.

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  2. BEveryone has a right to do what they want with their own private property.✗ The reason for the conclusion.
  3. CPublic opinion is strongly opposed to the sale of bodily organs for transplant.✗ Overstates “many people object”, and it is the opposing view anyway.

Which one of the following can be inferred from claims made in the passage?

  1. AIf cadmium and zinc were removed from sewage sludge, it could safely be used as fertiliser.✗ Other heavy metals could still be there (“some of which”), so we cannot say it would be safe.
  2. BThe greater risk to health from sewage sludge used as fertiliser would be its contamination with zinc.✗ Backwards: zinc kills the plants before they are toxic to animals. Cadmium is the hidden health risk.

Which one of the following is an assumption of the above argument?

  1. AForensic science is a foolproof way of determining guilt.✗ Too strong: the argument only says mistakes have “receded”, so it does not assume they are impossible.
  2. BReal justice can still be achieved even if there are occasional miscarriages.✓ Mistakes are now less likely, not impossible. Claiming hanging would “ensure real justice” needs occasional miscarriages to be compatible with real justice.

Asthma has increased globally by about 160% in the last two decades. In the UK alone, 1318 people have died from asthma attacks within the last year.Context However new research has shown that asthma rates fall significantly in those parts of cities which have more trees and green spacesEvidence / example indicating that we can reduce asthma attacks by a programme of tree planting in our inner city waste lands.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. APlanting more trees in inner city areas could lead to an increase in hay fever.✗ Hay fever is a different illness. It does not show trees fail to cut asthma.
  2. BThe cost of planting large numbers of trees in inner city areas could divert funds from other more important needs such as reducing crime in those areas.✗ A worry about cost. It does not show tree planting would not work.
  3. CUrban areas which have more trees and green spaces are invariably further removed from factories and congested roads which are known to trigger asthma.✓ Green areas are further from factories and traffic, which are known asthma triggers. That, not the trees, could explain the lower rates.
  4. DThere are fewer people living in areas with more green spaces so there are bound to be fewer cases of asthma.✗ The research is about rates, which already allow for how many people live there.
  5. EAsthma has increased by 160% in two decades but there is no evidence that trees have been removed at the same rate from urban centres.✗ The argument never says losing trees caused the rise in asthma.

24(Q46 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
Few tourists are likely to visit the town in the future.Main conclusion The Mining Museum is the only thing in the town that has ever attracted tourists in any substantial numbers. Now the Museum is closing.Reason

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AI cannot eat anything with wheat gluten in it. Unfortunately pasta is made with wheat and contains gluten. So I cannot eat pasta.✗ “Nothing with gluten; pasta has gluten; so no pasta”: a rule applied to a case. No cause is removed.
  2. BMy skin rash may have been caused by this washing powder. The rash came when I started using this washing powder and went again when I stopped.✗ Works out a possible cause from a pattern. No prediction is made.
  3. CThe only reason I get headaches is that I am allergic to dairy products. I am giving up dairy products, so I will probably stop getting headaches.✓ Same shape: the only cause of something is being removed, so that thing will probably stop.
  4. DIf Moira were stung by a bee and did not take these tablets immediately, she could die. So she will need to carry the tablets with her all the time.✗ A precaution against a risk, not a cause being removed.
  5. EEvery time I go into a house with a cat in it, I start sneezing. You have a cat in your house. I would start sneezing if I came in.✗ A cause is present, so the effect will happen: the reverse of the only cause being removed.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: only X (the Museum) brings Y (tourists) X is going (closing) so Y will probably stop (“few… likely”).

COnly X (dairy allergy) causes Y (headaches) → X removed (giving up dairy) → Y will “probably” stop. ✓ Same shape, same hedge.
ACan’t eat anything with gluten → pasta has gluten → can’t eat pasta. A rule applied to a case. ✗
BRash came and went with the powder → powder may be the cause. Finding a cause, not predicting. ✗
DSting without tablets could kill → carry the tablets. A precaution. ✗
ECat → sneezing every time → you have a cat → I would sneeze. Cause present, effect follows. ✗
Tip Match the key words as well as the shape: “only” and the hedge (“likely”, “probably”).

Which one of the following identifies the principle underlying the above argument?

  1. ANo one needs a servant in order to dress.✗ A fact the argument uses (people dress themselves), not a rule about what should happen.
  2. BWealth should not determine fashions in clothing.✗ Wealth explains the old habit, but the reason to change is ease for most wearers.
  3. CCost effectiveness should determine clothing design.✗ Cost is only history here. It plays no part in the reason for the change.

Right-side buttons suit right-handed people women’s went on the left for servants, who did the buttoning now almost everyone buttons their own so put them on the right.
