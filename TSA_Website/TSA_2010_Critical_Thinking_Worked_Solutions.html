1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer E
In the case of plastic drink bottles, recycling is not the best answer to the problem of garbage disposal.Main conclusion Ninety five per cent of soft-drink bottles sold in the United States are made of a compound of polyethylene (PET) which poses recycling problems.Reason They are potentially carcinogenic if recycled into new food or drink containers. Recycled PET tends to absorb other chemicals before re-melting and these chemicals could leach into food or drink that is stored in a recycled container.Evidence / example However, if disposed of in landfill sites, plastic is not harmful to the environment.Reason It can be squashed flat, so that it occupies little space and it is inert. Unlike other waste, it does not leach into soil or water, nor does it produce a dangerous build-up of methane.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the argument above?

  1. APlastic waste cannot be recycled safely.✗ Too strong (“cannot”), and it is only a reason. The passage says recycled PET is potentially risky.
  2. BPlastic waste is not a danger to the environment.✗ A reason in favour of landfill, not the overall verdict on recycling.
  3. CFood containers made from recycled plastic are potentially carcinogenic.✗ A reason (the health risk of recycling), not the conclusion.
  4. DThere is sufficient space in landfill sites to accommodate plastic waste.✗ Never claimed: squashed plastic takes up little space, but nothing says landfill has enough room.
  5. ERecycling is not the best method of disposing of plastic drink bottles.✓ The first sentence is the verdict, and E says it in other words. The PET problems and the landfill benefits are the reasons.
Tip The conclusion can come first. Ask which sentence all the others are there to prove.

2(Q3 in the original paper) FlawAnswer C
Every year in this country there are over 8000 personal accidents on golf courses, yet it is estimated that as few as five per cent of all golfers take out adequate insurance against claims for injury.Evidence / example If more golfers could be encouraged to take out appropriate insurance policies the number of accidents could be dramatically reduced.Main conclusion

Which one of the following identifies the flaw in the argument above?

  1. AIt ignores the fact that millions of golfers never have an accident.✗ Golfers who never have accidents do not touch the step from insurance to fewer accidents.
  2. BIt assumes that all insurance policies provide adequate insurance against claims.✗ The argument talks about “adequate” and “appropriate” insurance, so it does not assume every policy is adequate.
  3. CIt implies that the occurence of golfing accidents is causally related to the lack of insurance.✓ Insurance pays out after an accident. It does not stop one happening, yet the argument treats “no insurance” as a cause of accidents.
  4. DIt overlooks the possibility that some accidents would not be covered by insurance.✗ Covered or not, insurance still would not prevent the accident. This misses the real gap.
  5. EIt ignores the fact that there are diffferent kinds of insurance for different kinds of activity.✗ Kinds of insurance do not matter. No kind of insurance makes accidents rarer.
Tip Ask: would the suggested action really cause the result? Insurance changes who pays, not how often accidents happen.

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
In 1997 the UK Government banned the owning of handguns after a man went on the rampage, killing several people, with a collection of legally owned firearms.Context In the time since then the number of crimes involving firearms as a whole has increased. The number involving legally owned firearms has gone up by 0.1% as compared to a 5% increase overall.Evidence / example

Which one of the following could be reliably concluded from the above information?

  1. ABanning handguns has simply increased gun crime.✗ “Has simply increased” says the ban caused the rise. The figures only show the rise came after it.
  2. BThe number of legally owned firearms has decreased.✗ We are told about crimes with legal firearms, not how many legal firearms there are.
  3. CThe number of crimes involving handguns has stayed the same.✗ Handgun crimes are never counted on their own.
  4. DThe law change in 1997 has not resulted in a reduction in gun crime.✓ Gun crime has gone up since 1997. Whatever else the ban did, it has not brought gun crime down. That is all we can safely say.
  5. EIt has become easier to acquire illegal firearms.✗ A guess at why crime rose. Nothing in the figures shows it.
Tip “Reliably concluded” means no guessing about causes. Stick to what the numbers show.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
If drivers were made to pay for road use according to the amount of time they spent on the road, they would drive more dangerously.Intermediate conclusion Experiments were conducted with volunteers using a driving simulator. In the first experiment, the volunteers were charged for time spent driving below 10mph in order to see how people coped with trying to avoid congestion. In the second experiment, they were charged for the time spent in a particular area. The results were alarming. The volunteers in the first experiment jumped many red lights in an attempt to keep their speed up. Those in the second frequently broke the speed limit, and overtook in dangerous situations.Evidence / example In the interests of road safety, we should not introduce such payment schemes.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. ADriving simulators are a reliable guide to real-life driving.✓ All the evidence comes from a simulator. To say how people would drive on real roads, the simulator must match real driving. Negate it and the evidence is useless.
  2. BWe need to do more experiments on schemes of payment for road use.✗ The argument is already confident. It does not need more experiments.
  3. CMore people should practise their driving skills using a simulator.✗ Practising on simulators has nothing to do with the argument.
  4. DSchemes involving payment for road use will not solve the problem of road congestion.✗ Congestion is not the point. The schemes are rejected on safety grounds.
  5. ERoad congestion could be solved in ways other than payment for road-use schemes.✗ Other ways to tackle congestion are not needed for a road-safety argument.
Tip When evidence comes from a test, a lab or a model, look for the assumption that it reflects real life.

5(Q9 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
A two-month study of major producers of ivory products showed that worldwide demand for elephant tusks for ivory had fallen sharply.Evidence / example Consequently it is safe to assume that there will be a general decline in elephant poaching.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AThere are far fewer elephants than there were ten years ago, so poachers are finding it increasingly difficult to make a living from the ivory trade.✗ Fewer elephants and harder times for poachers point to less poaching. This strengthens.
  2. BPoachers now use high-powered rifles which make it far easier for them to kill larger numbers of elephants than was possible in the past.✗ Being able to kill more easily does not mean they will poach more when buyers want less ivory.
  3. CThe worldwide demand for ivory fluctuates considerably at certain times of the year, so poachers stockpile ivory to sell when the market improves.✓ A two-month study may have caught a seasonal dip. If poachers stockpile ivory for when the market picks up, they keep poaching even while demand is low.
  4. DMore and more synthetic materials are being used as substitutes for ivory in the construction of such things as piano keys and billiard balls.✗ Substitutes explain why demand has fallen. This supports the argument.
  5. EThe worldwide demand for ivory has been falling consistently for the last twenty years and many ivory poachers have found alternative sources of income.✗ A long fall in demand and poachers finding other work both support a decline in poaching.
Tip Check how the evidence was gathered: a “two-month study” is a short snapshot and may not show the real trend.

6(Q10 in the original paper) FlawAnswer B
Alcohol is largely to blame for a rise in the incidence of breast cancer.Main conclusion Research from St. George's hospital in Tooting shows the number of women with breast cancer rose significantly over a 10 year period. Researchers also found the average amount of alcohol women drank rose by more than 40% over the same period.Evidence / example The study used figures from a number of regional hospitals and compared drinking habits between 1997 and 2007.Context

Which one of the following best identifies the flaw in the above reasoning?

  1. AIt confuses actual amounts drunk by some women with average consumption.✗ Only averages are used. Nothing mixes them up with what some women drank.
  2. BIt gives a causal explanation when only a correlation is given.✓ Two things rose over the same years. That is a correlation. It does not show drinking caused the cancer, as other things changed too.
  3. CIt makes a sweeping generalisation on the strength of a very local sample.✗ The study used “a number of regional hospitals”, so it is not a very local sample.
  4. DIt assumes that all serious illnesses are related to lifestyle.✗ It makes a claim about one illness, not all serious illnesses.
  5. EIt fails to specify the amount by which breast cancer has increased.✗ “Rose significantly” is enough for the argument. The exact size is not the problem.
Tip “Over the same period” is a correlation clue. Ask: could something else explain both rises?

7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
Many people imagine that large country houses in the eighteenth century had smooth lawns.Opposing view But in those days there was no way of killing wild plants and weeds and, with the lawnmower not invented, the only method of cutting a lawn was by teams of gardeners with hand scythes;Reason today's weed-free close cuts were unthinkable.Intermediate conclusion Given this, lawns must have been like flowery meadows.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the argument above?

  1. AModern lawns are kept smooth only with the use of weedkillers and lawnmowers.✗ About modern lawns. The passage hints at it, but argues about eighteenth-century lawns.
  2. BThe upkeep of eighteenth century lawns required the employment of large numbers of gardeners.✗ A detail from the reasons (teams of gardeners), not the point being argued.
  3. CIn the eighteenth century, lawns were not smooth but more like flowery meadows.✓ “Given this… must have been” flags it: the lawns were like flowery meadows, not the smooth lawns people imagine.
  4. DIt is more natural to have a lawn like a flowery meadow than one kept in smooth condition.✗ The passage never says which kind of lawn is more natural.
  5. EModern lawns would seem boring to a person from the eighteenth century.✗ What an eighteenth-century person would think is never discussed.
Tip “Given this” is a conclusion signal, and it answers the opposing view in the first sentence.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer E
The siting of mobile phone masts is a controversial matter in the UK, particularly when they are situated at or near schools and hospitals.Context The Stewart Report concluded that the balance of evidence indicated that there was no general risk to the health of people living near mobile phone masts but recommended a regular audit of Radio Frequency Radiation (RFR) emissions from masts sited near schools and hospitals. The Government have complied with this latter recommendation and carried out regular audits all of which show that RFR emissions are far below the levels of national and international guidelines.Evidence / example Therefore, mobile phone masts are safe and not injurious to health and can be sited anywhere.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. ARFR emissions are more injurious to younger people.✗ This would make masts near schools less safe, so it weakens the argument.
  2. BRFR emissions can affect sensitive equipment in hospitals.✗ This counts against siting masts anywhere (such as hospitals), so it weakens the argument.
  3. CThe audits are not carried out frequently.✗ The passage says the audits are regular. This contradicts it.
  4. DMobile phones are an essential part of modern communications.✗ How useful phones are has nothing to do with whether masts are safe.
  5. EThe only health risk from mobile phone masts is RFR emissions.✓ The audits only check RFR. To conclude masts are safe in every way, RFR must be the only health risk.
Tip Evidence about one risk, then a conclusion of “safe”. The gap is that there are no other risks.

10(Q17 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer B
The expense of advertising adds greatly to overall production costs and thus to the prices of goods when they reach the public.Reason There is particular public resentment of mass advertising for rival brands of products such as petrol or detergents - which, most people suspect, are so similar in character as to be virtually indistinguishable except in their packaging.Evidence / example Much of the huge sum devoted to advertising could be used more fruitfully to bring down prices.Reason Therefore, advertising disadvantages the consumer.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, would most weaken the above argument?

  1. AManufacturers would not spend money on advertising unless they believed it would benefit the public.✗ What manufacturers believe does not show the public really benefits.
  2. BBy increasing competition, advertising leads to an overall improvement in the quality of goods.✓ If advertising pushes up quality, consumers get something back for the higher price. Then it may not disadvantage them at all.
  3. CAdvertising does not affect in any way what customers choose to buy.✗ If advertising changes nothing, the money is even more wasted. This strengthens.
  4. DThe advertising industry is able to employ people who would otherwise be out of work.✗ Jobs in advertising help workers, not consumers.
  5. EAdvertisements often make misleading claims about the quality of the product.✗ Misleading adverts are another harm to consumers. This strengthens.
Tip The conclusion is about the consumer, so a weakener must show a benefit to the consumer.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer C
Increasingly, the affluent middle classes are choosing private health care. This has been accompanied by steady decline in the National Health Service (NHS), evidenced by poor funding, understaffing, long waiting lists and substandard hygiene.Evidence / example The obvious solution would be to ban private healthcare,Main conclusion since its take-up by a privileged but growing minority is inevitably driving down standards in the NHS.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following is the best illustration of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AThe NHS has recently received funding increases.✗ Challenges a piece of evidence (funding). It does not expose the faulty reasoning.
  2. BThe NHS is not in decline, we simply expect more of it these days.✗ Denies the evidence rather than showing why the reasoning fails.
  3. CPeople might choose private health care because of decline in the NHS.✓ The two trends go together, but the cause may run the other way: people leave the NHS because it is declining. Then a ban fixes nothing.
  4. DPeople should have the freedom to choose the best healthcare option.✗ A separate objection to a ban (freedom of choice), not a flaw in the reasoning.
  5. EThe NHS would flourish if funds were not diverted to private health care.✗ This backs up the argument rather than finding a flaw.
Tip “Accompanied by” is only a link. Check whether cause and effect could be the other way round.

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
Amrik cannot afford to buy the Advanced version of the software. The Professional version is even more expensive,Reason so Amrik cannot afford that either.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AAmrik does not like foods containing garlic. This pizza contains garlic and anchovies, so Amrik won't like it either.✗ A “contains garlic” rule: garlic is in it or not. There is no “even more”.
  2. BIt is too far for Amrik to walk to the garden centre. The shops are closer, so he will go there instead.✗ Goes the other way (the shops are closer) and ends with a choice.
  3. CAmrik cannot sleep at night if he drinks a cup of tea after 9pm, because tea contains caffeine. Coffee contains more caffeine than tea, so Amrik won't drink that after 9pm either.✓ Same skeleton: one thing is already too much for Amrik; the other has even more of the same; so it is too much too.
  4. DAmrik didn't have enough patience to complete the 1000 piece jigsaw he got for his birthday. The Times crossword also requires patience, so he won't complete that either.✗ The crossword “also” needs patience, not more. It might need less, so it does not follow.
  5. EAmrik's hair is shorter, and Callum's hair is longer, than Bill's. So Amrik's hair is shorter than Callum's.✗ A chain of comparisons with no limit that Amrik fails to reach.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: X is already too much for Amrik (Advanced price) Y has even more of the same thing (Professional costs more) so Y is too much as well.

CX = tea’s caffeine, already too much after 9pm → coffee has even more caffeine → so no coffee after 9pm either. ✓ Same shape.
ADislikes anything with garlic → pizza has garlic → dislikes pizza. A “contains it” rule, not “even more”. ✗
BGarden centre too far → shops closer → goes to the shops. Less, not more, and a choice. ✗
DNot enough patience for the jigsaw → crossword also needs patience → won’t finish it. “Also”, not “more”. ✗
EAmrik < Bill < Callum → Amrik < Callum. Just comparisons, no limit. ✗
Tip The key words are “even more”. The match needs a limit Amrik already fails, then something with more of the same.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer D
We do not pay a fixed amount for electricity, gas or telephone calls irrespective of how much we use. How is it then that some people are still expected to do so in the case of water?Comparison People who use relatively little water should no longer be required to subsidise those who use a great deal.Intermediate conclusion Water meters should therefore be introduced in all regions as soon as it is feasible to do so.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle underlying the argument above?

  1. AOur town is providing a series of recycling banks so that residents who wish to recycle household waste can do so.✗ Recycling banks are a service for those who want them. Nothing about who pays.
  2. BWhen we eat out as a group we divide the bill by the number of people dining.✗ Splitting the bill equally is the opposite: light eaters subsidise heavy eaters.
  3. CThe government is paying compensation to house owners whose properties were damaged in the floods.✗ Compensation is about helping victims, not paying according to use.
  4. DThe new road is going to be financed by a toll system rather than out of taxation.✓ Principle: people should pay for what they use, so light users don’t subsidise heavy users. A toll makes the road’s users pay, not every taxpayer.
  5. EPeople who have difficulty paying the annual television licence fee may pay in monthly instalments.✗ Instalments change when you pay, not how much according to use.

Working: write the principle first

We pay for electricity, gas and calls by how much we use water should be the same light users shouldn’t subsidise heavy users so meters.
So the principle: those who use something should pay for it, in line with how much they use.

DRoad paid for by tolls (only drivers who use it pay) instead of taxes (everyone pays). ✓ Same principle, new setting.
ARecycling banks for those who want them. No paying at all. ✗
BEveryone pays the same share whatever they ate. The opposite. ✗
CCompensation for flood damage. About help, not paying for use. ✗
ESame fee, paid monthly. The amount still doesn’t depend on use. ✗
Tip Write the rule without water or meters in it, then look for it in a new setting.

14(Q27 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
Store loyalty cards are designed to encourage customers to use one supermarket only.Context But all the major supermarkets now have loyalty cards, so people are able to possess a loyalty card for all of their local supermarkets.Reason So it is pointless for the supermarkets to continue using them and they might as well abandon the costly schemes,Main conclusion because they cannot be the primary reason that people choose to shop at a particular store.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. APeople obtain loyalty cards for all of their local supermarkets.✗ Overstates a reason: people are able to hold every card, not that they all do.
  2. BLoyalty schemes are not the primary reason that people choose to shop at a particular store.✗ An intermediate conclusion. It comes after “because”, so it supports the main point.
  3. CSupermarkets might as well abandon loyalty schemes.✓ Everything leads to “they might as well abandon the costly schemes”. The “because” clause after it is a reason for it.
  4. DLoyalty card schemes cost too much to be worthwhile.✗ The schemes are called costly, but the reason given is that they are pointless, not too expensive.
  5. EStore loyalty cards are designed to encourage customers to use one supermarket only.✗ The opening context about what the cards are for.
Tip A “because” clause straight after a claim is a reason, even when it comes last.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer E
The demand for blood donors is increasing all over the world. In Western countries, in particular, demand has been rising so rapidly that shortages have begun to appear. In all such countries, demand is growing much faster than rates of growth in populations aged 18-65 from whom donors are drawn. And, despite a massive research effort to find alternatives, it remains true that in medicine there is no substitute for human blood.Evidence / example

Which one of the following conclusions can be drawn from the passage?

  1. AAs the demand for blood has increased, so has the supply fallen.✗ Supply may still be rising, just more slowly than demand. A fall is never stated.
  2. BThe rate of growth of the blood-donor population has been slowing recently.✗ We only know demand grows faster than the donor population, not that its growth is slowing.
  3. CThe increase in the rate of demand for blood is mainly due to population growth.✗ The reverse: demand grows much faster than the population, so population growth cannot be the main reason.
  4. DIf more blood donors could be found, there would be no need to find a substitute for human blood.✗ Too strong. The passage never says enough donors would end the search for a substitute.
  5. EThe problem of the increase in demand for blood shows no sign of disappearing.✓ Demand keeps rising faster than the donor population, and there is no substitute. So the problem is not going away.
Tip Pick the option that only joins up the facts given: rising demand + no substitute = the problem stays.

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
Powdered rhinoceros horn has often been claimed to have aphrodisiac properties.Opposing view However, a recent investigation showed no evidence that it has any chemical effect whatsoever on the human nervous system. What is more, in an experiment in which 100 people were given powdered rhinoceros horn and another 100 people were given powdered rice, without knowing what they were taking, more of those taking the rice indicated that they felt an increase in sexual arousal than did those taking the rhinoceros horn.Evidence / example This demonstrates conclusively that rhinoceros horn is not an aphrodisiac.Main conclusion

Which one of the above is an underlying assumption of the argument in the passage?

  1. APowdered rice does not have significant aphrodisiac properties.✓ Rice is the comparison. Rhino horn doing worse than rice only shows it is not an aphrodisiac if rice is not one either.
  2. BClaims made about aphrodisiacs are in general untrustworthy.✗ The argument rests on its own evidence, not on aphrodisiac claims in general.
  3. CPowdered rice has no effect on the human nervous system.✗ Stronger than needed. Rice may affect the nervous system in other ways; it just must not be an aphrodisiac.
  4. DThe claims about its aphrodisiac properties are spread to create a demand for rhinoceros horn.✗ Why the claims are spread does not matter to whether they are true.
  5. EA ban on the trade in rhinoceros horn should be enforced worldwide.✗ A policy the argument never suggests.
Tip In a test with a comparison group, the hidden assumption is usually that the comparison is neutral.

17(Q33 in the original paper) StrengthenAnswer D
The United States attempts to reduce the supply of illegal drugs by intercepting shipments and eradicating illegal crops.Context Despite these efforts, illegal drugs are still readily available, because growers, for example those in Colombia, move to different areas and plant smaller plots that are harder to find.Evidence / example So more effort should be made to reduce demand.Intermediate conclusion This does not simply mean reducing the total number of people using illegal drugs.Context Because the important task is to cut consumption by heavy users,Reason drug-dependent criminals in the country's jails should be treated for their addiction.Main conclusion In this way drug-related social problems can be reduced.Reason

Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. AThe price of drugs has not fallen despite efforts to reduce their supply.✗ About prices and the supply side. It gives no support for treating heavy users in jail.
  2. BStatistics show that many crops of illegal drugs in Colombia have been eradicated.✗ Shows the supply-side efforts working, which weakens the case for switching to demand.
  3. CMost of the drug users in US jails do not want treatment for their drug addiction.✗ If prisoners refuse treatment, the plan may fail. This weakens.
  4. DHeavy drug users are responsible for committing most of the drug-related crime.✓ If heavy users commit most drug-related crime, treating them really would cut drug-related social problems, just as the argument claims.
  5. EThe majority of the US public are in favour of rehabilitating prisoners who are drug users.✗ Public support does not show the plan would reduce social problems.
Tip Back up the final step: why target heavy users? Because they cause most of the harm.

18(Q34 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
While the benefits of cardio-vascular exercise are well known,Opposing view studies have shown that strenuous exercise is actually damaging to the immune system.Reason Athletes, although fit in general terms, are highly susceptible to colds and flu. What's more, many sports people develop cancer at an early age despite their healthy diets and high levels of physical fitness.Evidence / example It is thought by doctors that a healthy immune system is the best way to prevent cancer.Reason Therefore, to increase your chances of a long life, maintaining a high level of physical fitness is ill-advised.Main conclusion

Which one of the following identifies the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AThere is little or no evidence of a link between strenuous exercise and the immune system.✗ Denies the passage’s evidence instead of finding a gap in the reasoning.
  2. BThere may be other reasons for cancer amongst sports people, e.g. the use of performance-enhancing drugs.✗ Attacks one piece of evidence only. Even if exercise did raise cancer risk, the jump to “bad for a long life” would still fail.
  3. CThere is no guarantee that a healthy immune system will prevent someone from developing cancer.✗ The argument only says a healthy immune system is the best way to prevent cancer. It never needs a guarantee.
  4. DThere may be more lives prolonged by high levels of fitness than are lost to early-age cancer.✓ It counts only the cost (early cancer) and ignores the benefits it admits are “well known”. Fitness may save more lives than it loses.
  5. EThere are much more damaging things for the immune system than strenuous exercise.✗ Other harms to the immune system do not change whether exercise harms it.
Tip When a conclusion is about the overall result (a long life), check it weighs the benefits as well as the harms.

19(Q35 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer D
Although the general public are demanding action to reduce the number of pedestrians killed in road accidents in residential areas,Opposing view road bumps in these areas should be abolished.Main conclusion Road bumps were introduced to residential areas because they were cheap, quick to implement, and showed that Local Authorities were acting to reduce road deaths.Context They are admittedly effective at restricting the speed of trafficOpposing view but they also hinder emergency services.Reason The London Ambulance Service has stated that they cause delays in reaching critically ill people and this results in 500 deaths per year from heart failure alone in the London area.Evidence / example Electronic speed signs, and more effective signing of speed limits in residential areas, would be just as effective at reducing pedestrian deaths.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AThe general public are demanding action to reduce the number of pedestrians killed in road accidents in residential areas.✗ The opposing view the argument answers.
  2. BRoad bumps are cheaper and easier to implement than other ways of reducing traffic speed.✗ Background on why bumps were brought in, and it adds a comparison the passage never makes.
  3. CElectronic speed signs are just as effective as road bumps.✗ A reason for scrapping bumps (the replacement works as well), not the conclusion.
  4. DRoad bumps should be abolished in residential areas.✓ The claim everything else defends: road bumps should be abolished, despite what the public is demanding.
  5. ERoad bumps interfere with the Emergency Services causing many deaths.✗ A reason: the ambulance evidence.

Original: to get A (an interesting job) she must B (earn much less) not B (won’t give up her wage) so not A.

DTo get A (happy marriage) they must B (talk more) → not B (they find it impossible) → so not A. ✓ Same shape.
ATo get A he must B → he wants A → so B. Starts from the goal, not from B failing. ✗
