1(Q1 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
The 21st century is witnessing a significant rise in the proportion of people being diagnosed by medical professionals as having a mental illness.Reason For example, at the turn of the century, the number of Canadians being treated for depression increased by over 30% in just one year. A recent study in the US found that nearly twice the number of young people there were diagnosed with a mental disorder in 2018 as compared to 2003. The World Health Organization estimates that by 2028, depression will be the second leading type of disability worldwide.Evidence / example It is clear that aspects of the modern world make it more difficult for people to maintain good mental health.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AChanges in health care and a decrease in stigma attached to mental illness may be responsible for higher diagnosis rates.✓ The evidence is about more diagnoses, not more illness. Better care and less stigma could explain the rise without mental health getting any harder.
  2. BMany people with a mental health diagnosis may be able to function well in society.✗ How well people function says nothing about why diagnoses have risen.
  3. CSignificant numbers of people may be hesitant to seek help for mental health concerns from doctors or other medical professionals.✗ Reluctance to seek help would hide cases, so it can’t explain the rise.
  4. DSome physical and behavioural factors may make mental health diagnoses more difficult.✗ Harder diagnosis would mean fewer diagnoses, not more.
  5. EIn some cultures people may prefer to seek help for mental health concerns from religious leaders as opposed to doctors.✗ Again this would mean fewer diagnoses by doctors. It doesn’t explain the rise.
Tip Watch the jump from “more people diagnosed” to “more people ill”. Look for another reason for the rise.

2(Q3 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
In the UK we are increasingly engrossed by entertainment: we devote more time to it than sleep and spend more money on it than food and drink.Evidence / example The aim of entertainment is to create a make-believe situation in which emotions can be excited and discharged without affecting real life.Context While entertainment has its proper place, it can become a preoccupation, dominating our thoughts and sapping our emotional energy. When this happens, the ‘real’ world of action starts to appear comparatively dull and tedious and this, in turn, undermines social cohesion.Reason Our increasing use of entertainment is, then, threatening our wellbeing.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AOur wellbeing depends on social cohesion.✓ The reason ends at “undermines social cohesion”; the conclusion jumps to “threatening our wellbeing”. That only works if wellbeing depends on social cohesion.
  2. BPeople prefer entertainment to food and drink.✗ Spending more money on it is stated; a preference isn’t needed.
  3. CViewers copy the behaviour they see in entertainment media.✗ Copying behaviour is never part of the argument.
  4. DEmotions pose a threat to society.✗ The passage says entertainment lets emotions out harmlessly. It isn’t emotions that are the threat.
  5. EThe general public are addicted to entertainment.✗ “Addicted” is too strong. The argument only says entertainment can become a preoccupation.
Tip Bridge the gap between the last reason (social cohesion) and the conclusion (wellbeing).

3(Q4 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer E
Adults over the age of 60 are often depicted by the media as depressed, lonely and grumpy. Of course, old age is associated with physical decline, health issues become more common and memory gets fuzzier.Context Previously, theories of ageing assumed that emotional experiences would follow a similar pathway towards dysfunction.Opposing view They were wrong.Main conclusion Recent research has shown that in old age negative emotions (like anger) wane, while positive emotions (like contentment) become more common.Evidence / example Why? Our lives undergo pronounced changes in later life. Work demands are eliminated, there is more time for leisure pursuits, and older people structure their environments in ways that reduce unnecessary stress. Time horizons shrink: people learn to spend their remaining precious time on the things that matter most.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ANot everyone experiences the negative physical decline in old age often depicted in the media.✗ The passage accepts physical decline. Its point is about emotions.
  2. BIn old age people experience both positive and negative emotions less strongly.✗ Contradicts the research: positive emotions become more common.
  3. CBeing freed from the demands of work allows retired people to feel happier.✗ One part of the explanation (“Why?”), not the conclusion.
  4. DOlder people find better ways of avoiding stress and using their time productively.✗ Also part of the explanation, and “productively” is never said.
  5. EAssumptions that the emotional aspects of ageing mirror the physical aspects were wrong.✓ “They were wrong” is the verdict on the old theories that emotions follow physical decline. E spells it out in full.
Tip A short sentence like “They were wrong” can be the conclusion. Rewrite it in full: who was wrong, about what?

4(Q5 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer B
You are allowed to vote in the general election only if you are at least 18 years old. You have reached 18Reason so you will be able to vote in the forthcoming election.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AYou cannot obtain foreign currency from this bank if you cannot produce acceptable identification. You cannot produce the required identification, therefore we cannot let you have the foreign currency.✗ The condition is not met, so no currency: the opposite direction.
  2. BYou can visit your grandmother in hospital providing you have no illness yourself. As you have no illness, you will be able to visit your grandmother.✓ Same shape: a condition for being allowed to do something is met, so you will be able to do it.
  3. CWe can afford to go to America this year only if you save up sufficient money. You haven’t saved enough so we will not be able to go.✗ Condition not met (not enough saved), so no trip. Denies the condition.
  4. DTo win the jackpot prize in the national lottery you need a ticket with all six prize-winning numbers. When I heard you had won the jackpot, I knew you had the lucky ticket that matched all the numbers from the draw.✗ Works back from the result (won) to the condition (six numbers).
  5. EPassport applications can only be accepted if photographs are witnessed as a likeness by someone in a professional job. Your application was refused so your witness cannot have been a professional person.✗ Works back from the result (refused) to the condition (not a professional).

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: you can do B (vote) only if A (18 or over) you have A so you will get B.

The flaw: A is needed but may not be enough (you might not be registered).

BCan do B (visit) providing A (no illness) → you have A → so you will get B. ✓ Same shape.
ANo A (ID) → no B (currency). You don’t have A → so no B. Condition failed. ✗
CB (America) only if A (savings) → no A → so no B. Condition failed. ✗
DNeed A (six numbers) for B (jackpot) → B happened → so you had A. Starts from the result. ✗
EB (accepted) only if A (professional witness) → not B → so not A. Starts from the result. ✗
Tip Check which part the middle sentence gives you: the condition (A) or the result (B), and whether it is met or not.

5(Q9 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer C
Microscopic malaria parasites enter a person’s bloodstream through mosquito bites and infect red blood cells. They replicate in these cells and penetrate other organs. Until recently, microbiologists sought to block the parasites from entering red blood cells.Context Rhode Island University studied 700 Tanzanian children infected with malaria and found the parasites use a protein, since named PfSEA-1, to escape from the red blood cells they infect. Having investigated a subgroup of children immune to malaria, the researchers realised these children were producing an antibody that locked protein PfSEA-1 into their red blood cells. Trapped in the red cells, the parasites were destroyed by immune system cells. Scientists have reproduced the antibody and successfully tested it on mice. Experts believe that, after trials, the antibody will be certified for use in the treatment of humans.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. ARhode Island University is a world leader in medical research.✗ One study doesn’t make the university a world leader.
  2. BAfter the discovery of protein PfSEA-1, the traditional approaches to fight malaria will be abandoned as ineffective.✗ Too strong: nothing says the old approaches don’t work or will be dropped.
  3. CThe discovery of protein PfSEA-1 is likely to help scientists to develop a new way of combatting malaria.✓ Old approach: block entry. New idea: trap the parasite inside the cell, already working in mice and expected to be used in humans. So PfSEA-1 is likely to lead to a new treatment.
  4. DWorld leaders should support and fund the research being conducted by Rhode Island University.✗ A recommendation about funding that the passage never makes.
  5. EA unique feature of the Tanzanian children helped in the discovery of protein PfSEA-1 and its function in spreading malaria.✗ Nothing says the immunity is unique to Tanzanian children, and the protein helps parasites escape cells, not spread malaria.
Tip A drawn conclusion stays as cautious as the evidence: “likely to help” matches “experts believe”.

6(Q10 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
Despite the government’s promise to be more business friendly, plans have been announced to change the law regarding the paid leave that couples are entitled to take following the birth of a child. This will allow new parents to take more time off work than under the current regulations.Context It is claimed that the current legislation makes it difficult for those with family commitments to manage all of their responsibilities,Opposing view but it needs to be recognised that businesses (and in particular small businesses) need to operate in a way that guarantees that staff are available to do the jobs that need doing. The new proposals are undoubtedly going to make the system more complicated and could dissuade businesses from employing certain groups of people.Reason They should be opposed.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AThe example given is only one of a range of measures being proposed.✗ We aren’t told what the other measures do, so this changes nothing.
  2. BMany businesses now allow their employees to choose to work from home.✗ Working from home doesn’t help when someone is on leave and not working at all.
  3. CThe proposals from the government provide support for businesses to help with the cost of covering the work of staff taking leave.✓ The worry is that businesses can’t cover absent staff, so they may avoid hiring some groups. Help with the cost of cover takes away much of that worry.
  4. DThe problems associated with covering the work of absent members of staff become increasingly difficult the longer the absence goes on.✗ Longer absences being harder to cover strengthens the argument.
  5. EThe proposals will increase the amount of leave that employees are allowed to take in other circumstances as well as following the birth of a child.✗ Even more leave makes the problem bigger: this strengthens it.
Tip Find the reason the proposals are bad for business (covering absent staff) and look for the option that fixes it.

7(Q11 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer E
As long as there is the right combination of guests, any party will be a success.Reason The last party was great,Evidence / example so there must have been a perfect selection of guests.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AAs long as there is a high level of unemployment, all economies will perform below their maximum potential. Unemployment levels here have risen sharply, so our economy will perform less well than it could do.✗ If A then B; A happened; so B. Goes forwards from the condition.
  2. BAs long as there are good hotels, any seaside town can get on the tourist trail. Our town is a tourist hotspot, yet none of the hotels are any good.✗ States an exception (B without A) and draws no “must” conclusion.
  3. CAs long as there is somebody in charge, any project will go smoothly. James has taken firm control, so this week’s project will be fine.✗ If A then B; A happened (James in charge); so B. Goes forwards.
  4. DAs long as there are lots of security personnel around, public order is sure to be maintained. We have been having huge problems with public disorder here, so there cannot be many security people.✗ If A then B; B did not happen; so not A. A different (and valid) shape.
  5. EAs long as there is lots of water, any habitat will support life. Life is abundant in that habitat, so there must be lots of water.✓ Same shape and same mistake: if A then B; B happened; so A must be true. Something else could have caused B.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if A (right guests) then B (success) B happened (great party) so A must have been true.

The flaw: A is enough for B, but B might have had another cause (great food, great music).

EIf A (lots of water) then B (life) → B (abundant life) → so A must be true. ✓ Same shape, same flaw.
AIf A (unemployment) then B → A happened → so B. Forwards. ✗
BIf A (good hotels) then B (tourists) → B but not A. Just an exception. ✗
CIf A (someone in charge) then B → A (James) → so B. Forwards. ✗
DIf A (security) then B (order) → not B → so not A. ✗
Tip All five start with “As long as…”, so the first sentence can’t separate them. Look at what the second sentence gives you and what is concluded.

8(Q15 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer D
Although the Scottish Government has announced plans to ban corporal punishment of children (smacking),Opposing view we must preserve the right of parents in the rest of the UK to smack their children when it is reasonable.Main conclusion This is not because we are violent people eager to hit our kids: we recognise that smacking is damaging to children.Context The point is rather that people resent being legislatively compelled to do what they would have done anyway. It only breeds contempt for the law and since the wellbeing of citizens depends on people respecting the law,Reason we should take steps to prevent it falling into disrepute.Intermediate conclusion We must, therefore, protect the rights of parents in Northern Ireland, Wales and England to smack their children.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ANo published study has identified any developmental benefits associated with smacking.✗ The argument already accepts smacking is damaging and doesn’t claim any benefits.
  2. BRespect for the law is the decisive factor that underpins social cohesion in the UK.✗ Makes respect for the law even more important: this strengthens.
  3. CThe ban is one element in a larger mental and physical health programme for children being undertaken by the Scottish Government.✗ What else Scotland is doing doesn’t touch the reasoning for the rest of the UK.
  4. DThe damage caused by smacking far exceeds the damage caused by negative attitudes towards the law.✓ The argument admits smacking does damage but says a ban harms respect for the law. If the smacking damage is far greater, the ban is worth it.

Tip The argument weighs two harms. Weaken it by showing the other harm is bigger.

Sometimes we are mistreated by others, and forgiveness is one kind of response to those who wrong us. However, sometimes we do things that appear to be morally wrong but, in fact, are not. If we are reproached, we can give an explanation for our action that justifies it. In these cases, we are claiming that, despite appearances to the contrary, what we did was morally permissible.Context Forgiveness and justification ought to be distinguished.Main conclusion When conduct is justified, the implication is that it was not morally wrong, but when conduct is forgiven, there is no such implication. Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ASometimes we do things that appear to be morally wrong but, in fact, are not.✗ Background that introduces justification.
  2. BForgiveness and justification ought to be distinguished.✓ The “ought” sentence. The first part sets up the two ideas, and the last part gives the difference between them.
  3. CWhat we are forgiven for is the morally wrong things we do.✗ A reason: it shows how forgiveness differs.
  4. DForgiveness is one kind of response to those who wrong us.✗ Background that introduces forgiveness.

10(Q17 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer B
There has been a choral singing boom.Context Three hundred thousand more people sing in choirs than play amateur football, yet football receives £30 million from the government every year compared to £1 million for choirs.Evidence / example It is only fair that a substantial increase in funding is made that reflects the numbers of participants and thus treats choral singers fairly.Main conclusion The benefits for those who sing in a choir are comparable to those who play football: increased lung capacity, better posture, improvements to the immune system, and a general sense of well-being. A choir also fosters teamwork, where there is cooperation, determination, discipline and imagination, drawing the best from every individual involved and creating something much greater than the sum of its parts.Reason

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AThe better-off countries have to tackle poverty in our world when we still have over a billion people living on less than a dollar a day.✗ About a duty to help those in need, not sharing funding by numbers.
  2. BAudience figures at regional theatres are now greater than in the capital, so they deserve an increased share of the financial aid set aside for the arts.✓ Principle: public money should be shared out in line with how many people take part. Bigger regional audiences, so a bigger share of arts aid.
  3. CThe proportion of people needing treatment for mental health issues has increased by 25% in a decade and more funding is needed to deal with this crisis.✗ More need means more money, but there is no comparison with another group’s share.
  4. DIf citizens are able to raise a large amount of money towards a community sports centre, then the local council should show good faith by matching that amount.✗ About matching money that people raise themselves.
  5. EOur arts sector continues to generate an increasing share of national income, so we need to keep building on this success by encouraging the next generation of young actors, musicians, and other artists.✗ About building on economic success, not numbers taking part.

Working: write the principle first

Choirs have more participants than football but get far less money unfair: funding should reflect numbers.
So the principle: when one group has more participants than another, it deserves a bigger share of the funding.

BRegional theatres now have bigger audiences than the capital → they deserve a bigger share of arts aid. ✓ Same rule, new setting.
ARich countries must tackle poverty: a duty based on need. ✗
CNeed has gone up → more funding. No comparison between two groups. ✗
DMatch what citizens raise: rewards effort, not numbers. ✗
EArts earn money → invest in young artists: about economic success. ✗
Tip The key words are “reflects the numbers of participants”. Look for the option where a share of money follows the number of people.

11(Q21 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
In real terms, spending on education in the UK is going down. But reductions in funding always mean a reduction in the quality of education provided.Reason In the current climate, we should expect to see the quality of education in the UK diminishing.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above passage?

  1. ANo one ever succeeds at anything unless they make an effort. But you’ve just won an award for your work in physics, so you must have made an effort.✗ Starts from the result (success) and works back to what it needs (effort).
  2. BWhen the water is at this level, it’s either high tide or a flood. High tide is not for another eight hours. So there must be a flood upstream.✗ An either/or: one option ruled out, so the other.
  3. CIf you take regular exercise, you’ll be fit in heart and mind. You’ve been working out twice a week for three months now, so you must be happy and healthy.✓ Same shape: A always leads to B; A is happening; so B. Regular exercise → fit; you exercise; so you are fit.
  4. DIf you eat sweets every day you’re more likely to need dental treatment. Sam has had a lot of dental treatment so he must eat sweets every day.✗ Works back from the result (dental treatment) to the cause, and it is only “more likely”.
  5. EThe car won’t run without a working battery. I can’t get the car to start so the battery must be dead.✗ Works back from the result (won’t start) to one possible cause.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: A is happening (spending down) + A always leads to B (lower quality) so expect B.

It goes forwards from the cause to the result. The order of the sentences doesn’t matter.

CA (regular exercise) → B (fit in heart and mind) + you do A (twice a week) → so B (happy and healthy). ✓ Same shape.
AB (success) needs A (effort) + B happened → so A. Backwards. ✗
BEither X (high tide) or Y (flood) + not X → so Y. ✗
DA (sweets) → more likely B (dentist) + B → so A. Backwards. ✗
ENo A (battery) → no B (car runs) + no B → so no A. Backwards. ✗
Tip Mark each option “forwards” (cause given, result concluded) or “backwards” (result given, cause concluded).

12(Q22 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
Maths is the most popular subject taken at A Level in the UK. Most of the best-paid jobs are in the technology and finance sectors, which tend to require maths. The majority of students achieving the best grades in maths are boys, which helps to explain the persistence of the gender pay disparity. But what explains the gender disparity in Maths A Level results – genetic inheritance or social conditioning?Context International studies of achievement in maths show that in some countries girls outperform boys in maths,Evidence / example suggesting that social conditioning is the explanation.Intermediate conclusion From this we can conclude that the better achievement in maths of boys in the UK is caused by low expectations of girls in maths by teachers and parents.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt assumes that the only factors in social conditioning are the expectations of teachers and parents.✓ The evidence points to “social conditioning” in general. The conclusion narrows it to teachers’ and parents’ expectations, ignoring friends, media and so on.
  2. BIt fails to explain why the relative achievement of boys and girls varies country by country.✗ It does explain it: social conditioning differs between countries.
  3. CIt fails to consider the changing picture of achievement by boys and girls over time.✗ Changes over time aren’t needed to get from social conditioning to the conclusion.
  4. DIt ignores the relatively better achievement of girls in some other subjects.✗ Other subjects don’t affect the reasoning about maths.
  5. EIt confuses the gender pay disparity with achievement disparities.✗ It treats them separately: grades help explain pay, but it doesn’t mix them up.
Tip Compare the intermediate conclusion (social conditioning) with the main conclusion (teachers and parents). It has narrowed.

13(Q23 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer B
In many cultures, traditional heterosexual male preferences for spouses have centred upon a woman’s preparedness – and willingness – to fulfil childcare and other domestic duties and not on her academic achievements or career potential. This kind of expectation on the part of men has served as a significant obstacle to women pursuing careers outside the home.Context It is for this reason that recent data on educational attainment within married, heterosexual couples is a cause for celebration.Main conclusion In many countries, university-educated men are now proportionally much more likely to marry university-educated women than was the case in their fathers’ generation.Evidence / example This shows that more men are valuing the potential of their female partners to develop a career outside the home, and represents the breaking down of another barrier to equality.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AIn many cultures, men now expect to have more ‘hands-on’ involvement with domestic duties than was the case in previous generations.✗ Men doing more at home fits the idea that attitudes are changing.

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AUnless a firm makes fair pay and good conditions a top priority for its workers, it cannot be judged a good employer.✗ Says the motive (priority) matters: the campaigners’ view, the opposite.
  2. BAwards should only be given to individuals or organisations that intentionally put the good of others before their own advantage.✗ Says intentions matter: again the opposite.

Developers go green to make profit the result is good for the environment “all to the good”: only outcomes matter.

DDrug research done for profit → saves more lives → desirable. ✓ Same rule, new setting.
AMust prioritise fair pay to be good: motive matters. ✗
BOnly reward those who intend good: motive matters. ✗
CCut some services → more for others. Outcomes, but no selfish motive. ✗
EPunish a few for the good of many. No motive in sight. ✗
Tip Both halves must match: a selfish motive (profit) and a good result that makes it acceptable.

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer C
The ‘ticking time bomb’ scenario demonstrates that it is possible for torture to be morally permissible.Main conclusion In the scenario, a person with knowledge of an imminent terrorist attack that will kill many people is in the hands of the authorities, and will disclose the information needed to prevent the attack only if she is tortured.Context The consequences of not torturing her – many people dying – are far worse that the consequences of torturing her – one person being tortured –Reason so in this situation torture would be morally permissible.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AIf an action is morally permissible in one situation, it is morally permissible in other situations too.✗ Not needed: the argument only claims it is permissible in this one scenario.
  2. BDying is worse than torture.✗ Already stated (many deaths are far worse than one person tortured), so it isn’t hidden.
  3. CThe moral status of an action is determined by its consequences.✓ The only reason given is that the consequences are better. That proves torture is permissible only if consequences decide what is right.
  4. DOrdinarily, torture would be morally wrong.✗ The argument works whether or not torture is usually wrong.
  5. EIf an action is morally permissible, then it ought to be carried out.✗ The argument only says “permissible”, not that it must be done.
Tip Negation test: “consequences do NOT decide whether an action is right”. Then the better outcome proves nothing.
