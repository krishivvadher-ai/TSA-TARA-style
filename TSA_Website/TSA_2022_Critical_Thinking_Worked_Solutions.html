1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer A
Throughout history, gold has been revered and used for expensive jewellery and ornamentation.Context But in the future, it will be prized for its practical uses rather than for its aesthetic appeal.Main conclusion It wasn’t until early in the last century that gold’s electrical conductivity properties were discovered.Context Solid state electronic devices use very low voltages and currents, which are easily interrupted by corrosion at the contact points. Gold is such an efficient conductor that it can carry these tiny currents and remain free of corrosion, making the devices highly reliable.Reason A small amount of gold is used in almost every modern electronic device, and it is predicted that within ten years, more gold will be used in the production of electronics than in jewellery.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AIn the future gold will be prized for its practical uses rather than for its aesthetic appeal.✓ The “But” sentence: gold will be valued for its uses, not its looks. Everything about conductivity and electronics backs it up.
  2. BIt wasn’t until early in the last century that gold’s electrical conductivity properties were discovered.✗ Background history: when the conductivity was found. It sets the scene.
  3. CGold is such an efficient conductor that it can carry tiny electrical currents and remain free of corrosion.✗ A reason: why gold is so useful in electronics.
  4. DA small amount of gold is used in almost every modern electronic device.✗ Evidence that gold is already used for practical things.
  5. EWithin ten years, more gold will be used in the production of electronics than in jewellery.✗ A prediction used as evidence. It is narrower than A (electronics against jewellery only).
Tip “But” straight after the history often flags the conclusion. Ask which sentence the rest supports.

2(Q3 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer E
Whether known as the high street, main street, or market square, central shopping districts in many towns throughout the world are in danger of disappearing. The decline in some downtown shopping areas began decades ago because of various social and economic trends, including an increase in the number of women working outside of the home and the development of large supermarkets and retail parks further out on the outskirts of towns.Context While change is inevitably difficult,Opposing view communities should not be fearful about the loss of their traditional shopping areas.Main conclusion Shopping is increasingly conducted via the internet. Compared to traditional ways of shopping, the online model offers savings in terms of time and other resources while also providing greater consumer choice.Reason

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AWomen in many countries do less shopping than in previous decades.✗ Women working is just one background cause of the decline. Nothing depends on women shopping less.
  2. BThe decline in central shopping districts is happening more quickly in places with wealthier populations.✗ Wealthier places are never mentioned.
  3. CIt is difficult for governments to try to stop the decline of central shopping areas.✗ The argument says don’t fear the loss, not that it can’t be stopped.
  4. DThe growth in online shopping will not threaten the profitability of large supermarkets on the outskirts of towns.✗ Supermarket profits don’t affect whether communities should fear losing the high street.
  5. ETraditional shopping areas serve no important purpose that cannot be satisfied by alternative shopping methods.✓ “Online does the shopping better, so don’t fear the loss” only works if high streets do nothing else important that online shopping can’t replace.
Tip Negation test: “high streets DO serve an important purpose online shopping can’t meet”. Then the fear is justified, so E is assumed.

3(Q4 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
I have always said that owners of expensive cars are less considerate drivers, and now a study has proved me right.Intermediate conclusion The study showed that they are less likely to stop at a pedestrian crossing to allow someone to cross the road than drivers of less expensive cars – in fact, their likelihood to do so decreased by about 3% for every £800 increase in the vehicle’s value. Previous studies have also shown that wealth is associated with greater self-regard and lower levels of fellow feeling.Evidence / example So next time an expensive sports car comes along while you are waiting at a pedestrian crossing, you can be sure that it won’t stop.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt ignores the possibility that the drivers are wealthy because they are selfish, rather than vice versa.✗ Which causes which doesn’t matter: either way they stop less often. The flaw is the jump to certainty.
  2. BIt assumes that owners of expensive cars are unlikely ever to be pedestrians themselves.✗ Drivers being pedestrians is never part of the reasoning.
  3. CIt fails to take account of the greater safety features available in more expensive cars.✗ Safety features don’t explain whether a driver stops at a crossing.
  4. DIt assumes that just because drivers of expensive cars are less likely to stop, none of them will.✓ The study shows they are less likely to stop. The conclusion jumps to “you can be sure it won’t”, as if none of them ever stop.
  5. EIt ignores the fact that pedestrians can take undue risks when trying to cross the road.✗ Risky pedestrians are not part of the reasoning at all.
Tip Watch the strength words: “less likely” in the evidence, “you can be sure” in the conclusion.

4(Q5 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer E
You only win an election if you gain a majority of votes.Reason You must have gained a majority of votesMain conclusion because you were named the winner in the election.Reason

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AYou are only promoted to be head teacher if you have taught for many years. You have taught for many years, so you are sure to be promoted to be head teacher.✗ Runs it backwards: has A (many years), so “sure” to get B. Having what is needed doesn’t guarantee it.
  2. BYou can only go to the concert if you buy tickets or win them in the competition. You must have bought your tickets as someone else won the competition.✗ An either/or: buy or win; someone else won; so you bought. Elimination, a different shape.
  3. CYou only get a high-paying job if you study hard in school. You are certain to get a high-paying job because you studied hard in school.✗ Same backwards mistake as A: studied hard, so “certain” to get the job.
  4. DYou can only apply for a loan from the bank or the credit union. You missed the bank’s deadline for submitting forms, so you will have to apply to the credit union.✗ Either/or elimination: bank or credit union; not the bank; so the credit union.
  5. EYou are only eligible for the scholarship if you can prove financial need. You have been declared eligible for the scholarship so you must have proved financial need.✓ Same shape: you only get B if you have A; you got B; so you must have A. A sound argument, like the original.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: you only get B (win) if A (majority) you got B (named the winner) so you must have A.

EOnly B (eligible) if A (financial need) → got B (declared eligible) → must have A. ✓ Same shape.
AOnly B (head teacher) if A (many years) → have A → so sure of B. Starts from A, not B. ✗
BOnly concert if buy or win → not win → so bought. Either/or elimination. ✗
COnly B (high-paying job) if A (study hard) → have A → so certain of B. Starts from A again. ✗
DBank or credit union → not the bank → so the credit union. Either/or elimination. ✗
Tip “Only if A, B” means B needs A. If B happened you can say A happened, but having A does not guarantee B.

5(Q9 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer C
Bicycle owners have a duty to have their bicycles maintained properly. This is to ensure the safety both of the rider and of other road users.Reason If an injury is caused to another person as a result of a failure to maintain a bicycle properly, then the injured person should be entitled to compensation from the owner of the bicycle.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. ASkiers injured whilst skiing off the official ski run should be entitled to compensation from the manufacturer of the skis.✗ The skier chose to leave the run, and the claim is against the maker, not an owner who failed to maintain.
  2. BA passenger on a train who is injured tripping over luggage not put in the luggage rack should be entitled to compensation from the train company.✗ The luggage was left by another passenger. Nothing the company owns was badly maintained.
  3. CA child injured while playing on a broken swing in a park should be entitled to compensation from the park owner.✓ Principle: an owner must keep a thing properly maintained, so someone hurt because it wasn’t can claim from the owner. A broken swing is the park owner’s maintenance failure.
  4. DA householder scalded when pouring water from a kettle should be entitled to compensation from the manufacturer of the kettle.✗ A scald while pouring is the user’s own accident, and the claim is against the maker.
  5. EPeople who eat too many biscuits and damage their health should be entitled to compensation from the shops which sold the biscuits.✗ Eating too many biscuits is the person’s own choice. Nothing was badly maintained.

Working: write the principle first

Owner has a duty to maintain the bike (for everyone’s safety) someone is hurt because it wasn’t maintained the owner pays.
So the principle: if something is not properly maintained and it injures someone, its owner should compensate them.

CSwing owned by the park → broken (not maintained) → child hurt → claim from the park owner. ✓ Every part fits.
ASkis not faulty; skier chose the risk; claim from the maker. ✗
BLuggage left by other passengers, not poor upkeep by the company. ✗
DNothing badly maintained; a user’s accident; claim from the maker. ✗
EOwn overeating; the shop did nothing wrong. ✗
Tip Check each part: who owns it, was it badly maintained, did that cause the injury, and is the claim against the owner?

6(Q10 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer D
Despite ranking top of gender equality lists, Sweden is like practically every other country in terms of having an unequal division of labour for unpaid housework. Perpetuating stereotypes about who should be responsible for cleaning tasks, women still perform more domestic chores than their male partners. To address this enduring sexism about ‘women’s work’, Sweden has recently implemented a tax policy through which the government pays half of the bill when households employ a cleaner.Context This policy has been a success:Main conclusion couples that hired a cleaner because of the subsidy report fewer arguments over housework, and the female partners have been able to devote a significant number of additional hours per month to their own paid employment.Evidence / example

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ASwedish women do fewer hours of housework a week on average than women in many other countries.✗ Comparing with other countries says nothing about whether the subsidy worked.
  2. BMany women enjoy doing housework.✗ Enjoying housework doesn’t touch the stereotype or the policy’s results.
  3. CSweden has a long-standing policy of paid paternity leave for fathers of newborn children.✗ Paternity leave is a different policy. It says nothing about this one.
  4. DMost of the cleaners hired because of the subsidy are women.✓ The policy was meant to tackle the idea of cleaning as “women’s work”. If the cleaners are mostly women, women still do the cleaning, so the stereotype hasn’t been tackled.
  5. EThe Swedish educational system has a long tradition of emphasising gender-neutral teaching methods.✗ Gender-neutral teaching is a different measure. It doesn’t touch the claim of success.
Tip Test the policy against its stated aim (“to address this enduring sexism”), not just its side benefits.

7(Q11 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer A
People are attracted to cities because of the job opportunities that they bring. At the same time, house prices tend to push people away from urban centres. The draw of healthy living and green environments similarly pulls people towards less urban environments, further from city centres. The limiting factor in suburban spread – the expansion of cities beyond their existing boundaries – is often travel time, by either public or private means.Reason One of the appeals of driverless cars is that they are set to cut delays by up to 40 per cent because they switch lanes more efficiently, drive closer to the vehicle in front, and travel at a constant speed without repeatedly braking and accelerating, which is the main cause of congestion.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AWe can expect some cities to expand beyond their current limits as a result of driverless cars.✓ Travel time often limits how far cities spread, and driverless cars cut delays. So some cities can be expected to spread further. “Some” and “can expect” keep it modest.
  2. BThere will no longer be any limits to suburban spread with the introduction of driverless cars.✗ “No longer any limits” is far too strong. Delays are cut by up to 40 per cent, not removed.
  3. CThe attractions of living in city centres will disappear once people have access to a driverless car.✗ “Will disappear” is too strong. Jobs still draw people to cities.
  4. DThe introduction of driverless cars could mean that there are no longer any green spaces for people to enjoy.✗ Losing all green spaces is never suggested.
  5. EThere will be more people living in suburban environments than city centres after the introduction of driverless cars.✗ Nothing lets us compare numbers in suburbs and city centres.
Tip Link the two key pieces: travel time limits spread + driverless cars cut travel time. Then pick the cautious option.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
There is no denying the unwanted consequences of car emissions, burning of fossil fuels, chemical effluent, and other pollutants. Their negative effects include the increasing incidence of respiratory, skin, and heart conditions.Context But ‘environments’ consist of more than just the elements of air, earth, and water.Reason Whilst it is quite right to headline the damage we are doing to our lungs and other bodily organs,Opposing view it must not be thought that this is the only way in which we are polluting our world.Main conclusion The effects of artificial light and constant noise are also detrimental to mental and physical wellbeing.Reason Many people, to their cost, rarely see the stars in the night sky, or experience the quiet of the natural environment in which our species evolved.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AThe word ‘environment’ does not apply only to the elements of air, earth, and water.✗ A reason: what ‘environments’ include.
  2. BIt is right to give prominent coverage to the damage human activity is doing to our bodies.✗ A point the author concedes (“quite right to headline”), not what is argued for.
  3. CIt is wrong to think that pollution of the air, earth and water is the only form of environmental damage.✓ “It must not be thought that this is the only way we are polluting our world.” C says the same in plain words.
  4. DThe effects of artificial light and noise are physically and mentally harmful.✗ A reason: light and noise do harm too.
  5. EMany people are deprived of the sight of the stars or the experience of natural silence.✗ An example of the harm from light and noise.
Tip After “Whilst X,” the main clause is the author’s own point.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer D
The past couple of decades have been fairly tricky for many countries due to economic challenges and political and social uncertainties. The UK has certainly been amongst such countries.Context But life there is getting better, according to the World Happiness Report, and indeed the latest findings of this annual review are something for the UK to celebrate.Intermediate conclusion The project measures the quality of life in over 150 countries by scoring such factors as life expectancy, social support infrastructure, personal freedoms, and absence of corruption.Context In the latest Report, the UK had risen in the rankings from 15th to 13th place.Evidence / example While still a number of places beneath Finland, once again rated the happiest country,Context the UK has definitely become happier.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AThe UK has made changes in direct response to the findings of the World Happiness Report in previous years.✗ The argument never says why the UK improved, so it doesn’t need this.
  2. BIt is rare for a country to move up two places in the rankings in a period of one year.✗ How rare the move is doesn’t matter. The conclusion is only that the UK is happier.
  3. CThe World Happiness Report is designed to show that happiness is not largely dependent on economic factors.✗ What the Report is designed to show isn’t needed for the step from ranking to happiness.
  4. DThe UK’s movement in the rankings was not solely due to a fall in happiness in other countries.✓ Rising from 15th to 13th only shows the UK “definitely” got happier if it wasn’t just that two countries above it fell back.
  5. EThe levels of happiness experienced in Finland are possible to achieve in most other countries.✗ Finland is mentioned in passing. Nothing depends on others reaching its level.
Tip A ranking is relative. You can move up because others fall, not because you rise.

10(Q17 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer D
People complain about the fact that newspapers are often full of recycled articles about celebrity gossip,Opposing view but they forget that real news costs money – investigative reporters need to go out into the world and unearth new information. The trouble is, those who complain are also unprepared to pay to read newspapers, expecting to get information for free online.Reason But what can people expect if they want to be able to read the news for free?Intermediate conclusion As they say, you get what you pay for.Reason If you want something for free, then don’t complain about the quality. This is as true of the news as of anything else.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AA person who is offered a free printer as a bonus when buying a new computer cannot complain if the computer is expensive.✗ The complaint is about the price of the paid-for computer, not the quality of the free printer.
  2. BA person who gives as gifts items obtained for free cannot complain if friends and family criticise them for being selfish.✗ About being called selfish for giving free gifts. Not quality.
  3. CA person who finds a winning lottery ticket cannot complain if the owner of the ticket tries to claim the winnings.✗ About who owns the ticket. Nothing about quality.
  4. DA person who obtains free legal advice online cannot complain if the advice ends up being unhelpful.✓ Principle: if you get something for free, don’t complain about its quality. Free legal advice that turns out unhelpful fits exactly.
  5. EA person who buys an illegally copied film cannot complain if the picture is not as clear as they would like it to be.✗ The film was bought, not free, and the issue is that it is illegal.

Working: write the principle first

News read for free poor-quality news “you get what you pay for” so don’t complain.
So the principle: if you get something for free, you cannot complain about its quality.

DFree legal advice → unhelpful (poor quality) → can’t complain. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
AFree printer, but the complaint is the computer’s price. ✗
BFree items, but the complaint is being called selfish. ✗
CFound ticket; the issue is who owns the winnings. ✗
EThe film was paid for; the issue is that it was copied illegally. ✗
Tip Check both halves: the thing must be free, and the complaint must be about its quality.

11(Q21 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer D
Some people have such good natural stamina that they can run a marathon with no training. John was able to run a marathon on Saturday.Reason It is possible he did no training in preparation.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. ASome experts believe that putting bath salts in the bath increases the likelihood of someone getting eczema. Charlie has eczema. It is likely he puts bath salts in his bath.✗ Guesses a likely cause (bath salts) from the effect. “Likely”, not “possible”, and a different shape.
  2. BMost superheroes have super powers which help them fight villains. Batman doesn’t have any super powers. Therefore he is not a superhero.✗ Uses “most” and draws a firm “not a superhero”. A different (and flawed) shape.
  3. CFrogs do not survive in ponds with duck weed. Mustafa has a pond in his garden with frogs living in it. It is possible that Mustafa’s pond has frogs and no duck weed.✗ Starts from an “all” rule (frogs never survive with duck weed), so “possible” is the wrong strength.
  4. DIt is possible to get lung cancer without ever being a smoker. Jill has got lung cancer. Therefore, she may not be a smoker.✓ Same shape: B can happen without A; this person has B; so it is possible they lack A.
  5. ESome trees can be killed by hammering iron nails into them. Oswald’s tree died. Therefore, he must have hammered an iron nail into it.✗ Concludes he “must” have used nails. The original only says “possible”.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: B (a marathon) can happen without A (training) John did B so it is possible he had no A.

DB (lung cancer) can happen without A (smoking) → Jill has B → she may not have A. ✓ Same shape, same strength.
AA (bath salts) raises the chance of B (eczema) → Charlie has B → likely A. Guesses the cause instead. ✗
BMost X have P → Batman lacks P → not X. ✗
CDuck weed means no frogs (always) → frogs present → “possible” no duck weed. Different shape; should be certain. ✗
ESome trees die from A (nails) → tree died → must be A. Too strong. ✗
Tip Match the strength words too: “can … without” in the premise and “possible” in the conclusion.

12(Q22 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer D
In a recent UK news broadcast, a white reporter appeared to mistake a Labour Member of Parliament (MP), who is Black, for another MP who is also Black.Evidence / example This shows the dangers of a lack of diversity in workplaces.Intermediate conclusion It is not the first time that such a mistake has occurred: a week earlier the same television channel had mistakenly shown footage of LeBron James in their coverage of the death of his fellow basketball star Kobe Bryant. A recent report showed that only 14.8% of that television channel’s workforce were from a minority ethnic or racial background.Evidence / example Media organisations in the UK should take this as a wake-up call, and take steps to ensure that the diversity of their workforce mirrors that of the wider population.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the argument above?

  1. AA reporter of the same racial background as the MPs would not have made this type of mistake.✗ Stronger than needed and about one reporter. A diverse team could catch errors even if anyone might slip.
  2. BMistakes like the ones identified in the passage are common in the media.✗ Not needed: even rare mistakes like these could justify the call.
  3. CMedia organisations have a moral duty to ensure they have a diverse workforce.✗ The argument rests on avoiding mistakes, not on a moral duty.
  4. DHaving a more diverse workforce enables mistakes like these to be avoided.✓ The mistakes are used to show the need for more diversity. That only works if a more diverse workforce would help avoid such mistakes.
  5. ESimilar types of errors do not take place in any other workplace.✗ Far too strong, and other workplaces are irrelevant.
Tip Negation test: “a more diverse workforce would NOT help avoid these mistakes”. Then the wake-up call collapses.

13(Q23 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer E
Recent figures issued by the UK Department of Education have revealed that the proportion of students with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) who have reached the expected level of educational achievement by the time they leave secondary school is lower than that of the population as a whole.Evidence / example The Government cannot evade responsibility for this disappointing statistic.Main conclusion By not providing the funds required by schools who have students with these needs, the Government has failed to ensure the right of such students to equality of opportunity.Reason

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ASome students with SEND have problems with reading and writing but have good ability to understand and reason.✗ Good reasoning held back by reading problems is something support (funding) could fix. That fits the argument.
  2. BSome students with SEND require a quiet environment to help them to concentrate on what they are doing.✗ A quiet space can be paid for, so this backs the funding point.
  3. CSome students with SEND require content and tasks from lessons to be explained to them in simpler language.✗ Simpler explanations need extra staff time, which funding provides. Backs it.
  4. DSome students with SEND have physical problems which require the provision of a wheelchair or other aids.✗ Wheelchairs and aids cost money, so this backs the funding point too.
  5. ESome students with SEND have conditions which limit their ability to understand and to reason.✓ If some conditions limit understanding and reasoning, lower results may come from the conditions themselves, not missing money. Funding alone couldn’t close the gap, so the Government isn’t simply to blame.
Tip Weaken = another cause of the gap that money could not fix.

14(Q27 in the original paper) FlawAnswer B
Trains in the UK are operated by private companies approved by the government and awarded a franchise for which they must meet certain standards, for example on punctuality.Context SuperRail, which operates the trains in one part of the country, has a poor record to date and has been warned that if it again exceeds the annual limits set by the government for late departures or arrivals, it will receive a heavy fine and will lose its franchise.Reason At the end of the third quarter, however, SuperRail’s performance was within the defined limits.Evidence / example So if the operator can maintain their performance in the final quarter, they will keep the franchise for another year.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt draws a conclusion about what will happen from something that may or may not happen.✗ The conclusion is conditional (“if … they will keep”), so it doesn’t claim the condition will happen.
  2. BIt assumes that there are no other reasons why a rail franchise might be lost.✓ Exceeding the limits means losing the franchise. Staying within them doesn’t mean keeping it: it could be lost for another reason (“certain standards, for example on punctuality”).
  3. CIt predicts what will happen in the future on the basis of what has happened to date.✗ It makes no forecast from the past. It says what follows if performance holds.
  4. DIt denies the possibility that the company may exceed the limits and still keep the franchise.✗ The warning says exceeding the limits will cost the franchise, so this is no flaw.
  5. EIt concludes that the franchise will be kept but not that the fine will be avoided.✗ Leaving the fine out is a gap in coverage, not a mistake in the reasoning.
Tip “If you exceed the limits, you lose it” does not give “if you don’t, you keep it”.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer A
If you arrive late for work, you will definitely get fired. You lost your last jobReason so you must have arrived late for work.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AIf you turn up the volume, the music gets louder. The music is very loud now which means that you turned up the volume.✓ Same shape and same flaw: if A then B; B happened; so A. Loud music has other causes, just as a job can be lost for other reasons.
  2. BIf a company wants to be successful, it needs to insist on the punctuality of its employees. This company does not take the punctuality of its employees seriously, so it will not be successful.✗ Success needs punctuality; no punctuality; so no success. That is sound: it denies the result.
  3. CDisciplinary action is initiated against all workers who do not wear a helmet on the building site. No disciplinary action has been initiated against Mike, so he must have always worn a helmet on the site.✗ No helmet leads to discipline; no discipline; so a helmet. Sound: it denies the result.
  4. DYou need a membership card to use the services of the gym. You do not have a membership card, so you cannot use the gym.✗ Need a card; no card; so no gym. Sound, and a different shape.
  5. EIn order to anger a hippo, it is enough to drive close to it with a boat. They are driving close to this one, thus it will become angry.✗ Driving close is enough; driving close; so angry. Sound: it affirms the start.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if A (late) then B (fired) B happened (lost your job) so A. Flawed: you could lose a job for other reasons.

AIf A (volume up) then B (louder) → B (very loud) → so A. ✓ Same shape, same flaw.
BIf successful then punctual → not punctual → so not successful. Denies the result: sound. ✗
CIf no helmet then discipline → no discipline → so helmet. Denies the result: sound. ✗
DNeed a card → no card → no gym. Sound. ✗
EIf close then angry → close → angry. Affirms the start: sound. ✗
Tip If the original is flawed, the answer must share the flaw. Cross out every sound option first.

16(Q29 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer B
‘Progressive’ teaching methods – used in schools for the last 40 or 50 years – put an emphasis on collaborative ways of learning.Context It is time to reconsider the idea that this constitutes best practice in the classroom.Main conclusion Collaborative learning appeals to those with extrovert personalities, but much less so to introverts.Reason Extroverts are people with an outgoing or gregarious personality, who enjoy group projects and plenty of stimulation, whereas introverts prefer to work on their own and in a quiet place.Context Modern classroom environments embrace extroverted behaviour, through fast-paced lessons and social learning activities.Reason These can foster an appealing classroom environment, of course,Opposing view but over-emphasising them can undermine the learning of students who are inward-thinking and easily drained by constant interactions with others.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AProgressive teaching methods put an emphasis on collaborative learning.✗ Background: what progressive methods do.
  2. BThe idea that collaborative learning is best classroom practice needs to be reconsidered.✓ The second sentence: it is time to rethink collaborative learning as best practice. The rest (introverts lose out) is the reason.
