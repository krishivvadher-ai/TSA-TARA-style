1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer B
Fear of death prompted us to develop technology that helps people live longer.Context But those same technological advances have condemned us to fear of infirmity and dementia, since so many of us will live to an advanced age.Reason So as to eliminate this new fear, once we have reached old age we should be assisted to die if we choose death over a sad decline.Main conclusion According to current estimates, dementia affects almost 50 per cent of people by the age of 85, and bodily infirmity is guaranteed to develop further the older we get.Evidence / example There is no cure for old age.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ATechnology has advanced to the point where we can now choose when to die.✗ Technology helps us live longer. It never says technology lets us choose when to die.
  2. BEuthanasia should be readily available to people who have reached a certain age.✓ The “should” sentence: once we reach old age we should be assisted to die if we choose. B rewords it. The rest explains why.
  3. CThe fear of infirmity and dementia is worse than the fear of death.✗ No comparison of the two fears is made. Infirmity is just called a “new fear”.
  4. DWe must develop technology to cure dementia and infirmity.✗ The opposite: the passage says “there is no cure for old age”.
  5. EWe should focus on the health of the young instead of that of the old.✗ The young are never mentioned.
Tip “So as to…” plus “should” flags the conclusion, even when facts follow it.

2(Q3 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer E
The family had to choose between planting maize or soybeans.Context Maize received a better market price than soybeans last year,Opposing view but maize requires a much greater fertiliser input to ensure high yields. Because they could not obtain credit to purchase additional fertiliser,Reason they planted soybeansMain conclusion even though the crop is likely to earn less at harvest time.Opposing view

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AIn order to go on the trip to New York, the students have to raise enough money. They are failing to raise much money, so it is unlikely they will go on the trip.✗ Only one plan and a prediction about it. There is no choice between two options.
  2. BShe can apply for a loan from a bank or a local credit union. The credit union has higher customer service ratings, so she hopes it will give her the loan.✗ Two choices, but she picks the one with better ratings. Nothing is missing.
  3. CTo be able to buy their first home, they had to save money for a deposit. They bought a house last year, so they must have saved enough money for a deposit.✗ Works backwards from a result (they bought) to a condition (they saved).
  4. DYou only get a promotion if you meet your sales targets. You are sure to get a promotion because you met last quarter’s sales targets.✗ Treats meeting a needed condition as enough to guarantee the promotion.
  5. ETo exchange currency, the tourists could use a bank or, for a poorer exchange rate, use their hotel. They had to use the hotel as there was not enough time to find a bank.✓ Same shape: two choices, the better one needs something they lacked (time to find a bank), so they took the worse one (the hotel).

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: choose X (maize) or Y (soybeans). X is better (price) but needs R (fertiliser) could not get R (no credit) so chose Y, even though Y is worse.

EX (bank) or Y (hotel, poorer rate). X needs R (time to find a bank) → not enough time → so used Y. ✓ Same shape.
ANeed R (money) for the trip → not raising R → probably no trip. Only one option. ✗
BX (bank) or Y (credit union) → Y has better ratings → hopes for Y. Picks the better one. ✗
CNeed R (deposit) to buy → they bought → so they had R. Reasons back from the result. ✗
DNeed R (targets) for promotion → met R → so sure of promotion. Needed is not enough. ✗
Tip Check every part: two options, the better one blocked by a missing resource, so the worse one is taken.

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer D
The world is now producing more farmed fish than beef. In 2012, fish production reached a record 66 million tonnes; cattle-farm output levelled out at 63 million tonnes.Evidence / example This ought to be good news for the environment.Intermediate conclusion Cattle farming is one of the biggest contributors to global warming, while fish farming – as well as being sustainable – produces little pollution.Reason Fish such as the silver carp can be reared on existing rice paddies, and they feed on grass, plankton and detritus.Evidence / example Species such as salmon, however, are carnivorous and are fed on smaller fish like anchovies caught in the wild.Reason As a result, salmon can only be farmed by further depleting wild fish stocks.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following can most reliably be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AFish farmers who produce carnivorous fish should switch to producing non-carnivorous fish.✗ Too strong. It tells farmers what to do, but the passage only weighs the environment, not jobs or demand.
  2. BFish farming is likely to become increasingly more widespread in comparison to beef farming.✗ A prediction about the future. The passage only gives figures for 2012.
  3. CFish farming will always be better for the environment than cattle farming.✗ “Always” is too strong: salmon farming depletes wild fish stocks.
  4. DIt would be better for the environment if a greater proportion of farmed fish was non-carnivorous.✓ Plant-eating fish (silver carp) are kind to the environment, while carnivorous ones (salmon) deplete wild stocks. So more non-carnivorous fish would be better for the environment.
  5. EWhere possible, farmers should opt for more environmentally friendly forms of farming.✗ Too broad, and a “should” the passage never weighs against other needs. It only covers fish and beef.
Tip “Most reliably” means the cautious option: “it would be better for the environment” is all the passage supports.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer B
There is an urgent need to improve the recruitment procedures for teaching positions at universities.Intermediate conclusion There have been numerous cases in which academics have failed to hire outstanding young researchers and talented lecturers.Reason For example, a research fellow from Germany complained that his applications for lecturing jobs were rejected despite his impressive research and publication record. Another scholar from Spain, who had received the Excellence in Teaching Award, also failed to get a job.Evidence / example A number of similar complaints have forced the Department of Education to introduce a policy which requires universities to staff the relevant selection committees with representatives from independent human resources consultancies.Context This measure will improve recruitment procedures.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AAcademics are driven by egotistic interests when they recruit faculty members.✗ Not needed. The argument never says why academics get it wrong.
  2. BHuman resources consultants will be able to spot suitable candidates for teaching positions.✓ Adding HR consultants only “improves recruitment” if they can tell which candidates are good. The argument takes that for granted.
  3. CA good publication record can diminish your chances of being hired.✗ One example hints at it, but the argument does not rely on it.
  4. DThe primary goal of universities is to maintain the quality of teaching, rather than excellence in research.✗ Not needed. Both researchers and lecturers are given as examples.
  5. EGraduates of German and Spanish universities have fewer chances of being hired for teaching positions.✗ The nationalities are just details of the examples.
Tip Negation test: “HR consultants will NOT spot suitable candidates”. Then the measure won’t help, so B is assumed.

5(Q9 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer E
Shooting is not just positive for rural economies, but is also necessary for conservation efforts.Main conclusion In various peripheral rural places in Britain, sport shooting related jobs are a mainstay of local economies, often accounting for as much as a third of the labour market.Evidence / example However, since humans disturbed the ecosystem by eradicating the top predators, destroying habitats and introducing non-native species, we are also now responsible for reducing further damage – conserving – by continuous management. This means shooting overpopulated species to minimise the risks of disease and starvation, and dominant foreign species in order to conserve weaker native ones.Reason Additionally, the management of the environment for the purposes of sport shooting can have very positive conservation effects; for example, many endangered birds of prey now thrive on shooting estates.Evidence / example

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AWild flower numbers and bee populations have increased on shooting estates.✗ More good news on shooting estates: this strengthens.
  2. BMany animals that are shot are sold on to butchers and enter the food market.✗ Another economic benefit: this strengthens the first part.
  3. CPeople from all over the world travel to Britain to engage in sport shooting.✗ More income from shooting: this strengthens the first part.
  4. DMany native species such as the red squirrel are at threat from foreign species.✗ Shows foreign species must be controlled: this strengthens.
  5. ERe-introducing top predators such as wolves is a more effective means of conservation.✓ Shooting is called necessary because we removed the top predators. If bringing predators back works better, shooting is not necessary for conservation.
Tip Attack the strong word: if there is a better alternative, shooting is not “necessary”.

6(Q10 in the original paper) FlawAnswer D
Thousands of parents have recently been prosecuted by local education authorities for taking their children out of school to go on holiday during term time.Context I took my daughter out of primary school for a two-week holiday every year. The headmistress made no objection and said it would give the other kids time to catch up with her! She was able to learn a foreign language and spend some rewarding time with her French pen-pal. She eventually achieved top grades at school and a degree in mathematics,Evidence / example so it obviously did her no harm!Intermediate conclusion Parents, not governments, know best how to raise children.Reason This law is quite unnecessary.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt is an appeal to tradition because it suggests that taking children on holiday in term time used to be acceptable.✗ It never says term-time holidays used to be acceptable. It is about one family.
  2. BIt is based on authority as it uses the headmistress’s lack of objection in support.✗ The headmistress is a side detail. The main support is the daughter’s results.
  3. CIt assumes that because the daughter’s good grades were achieved after her foreign holidays, they were the result of the holidays.✗ It only claims the holidays did “no harm”, not that they caused the good grades.
  4. DIt is based entirely on the one example of the daughter and generalises from that.✓ One bright daughter who did fine is used to say a law for thousands of families is unnecessary. Other children may well be harmed.
  5. EIt assumes that learning some French adequately compensated for the work the daughter missed.✗ It relies on her final grades, not on French making up for the missed work.
Tip One personal story → a general law: a classic hasty generalisation.

7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer E
Evidence suggests that children whose parents are married tend to fare better – financially, academically and socially – than children of unmarried parents. The government is therefore proposing a tax break for married couples, whereby married couples are reimbursed by approximately £150 per person per annum.Opposing view This idea is flawed for several reasons.Main conclusion First, it punishes those whose partners have left them and broken up the marriage. Secondly, it punishes those who, through no fault of their own, have been unable to find a partner. Thirdly, it assumes that people in unhappy relationships will persevere in their unhappy state for something like £12.50 a month.Reason This is patently absurd.Intermediate conclusion Finally, it makes the bogus assumption that maintaining a poor marriage will be better for the children than separation.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AA poor relationship could be more damaging for a child than separation.✗ Behind the last reason, so it supports the conclusion.
  2. BChildren whose parents are married tend to fare better than children of unmarried parties.✗ The government’s evidence, the view being attacked.
  3. CIt is absurd to think that people will persevere in an unhappy relationship for the sum proposed.✗ A sub-conclusion about the third reason only.
  4. DThe government’s proposal punishes single parents.✗ Rewords the first two reasons, so it is a reason.
  5. EThe government’s idea of giving married couples tax breaks is flawed.✓ “This idea is flawed for several reasons”. Then come the reasons: first, secondly, thirdly, finally.
Tip “For several reasons” followed by a numbered list: the sentence before the list is the conclusion.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer C
The award of a Nobel Prize in economics, but not in psychology, sociology or anthropology, seems to imply that economics is not a social science, but an exact one, like chemistry or physics. Underpinning this idea is the thought that the human world operates like the physical world.Opposing view Economists themselves perpetuate this impression by dealing in hard quantitative data, as opposed to observation and qualitative analysis.Context The defining feature of the natural sciences is their consistent success in making predictions,Reason but almost all economists failed to predict the 2008 financial crisis. The striking collapse of LTCM, a hedge fund set up by two economists who were later awarded the Nobel Prize, further underscores the limits of economics to make accurate forecasts.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AThe Nobel Prize should not be awarded for economics.✗ Goes beyond the passage. It questions what economics is, not whether it deserves a prize.
  2. BThe human world is not part of the physical world.✗ Too strong. The passage only doubts that the human world operates like the physical world.
  3. CEconomics is not a science like chemistry or physics.✓ Natural sciences are defined by consistent successful predictions. Economics keeps failing to predict, so it is not an exact science like chemistry or physics.
  4. DEconomists cannot predict the behaviour of markets.✗ “Cannot” is too strong. It shows the limits of their forecasts, not that they can never predict.
  5. EHard quantitative data has no role in economics.✗ “No role” is too strong. The passage never says data is useless.
Tip The passage sets up a claim (“an exact one, like chemistry or physics”) and then knocks it down. The conclusion is the denial.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer B
Mobile phone use and driving under the influence of alcohol are not the only examples of irresponsible driver behaviour.Context Tiredness when driving a vehicle is said to be responsible for 300 deaths per year. Astonishingly, almost half of male drivers and 22% of female drivers admit to having fallen asleep at the wheel.Evidence / example If we assume (and it seems reasonable to do so) that even more people are injured than killed as a result of accidents caused by tiredness,Reason it is clear that tiredness while driving is a major road safety issue – and entirely preventable.Intermediate conclusion If we wish to preserve our own lives and those of others we should not drive a car knowing that we have had insufficient sleep or rest to drive safely.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AWomen are more careful than men about driving when they are tired.✗ The figures differ by sex, but the argument never needs a reason why.
  2. BDrivers are able to recognise when they are dangerously tired.✓ “Entirely preventable” and “knowing” both need drivers to be able to tell when they are too tired to drive safely.
  3. CFalling asleep when driving is more dangerous than using a mobile phone while driving.✗ No comparison is needed. Tiredness is just another example of bad driving.
  4. DPeople whose jobs involve long distance driving can always avoid driving while tired.✗ “Always” is too strong, and jobs are never discussed.
  5. ETiredness has been insufficiently highlighted in road safety campaigns.✗ Road safety campaigns are never mentioned.
Tip Negation test: “drivers can NOT tell when they are dangerously tired”. Then it is not “entirely preventable”.

10(Q17 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer B
There has been a significant rise in the number of children with rickets.Evidence / example This has resulted from the airless, indoor world we have created for ourselves.Intermediate conclusion Rickets is caused by a deficiency of vitamin D, which is produced in response to sunlight on the skin.Reason Where children used to play outside they mostly stay indoors immersed in virtual realities via various electronic media. Instead of long summer evenings outdoors, we close the curtains to prevent sunlight falling on our screens. The lure of technology is compounded by adult fears of the risks to children if they are left to roam free in the outside world; in any case there are fewer accessible public spaces for open air activity.Evidence / example Curing this will require an awful lot more than vitamin pills.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AThere has been a large growth in areas designated as national parks and in urban parkland.✗ Dents one small point (fewer spaces), but children still stay inside with screens.
  2. BDiet has a much more significant effect on vitamin D intake than sunlight.✓ The argument blames lack of sunlight. If diet matters much more for vitamin D, the rise is more likely down to diet, and pills could fix it.
  3. CThe incidence of rickets is confined to a few hundred cases a year.✗ The argument is about a rise and its cause, not about how common rickets is.
  4. DIn the 19th century, when the incidence of rickets was much higher, terrible air pollution and smog affected the large population of city dwellers.✗ Smog blocks sunlight, so this fits the sunlight explanation: it strengthens.
  5. EMany people can’t afford or be relied on to take vitamin D supplements.✗ Suggests pills won’t be enough: this strengthens the conclusion.
Tip A causal claim is weakened by another cause that explains the same rise.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
When mobile phones first became relatively inexpensive many parents bought them for their teenage children on the grounds that having access to a phone would keep them safe. High profile cases of abduction at the time underscored the need to improve safety for young people.Context But today mobile phones invariably have access to the internet, which has made young people vulnerable to stalking and cyberbullying. Since the stated case for buying teenagers mobile phones was to keep them safe,Reason we should now restrict their access to them.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is the best statement of the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AThe incidence of cyberbullying and stalking does not undermine the respects in which mobile phones keep teenagers safe.✓ Phones bring a new risk online, but they still help keep teenagers safe from the original danger (abduction). The argument ignores that the safety benefit is still there.
  2. BIf you restrict young people’s access to mobile phones they will continue to bully one another on other devices.✗ A practical snag with the remedy, not a fault in the reasoning.
  3. CAccess to mobile phones has not stopped teenagers from being attacked while out alone at night.✗ Says phones don’t keep teenagers safe, which supports restricting them.
  4. DThe majority of teenagers are not affected by the problems of stalking and cyberbullying.✗ Even a minority at risk could matter. It does not expose the faulty step.
  5. EIt ignores the fact that there are other ways of keeping children safe.✗ Other ways of keeping safe would, if anything, make restricting phones easier.
Tip The argument only counts the new risk. Ask what it forgot about the old benefit.

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer B
A new supermarket scheme awards money-off vouchers to customers who spend £40 a week on their grocery shopping. As your weekly shop only costs £15,Reason you are not eligible for vouchers.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AA discount is given to gym members who introduce new customers. You brought two friends who joined up, so you will get a discount.✗ The condition is met, so the reward follows. The original fails the condition.
  2. BDessert is given to all those who have completed their main course. You have not eaten your main course so you will not be given dessert.✓ Same shape: a reward goes to those who meet a condition, you do not meet it, so you do not get the reward.
  3. CAnyone between the ages of 18–30 inclusive gets admission into the nightclub half-price. You are 30 so you will not pay full price.✗ You meet the condition (you are 30), so you get the deal. Condition met, not failed.
  4. DA bursary is given to those on incomes below £10 000. You earn £9000 so you will receive a bursary.✗ You meet the condition (£9000 is below £10 000), so you get the bursary.
  5. EOnly experienced wine drinkers can appreciate a good wine. You prefer poor quality wine as you have only recently started drinking wine.✗ Not a reward rule, and “prefer poor wine” goes beyond “can’t appreciate good wine”.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: people who do X (spend £40) get R (vouchers). You do not do X (only £15) so no R.

BDo X (finish main course) → R (dessert). You have not done X → no R. ✓ Same shape.
ADo X (introduce customers) → R (discount). You did X → R. Condition met. ✗
CAged 18–30 → R (half price). You are 30 → R. Condition met. ✗
DIncome below £10 000 → R (bursary). You earn £9000 → R. Condition met. ✗
EOnly experienced drinkers appreciate good wine → you are new → you prefer poor wine. Not a reward, and a new idea (preference). ✗
Trap! Three options have the right topic (a reward for a condition) but the condition is met. Check the direction: the original fails it.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer E
The plethora of house-buying and relocation programmes on television these days may well stimulate an interest in lovely properties,Opposing view but they are a bad thingMain conclusion because at the same time they make viewers who could never afford the houses shown feel both envious and worthless.Reason

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. APeople who watch house-buying programmes do not tend to have nice houses themselves.✗ A fact about viewers, with no judgement that the programmes are harmful.
  2. BSeeing others enjoy luxuries that they have not got can motivate people to work harder to earn enough to support the same lifestyle.✗ The opposite: seeing luxuries has a good effect (motivation).
  3. CThe public thinks a celebrity lifestyle must be great, but most celebrities are constantly worried about dropping out of the public eye.✗ About the worries of the people shown, not the feelings of those watching.
  4. DIt is said that renting property is throwing money away, but so much of what one pays monthly on a mortgage goes on interest anyway that many people find it better to rent rather than buy.✗ Same topic (property), but it is about money, not how media makes people feel.
  5. EMagazines that show images of men and women who seem perfect are largely to blame for our discontent and for the increase in eating disorders among young people.✓ Same principle: media showing an ideal people can’t reach is harmful because it makes them feel bad about themselves. Perfect-looking people in magazines → discontent.

Working: write the principle first

TV shows lovely houses viewers can never afford viewers feel envious and worthless so the shows are bad (despite sparking interest).
So the principle: media that shows people an ideal they can’t reach is harmful, because it makes them feel bad about themselves.

EMagazines show “perfect” people → readers feel discontent (and worse) → magazines to blame. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
AViewers don’t tend to have nice houses. A plain fact, no harm claimed. ✗
BSeeing luxuries → work harder. A benefit, the opposite direction. ✗
CCelebrities worry about fame. The harm is to the people shown, not the audience. ✗
DRenting vs buying. Same topic, no media and no feelings. ✗
Tip Strip out the houses and TV. The rule is about unreachable images making the audience feel bad.

Long-term unemployment is a big problem for the UK, both for the government and for the individual.Context The solution is unlikely to be one the government wishes to consider: invest money in improving the skills of job centre staff.Main conclusion Some people argue that providing welfare benefits makes unemployed people less motivated to find work. A Prime Minister has a difficult judgement to make when deciding whether to sack one of their ministers or not.Context Faced, for example, with a minister involved in some kind of scandal, the Prime Minister might feel they should sack them to show they stand up for integrity and high moral standards, or, on the other hand, allow them to remain in post for other reasons such as loyalty to a colleague or a belief that they are, on balance, doing a good job.Evidence / example Sacking ministers shows that the Prime Minister is prepared to live by principles, even if by so doing they lose valued colleagues. Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
In a survey done by a school librarian during one year, borrowing of books in the library declined by 20%. Amongst books borrowed, rates of late return or loss of books also increased by 10%. On the other hand, student use of computers in the library increased by 25%. This finding was reinforced by frequent complaints from students that there were no computers available when they needed to use them.Evidence / example The librarian concluded that the best response to these findings was to cut the number of books and purchase more computers.Main conclusion 20% of books were sold off or given away, the space given over to additional computer tables, and the library’s entire available budget given over to purchasing new computers.Context

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the librarian’s reasoning?

  1. AThe library should cater for the students’ preferences rather than trying to change them.✓ Students use books less and computers more, so the librarian gives them what they want. That only follows if the library should follow students’ preferences, not try to win them back to books.
  2. BA decline in the borrowing of books indicates a decline in reading.✗ Not needed. The librarian responds to borrowing and computer use, not to how much students read.
  3. CStudents were unjustified in complaining about the lack of computers.✗ The opposite: the librarian acts on the complaints, treating them as fair.
  4. DStudents’ educational achievement is improved by using computers.✗ Not needed. The reasoning is about demand, not about results.
  5. ELibrary use will necessarily increase if more computers are provided.✗ “Necessarily increase” is too strong. The aim is to meet the demand already there.
Tip Negation test: “the library should try to CHANGE students’ preferences”. Then cutting books makes no sense.

17(Q33 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer C
The current advice from the NHS in the UK is to eat five portions of fruit and vegetables a day.Context However, a recent study suggested that at least seven portions are needed to maximise health.Opposing view Given that less than a third of adults in the UK, and even fewer children, currently meet the five-a-day target,Reason there is little to be gained from changing the NHS advice to a higher number.Main conclusion A higher target might even be counter-productive,Reason as evidence shows that a change in diet is best sustained when the change is made slowly and incrementally.Evidence / example

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. AEating seven portions of fruit and vegetables a day is needed to stay healthy.✗ The study’s claim, which the author argues against acting on.
  2. BAt least two thirds of the UK adult population ignore current guidelines.✗ Reworded evidence, and “ignore” is stronger than “don’t meet”.
  3. CCurrent guidance on fruit and vegetable intake should be maintained.✓ “Little to be gained from changing the NHS advice” means keep the five-a-day advice. C says that.
  4. DSetting high targets for a change in diet is unrealistic.✗ A reason (a higher target might be counter-productive), and too general.
  5. ECurrent advice on fruit and vegetable intake has not taken account of recent research.✗ Never claimed. The author knows about the research but rejects changing the advice.

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  2. BYou have to place your dustbin on the street outside before 8 a.m. today for the refuse collectors to deal with it. Since you put yours in the correct place at 7 a.m. your dustbin will be emptied.✗ Same mistake as A: meeting a needed condition does not guarantee the result.
  3. CYou have to score at the 98th percentile or higher in a standardised IQ test to become a member of Mensa. As you are not a member of Mensa it is clear that your IQ falls outside the top 2%.✗ Starts from “not B” (not a member) and concludes “not A”. You could have the score and not join.
  4. DOnly towns that have a bypass to divert their traffic are able to create a pedestrian-only high street for local shoppers.   5. EYou need to have 45 hours in the air before you can take a pilot’s licence test. You have only flown 18 hours so you will not be able to take the test.✗ Valid, but it goes from “not A” (only 18 hours) to “not B”. A different shape.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: only if A (read Russian) can you have B (grasp Pushkin). You have B (insightful thesis) so you have A.

DOnly if A (bypass) can you have B (pedestrian street). Your town has B → so A. ✓ Same shape.
AOnly if A (clear sky) can you have B (astronomy). A (cloudless) → so B. Starts from A, not B. ✗
BNeed A (bin out before 8) for B (emptied). A (out at 7) → so B. Starts from A. ✗
CNeed A (top 2%) for B (Mensa). Not B → so not A. Invalid. ✗
ENeed A (45 hours) for B (take the test). Not A → so not B. Valid, but it starts from “not A”. ✗
