1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer E
Like other booming cities, including New York, London has thrived amid globalisation. It is growing by 100 000 new residents a year.Context However, if London has given striving people plenty of reasons to come, it has done a poor job of housing them.Intermediate conclusion The median price of a London home has tripled over the past two decades. Since 2001 the disposable income of people renting property has fallen by 30 per cent as a result. Businesses are worried too. Commercial property in central London is twice as expensive as in New York. Productivity has begun to slow as promising firms are forced out.Evidence / example Overpriced property is costing London the economic and human diversity on which its prosperity depends.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ALondon needs striving people in order to prosper.✗ Never argued. Striving people are only mentioned as the reason so many come to London.
  2. BLondoners in rented homes have 30 per cent less to spend.✗ Evidence: renters’ disposable income has fallen by 30 per cent.
  3. CLondon has done a poor job of housing its new residents.✗ An intermediate step. The passage goes further: poor housing is costing London its prosperity.
  4. DBusinesses are worried by the recent slowdown in productivity.✗ Muddles the evidence: businesses are worried about property costs, and the slowdown comes from firms being forced out.
  5. EExpensive property is costing London the diversity it needs to prosper.✓ The last sentence: overpriced property is costing London the diversity its prosperity depends on. The facts about homes, renters and businesses all lead up to it.
Tip Ask “so what?” of each claim. Poor housing → high costs → lost diversity → prosperity at risk. The end of the chain is the conclusion.

2(Q3 in the original paper) FlawAnswer E
A high degree of self-discipline is one quality exhibited by all athletes who have reached the uppermost levels of competition, whatever the sport. This amount of self-discipline is in evidence long after the initial excitement of a sport has worn off, and an athlete has had to keep going through years of painful and repetitive training sessions.Evidence / example The example of top athletes suggests one approach to improving student behaviour and educational achievement.Main conclusion If more young people could begin training programmes akin to those of elite athletes, they too would develop exceptional levels of personal discipline to apply within their studies.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AHigh levels of self-discipline may not be all that it takes to become an elite athlete.✗ The argument never says discipline alone makes an elite athlete. It is about discipline, not reaching the top.
  2. BYoung people may be able to develop self-discipline through other kinds of activities.✗ It only claims training is “one approach”. Other routes to discipline do not hurt it.
  3. CThe kind of coaching support available to elite athletes may not be accessible to the general population.✗ A practical snag. The argument says “if” young people could begin such training, so access is not the faulty step.
  4. DGood behaviour and educational achievement during one’s school years may not be essential for going on to lead a successful life.✗ Later success in life is never discussed. The aim is behaviour and achievement at school.
  5. EStrong self-discipline may not be an effect of the training but rather a capacity already possessed by athletes as they start their training.✓ It assumes the training causes the self-discipline. Maybe only people who already have it last through years of training and reach the top.
Tip When a group shares a quality, ask: did the activity give them it, or did the quality get them into the group?

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer B
Young people are becoming ever more responsible.Reason They are less likely to take drugs or drink than past generations. Very few have serious problems with alcohol or drugs. It is people in their forties who are most likely to be admitted to hospital for drinking-related problems. The latest figures on drug addiction show that 39% of heroin addicts are now over the age of forty, up from 19% in 2006. Fewer than ever are under the age of twenty-four. The number of young adults in prison is falling, but our overcrowded prisons are increasingly home to the older generation: between 2002 and 2013, the number of middle-aged men locked up increased by 130%. The same is true of suicide rates: on both sides of the Atlantic fewer young people but more middle-aged men are killing themselves.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AYoung people have far fewer health problems than older generations.✗ “Health problems” in general goes beyond drink, drugs, crime and suicide, and “far fewer” is too strong.
  2. BSome social problems are increasingly problems of middle-aged people.✓ Drink, drugs, prison and suicide are all shifting from the young to people in their forties and middle-aged men. “Some” and “increasingly” match the evidence exactly.
  3. CPeople who drink are more likely to engage in criminal activity.✗ Drink and crime are listed side by side. No link between them is drawn.
  4. DDrink, drugs and suicide are the biggest killers of middle-aged people.✗ “Biggest killers” needs a comparison with other causes of death, which is never given.
  5. EYoung people are likely to develop harmful behaviours later in life.✗ Nothing is said about today’s young people later on. The figures compare different generations.
Tip A good drawn conclusion uses careful words (“some”, “increasingly”) that every piece of evidence supports.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
Adults thinking back on their experience of school almost always remember with most gratitude the teachers who taught them more than just a subject, and prepared them for life, not just for an exam.Evidence / example But the current generation of students may not have the same experiences to look back on in future yearsIntermediate conclusion because the role of teachers has changed. The focus for both students and teachers is now mainly on exam results, and that is how their success or failure is measured.Reason For teachers to share their lives with their students, offering them a model of adulthood to which to aspire, is therefore no longer considered desirable.Intermediate conclusion So children today receive a less valuable education than their parents and grandparents did.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AEducation for life is of greater value than merely passing exams.✓ Losing “education for life” only makes today’s education less valuable if that kind of teaching is worth more than just passing exams.
  2. BAdults looking back on their childhood often remember only the good parts.✗ If memories were rosy, that would weaken the argument. It is not needed.
  3. CMany young people have higher ambitions than becoming like their teachers.✗ Students’ ambitions are never part of the reasoning.
  4. DExam results are the vital keys which open the door to education and a livelihood.✗ This praises exam results, so it pulls against the conclusion.
  5. EOffering students a model of adulthood is a more satisfying role than helping them to pass exams.✗ About which role is more satisfying for teachers. The conclusion is about the value of the education to children.
Tip Negation test: “education for life is NOT worth more than passing exams”. Then the new focus loses nothing and the conclusion falls.

5(Q9 in the original paper) FlawAnswer B
Texting is the preferred method of communication among young people, with 72% of teenagers saying they text regularly and one in three sending more than a hundred texts per day.Context Unfortunately, however, very few youngsters seem able to communicate using grammatically correct sentences. They seem to think it ‘gr8’ that correct spelling is no longer considered necessary.Reason Many local employers have complained that school-leavers have no idea how to communicate appropriately, whether face-to-face or in business letters. Many written job applications are phrased so poorly that they go straight in the bin. Mobile phones were banned in my daughter’s school last year,Evidence / example a move which has proved fully justified.Intermediate conclusion Since then test scores in English have improved.Evidence / example All schools need to introduce a similar ban.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt makes a false statistical comparison between the 72% of teenagers who merely text regularly and those who send more than a hundred texts in a day.✗ The two figures are just reported side by side. No comparison is drawn from them.
  2. BIt assumes that, because test scores improved after the ban on mobile phones, the improvement was due to the ban.✓ Scores improved after the ban, so the author decides the ban caused it. Something else (new teachers, easier tests) could explain it.
  3. CThe argument rests on the assumption that communicating by text is inappropriate.✗ The worry is texting harming grammar and spelling. Even if assumed, that is not the faulty step to a ban.
  4. DIts conclusion, that communicating by text is inappropriate, merely restates one of the reasons given in support of the argument.✗ The conclusion is that schools should ban phones, not that texting is inappropriate, so it is not circular.
  5. EIt assumes that correct spelling is a necessary requirement for gaining employment.✗ Binned job applications are evidence of poor writing. The argument does not rely on spelling being needed for a job.
Tip “Since then” is the clue: one thing following another does not prove one caused the other.

6(Q10 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer E
While it is a woman, Ada Lovelace, who is often credited with originating the concept of software, today’s computer tech-related jobs are held mostly by men.Context The tech sector must continue to work against factors that inhibit gender equality within it.Reason One such factor appears to be prejudice about women’s abilities to write computer programming code.Main conclusion A recent study of a program-sharing and development website analysed the rates by which suggested code changes were accepted by website users according to the gender of the user contributing the changes. In cases where the contributor’s gender was not made clear on the website, 72 per cent of the suggestions made by women were accepted. In cases where the contributor’s gender was revealed to be female, the acceptance rate fell to 63 per cent.Evidence / example

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. AOver the time period the website was studied, more female users than male users rejected suggested code changes overall.✗ Who rejects changes overall says nothing about why women’s changes were accepted less once their gender was known.
  2. BLeading firms in the tech sector have undertaken a variety of high-profile initiatives to encourage more women to pursue computer programming careers.✗ Firms encouraging women does not show the prejudice is absent. It fits the sector “working against” it.
  3. CNo meaningful differences existed between the code changes suggested by female users who revealed their gender on the website and those who did not.✗ Same quality of code means the drop can only come from gender being revealed: this strengthens.
  4. DEven by modern standards, Ada Lovelace received an extensive education in mathematics and the sciences.✗ Ada Lovelace’s education is background. It does not touch the study.
  5. EMale contributors to the website experienced a similar drop in their code change acceptance rates when their gender was revealed.✓ If men’s acceptance rate also dropped when their gender was shown, the fall is not about women. It comes from revealing gender at all, so it is no evidence of prejudice against women.
Tip For a “before and after” comparison, ask whether the same change happens to the other group (here, men).

7(Q11 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer D
Schools have a duty to support the well-being of their students, and sport is one way to enhance this. Although many young people don’t enjoy the competitive side of sport, there are many physical activities that don’t require a competitive element,Reason such as hillwalking and caving.Evidence / example Schools should offer these, or other non-competitive physical activities, as an alternative to traditional games lessons.Main conclusion Many people don’t continue to play sport after they leave school, yet physical fitness is important in maintaining general health and the ability to work.Reason Introducing alternative physical activities that are attractive for less competitive adults is therefore an important part of preparing students for life after school.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. ASchools have a duty to prepare students for life after school.✗ The stated duty is to support well-being. Preparing for life is a reason for the alternatives, not the claim itself.
  2. BMany young people don’t enjoy the competitive side of sport.✗ A reason: why non-competitive options are needed.
  3. CMany people don’t continue to play sport after they leave school.✗ A reason for the second line of support (fitness after school).
  4. DSchools should offer non-competitive alternatives to traditional games lessons.✓ “Schools should offer these… as an alternative to traditional games lessons.” The well-being reason before it and the life-after-school reason after it both support it.
  5. EYoung people should be encouraged to find a sport they enjoy.✗ The argument is about non-competitive activities like hillwalking, not finding a sport to enjoy.
Tip The “therefore” sentence at the end supports the “should”. A recommendation is usually the main conclusion.

8(Q15 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer B
Recently a pioneering natural flood management scheme slowed river peak flow by 15 to 20 per cent, saving a small town’s museum and several homes.Evidence / example As part of this scheme, special dams, designed to leak water gradually, were installed in nearby hills to slow the flow of water off the slopes. An upstream flood storage reservoir was installed, along with the planting of thousands of trees and the restoration of heather moorland, which acted as a sponge.Context Around half of the reduction in flood water in the town was due to the upstream land management measures, and half due to the effect of the flood storage area.Evidence / example Natural flood management measures improve water quality, prevent erosion and, in some cases, store carbon.Reason

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AThe reduction in peak flow of a river would be insufficient to prevent flooding if there was very heavy rainfall.✗ Very heavy rainfall is never discussed. The scheme actually saved the town.
  2. BThe benefits of natural flood management schemes go beyond reducing flood risk.✓ The last sentence lists extra benefits: cleaner water, less erosion, sometimes stored carbon. So the benefits go beyond cutting flood risk.
  3. CAll natural flood management methods are equally effective in reducing river flow.✗ “All equally effective” is too strong. Two kinds of measure did about half each, but every method is never compared.
  4. DNatural flood management should be introduced in all areas at risk of flooding.✗ “All areas” is too strong from one pioneering scheme.
  5. ENatural flood management methods are most effective in hilly upland areas.✗ Hills are part of this scheme, but no other kind of area is compared.
Tip Watch for “all”, “most” and “should”: one example rarely supports them.

9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
The government is planning an increase in fuel tax later this year as part of its strategy to meet carbon emissions targets.Context Yet motorists and road transport companies have suffered crippling price rises of up to 30% in petrol and diesel bills in the past year because of fuel taxes. The government has enjoyed a £505 million windfall from high oil prices in the last six months alone, with extra revenue coming from North Sea oil reserves. A further increase in fuel tax will impact on all UK households because it will eventually push up the price of shop goods and inflation.Reason So the government must abandon its planned tax increase.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above passage?

  1. AThe impact on people’s pockets is more important than the need to control carbon emissions.✓ The tax is meant to help meet carbon targets, yet the author says drop it because of the cost to people. That only works if the cost to people matters more.
  2. BThe cost of fuel is too high and must be reduced.✗ Too strong. The argument only says do not raise the tax, not that fuel costs must come down.
  3. CThe only justification for higher fuel taxes is to reduce carbon emissions.✗ Not needed. Whatever the reasons for the tax, the argument says the cost to households outweighs them.
  4. DRoad transport companies could go out of business if fuel tax is increased.✗ “Out of business” is too strong. Crippling price rises are enough for the argument.
  5. EThe government could use its increased levels of North Sea oil revenue to reduce fuel taxes.✗ A way to cut taxes. The argument only needs the rise dropped, so this is not needed.
Tip The argument never answers the stated aim (carbon targets). Ask what you must believe to drop the plan anyway.

10(Q17 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer E
Despite the recent decline in numbers of people getting married,Context it appears that there is still much to recommend it.Intermediate conclusion Married people are happier, wealthier and live longer.Reason Husbands and wives have a lower risk of disease and a higher life expectancy. They smoke less, drink less and eat more healthily. Those who get married and stay married have on average double the amount of wealth of those who never marry. Surveys show that they are happier than single people and less likely to experience mid-life depression. Married couples benefit from stronger networks of support, with relatives providing more care and assistance in old age and in times of need.Evidence / example Finding and committing to a marriage partner is a goal well worth pursuing.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above reasoning?

  1. AResearch suggests that being married boosts the chances of surviving cancer.✗ Another benefit of marriage: this strengthens.
  2. BStatistics on marriage do not differentiate between those who have children and those who do not.✗ A gap in the figures, but nothing suggests children explain the benefits.
  3. CIn many countries, being married gives advantages in terms of tax payments.✗ Tax advantages are one more reason to marry: this strengthens.
  4. DIndividuals in conflict-ridden relationships are more prone to illness than happy couples.✗ Compares unhappy couples with happy couples, not married people with single people.
  5. EWealthier couples are four times more likely to marry than poorer couples.✓ If wealthier people are far more likely to marry, the wealth (and the health and happiness that come with it) may lead to marriage, not come from it.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer A
It is universally recognised that Africa’s conservation tourism provides employment, generates wealth and educates future generations while saving endangered species.Evidence / example So we should champion tourism for the sake of conservation.Intermediate conclusion Tourism in the Arctic is thus to be welcomed.Main conclusion As the Arctic ice melts, new shipping routes are opening up.Context It is unfair to characterise the presence of a large cruise ship in the Northwest Passage asIntermediate conclusion ‘extinction tourism’ that merely indulges people who want to view endangered animals before it is too late.Opposing view The tourists will return home better informed about the Arctic’s fragile ecosystem.Reason

Which one of the following best expresses the flaw in the above argument?

  1. AIt argues a general rule on the basis of one successful example of wildlife tourism.✓ From one success (Africa’s conservation tourism) it makes a general rule, “champion tourism”, then applies it to Arctic cruises, which may be very different.
  2. BIt assumes that wildlife tourists only want to see endangered animals.✗ The argument denies this. It says the tourists are not merely there to see endangered animals.
  3. CIt assumes that wildlife tourism is more important than successful conservation.✗ It champions tourism for the sake of conservation, so it does not rank tourism above conservation.
  4. DIt assumes that the cruise-ship tourists will see enough to return home committed to the conservation of the Arctic ecosystem.✗ It only claims the tourists come home “better informed”, not committed to conservation.
  5. EThe argument implies that conservation tourism will inevitably lead to the extinction of some species of animal.✗ The argument implies the opposite: tourism helps save species.
Tip Count the examples behind a general rule. Here there is one, from a very different place.

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer E
If the vice president did act unlawfully in this matter, he would definitely have had to resign.Reason Since we have confirmation that he has resigned,Evidence / example he must have broken the law.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AIf the snake that bit this woman was a king cobra, then she would be dead by now. As she is alive and well, the snake can’t have been a king cobra.✗ If A then B; not B; so not A. Sound reasoning with a different shape.
  2. BIf the snake had yellow markings, then it was a pit viper. You say it did have yellow markings, so it must have been a pit viper.✗ If A then B; A happened; so B. It starts from the “if” part, not the result.
  3. CIf what Mahmood saw was a diamondback rattlesnake, he would have been in real danger. Since there are no rattlesnakes where Mahmood was, he was actually quite safe.✗ If A then B; not A; so not B. It denies the “if” part.
  4. DIf a snake bite goes untreated for too long, there can be serious complications. Since I got prompt treatment, I didn’t have any complications.✗ If A then B; not A; so not B. The same shape as C, a different flaw.
  5. EIf the snake that bit this victim was venomous, then the wound would be very inflamed. We can see that there is severe inflammation, so the snake must have been venomous.✓ Same shape and same flaw: if A then B; B happened; so A. The wound could be inflamed for another reason, just as he could have resigned for another reason.
Tip All five are about snakes, so the topic is no help. Label the “if” part A and the “then” part B.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if A (acted unlawfully) B (had to resign). B happened (he resigned) so A (broke the law).
The flaw: he could have resigned for some other reason, so B does not prove A.

EIf A (venomous) → B (very inflamed). B happened (severe inflammation) → so A. ✓ Same shape, same flaw.
AIf A (king cobra) → B (dead). Not B (alive) → so not A. Sound, and a different shape. ✗
BIf A (yellow markings) → B (pit viper). A happened → so B. Starts from A, not B. ✗
CIf A (rattlesnake) → B (danger). Not A (no rattlesnakes there) → so not B. ✗
DIf A (untreated too long) → B (complications). Not A (prompt treatment) → so not B. ✗
Tip Ask two things of each option: which part is it given (A or B), and is that part true or denied? The original is given B, true.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer B
It’s common for people to agree in principle to socially useful projects such as the opening of centres for people with serious mental health problems.Context However, if the project is planned for the area where they live, they tend to object and suggest that alternative locations would be preferable.Evidence / example While they approve of the general idea of such projects, they won’t tolerate any negative effects on their own lives. We really don’t want to go back to outdated practices of housing people with mental health problems in remote areas with no contact with the general population.Reason So we have to accept that these projects can only work if we live near, and interact with, the people they are designed to help. Practise what you preach!Main conclusion

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. APeople who think sport has been tainted by bribery and performance-enhancing drugs should not buy tickets to sporting events.✗ About not supporting something you disapprove of, not accepting the downside of something you approve of.
  2. BPeople who think more money should be spent on the health service should not complain when their income tax is increased to fund it.✓ Principle: if you back something in principle, you must accept the cost it brings to you. Backing more health spending means accepting the tax that pays for it.
  3. CParents who think too much ‘screen time’ is bad for young children should not buy them so many electronic gadgets.✗ Also “practise what you preach”, but about avoiding something you think is bad, not accepting a cost of something you support.
  4. DPeople who choose to be vegetarians should seek to persuade others not to eat meat or fish.✗ About persuading others, not accepting a cost yourself.
  5. EPeople who have benefitted from grants or bursaries for university education should lobby for others to have the same advantages.✗ About passing on a benefit you had, not bearing a cost.

Working: write the principle first

People back mental health centres in general but object when one is planned near them (the cost to them) the projects only work if we live near them so accept it: practise what you preach.
So the principle: if you support something in principle, you must accept the cost it brings to you.

BWant more health spending → accept the higher income tax that pays for it. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
AThink sport is tainted → don’t buy tickets. Acting on disapproval; no cost of something you back. ✗
CThink screen time is bad → don’t buy gadgets. Acting on disapproval again. ✗
DVegetarian → persuade others. No personal cost accepted. ✗
EHad a grant → lobby for others. Passing on a benefit, not putting up with a cost. ✗
Trap! “Practise what you preach” loosely fits A and C too. Be precise: you support a project, so you must put up with its downside for you.

14(Q27 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer B
People are quick to connect attempts to reduce the effects of climate change with the protection of the environment.Opposing view However, responding to climate change is much more important for humanity than for nature.Main conclusion In the history of the planet, the relative stability of temperatures in the last ten thousand years is the exception rather than the norm.Evidence / example Nature has always found a way to adapt to large temperature variations but human civilisations, all of which have evolved in the last ten thousand years, have developed in a period with unusually reliable and predictable weather.Reason Nature will find a way to move on, but climate change is potentially devastating for humans.Intermediate conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  1. APeople are quick to connect climate change policies with environmental protection.✗ The opposing view the author argues against.
  2. BActing against climate change is more important for humans than for the environment.✓ “However, responding to climate change is much more important for humanity than for nature.” It answers the common view, and the rest supports it.
  3. CNature has always found a way to adapt to large temperature variations.✗ A reason: nature adapts, so climate change matters less for it.
  4. DHuman civilisations have not yet been exposed to really large variations in temperature.✗ Only a step in the reasoning: civilisations grew up in stable weather.
  5. ENature will find a way to adapt, but climate change is potentially devastating for humans.✗ The last sentence: an intermediate conclusion that leads straight to the main one.
Tip “However” straight after a common belief often flags the main conclusion.

15(Q28 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer B
Chiropractors rely heavily on manipulating their patients’ spines.Context This has benefits for some patients,Reason but more than half of all patients suffer mild to moderate adverse effects after seeing a chiropractor. These are mostly local and referred pains that usually last for two to three days.Evidence / example Chiropractors often claim that these are necessary steps on the road to getting better.Opposing view However, several hundred cases have been documented in which patients were seriously and often permanently damaged after chiropractic manipulations. What usually happens in these tragic instances is that, upon manipulation of the upper spine, an artery supplying the brain is overstretched and simply breaks up, leading to a stroke that can prove fatal.Evidence / example

Which one of the following is a conclusion that can be drawn from the above passage?

  1. AChiropractic therapy should be banned.✗ Too strong. Some patients benefit, and a ban needs more than this.
  2. BChiropractic therapy may do more harm than good.✓ Benefits for some, but side effects for more than half and hundreds of serious or fatal cases. So it may do more harm than good.
  3. CThe spine is the most delicate part of the human body.✗ No other part of the body is compared.
  4. DPeople should see a doctor before going to a chiropractor.✗ Doctors are never mentioned.

16(Q29 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer A
Drivers found to be committing offences such as speeding are now offered the choice between paying the police for a re-education course or taking the traditional penalty of paying a fine and receiving penalty points on their licence.Context It is no surprise that the number of motorists opting for re-education courses has tripled in five years,Evidence / example but the high cost of such courses has led to accusations that police forces might use the new system for their own benefit. They could easily set up speed traps, catch a number of motorists slightly over the limit, and generate easy income for themselves.Reason Re-education courses are a good idea in theory, but open to abuse by cash-starved police forces.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AThe cost of running re-education courses is less than the combined contributions of participants.✓ Police can only “generate easy income” if the course fees bring in more than the courses cost to run.
  2. BRe-education is more effective than punishment in preventing further motoring offences.✗ Not needed. “Good in theory” does not depend on beating fines at stopping offences.
  3. CPolice forces are more interested in generating income than in preventing motoring offences.✗ Too strong. The argument only says the system is open to abuse, not that police care more about money.
  4. DThe number of driving offences committed in the last five years has tripled.✗ Misreads the evidence: the number choosing courses tripled, not the number of offences.
  5. EFear of losing a driving licence is greater than fear of financial hardship.✗ Not needed. Why drivers pick the course does not affect whether police could profit.
Tip Negation test: if the courses cost more to run than they bring in, there is no easy income and the worry falls.

17(Q33 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer A
A band of rebellious traders in Wales has decided to take their whole town ‘offshore’ in protest at the insignificant amounts of UK tax paid by big businesses. 24(Q46 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer E
Your cake will be tasty only if you use fresh ingredients which you can only buy at the market.Reason You did not go to the market,Evidence / example so your cake will not be tasty.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AYou can only be happy if you are healthy. To be healthy, you have to eat a lot of vegetables. You eat a lot of vegetables so you must be happy.✗ A chain of needed conditions, but the base one (vegetables) is met and happiness is assumed. Needing something does not make it enough.
  2. BShe either went to school or to the playground. She is not in the playground, so she must have gone to school.✗ An either/or with one option ruled out: a different shape.
  3. CIf you light a candle, there is light and, if there is light, I am able to read to you. You must have lit a candle because I can read.✗ Starts from the result (I can read) and works back to the cause: a flaw the original does not make.
  4. DListening to loud music through earphones damages hearing. His hearing is fine which means that he must not have listened to loud music through earphones.✗ Only one step, and loud music is something that causes damage, not something you need. No chain.
  5. EGood knowledge of the car is necessary for fixing it. Without fixing the car, we will not arrive at the wedding in time. We will surely be late as we do not have good knowledge of the car.✓ Same chain: you need good knowledge to fix the car, and a fixed car to arrive on time. No knowledge, so no fix, so late. Just like no market, no fresh ingredients, no tasty cake.
Tip Count the links. The original has two “only if” links in a chain, and the very first one is missing.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: need B (fresh ingredients) to get C (tasty cake). Need A (market) to get B. Not A (no market) so not B so not C.

ENeed B (fixing the car) to get C (on time). Need A (good knowledge) to get B. Not A → so not B → so not C (late). ✓ Same chain, first link missing.
ANeed B (healthy) to get C (happy). Need A (vegetables) to get B. A happened → so C. Treats needed as enough. ✗
BEither X (school) or Y (playground). Not Y → so X. Either/or elimination. ✗
CIf A (candle) → B (light) → C (read). C happened → so A. Works backwards from the result. ✗
DIf A (loud music) → B (damage). Not B → so not A. One step only, no chain of needed conditions. ✗
Tip Turn every “only if”, “necessary” and “without… not” into “need X for Y”. Then line up the links.
