1(Q1 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer E
Heroin and cocaine are not the only illegal drugs with a terrible impact on global health. Counterfeit medicines can also do damage.Reason Some of these fakes contain potentially toxic chemicals, and when taken to treat a disease they can kill.Evidence / example Others are like homeopathic remedies – while harmless in themselves, as they contain no active ingredients, they do not work as advertised.Comparison Efforts to tackle drug crime have prioritised illegal drugs and so have not dealt with crimes that are just as serious.Intermediate conclusion This should now be remedied.Main conclusion

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above passage?

  1. AHeroin and cocaine have a terrible impact on global health.✗ Background that sets the scene. Nothing is built on it.
  2. BCounterfeit medicines can be just as dangerous as heroin and cocaine.✗ The intermediate step (“crimes that are just as serious”). It is the reason for the “should”.
  3. CHomeopathic remedies are not effective.✗ A detail from the homeopathy comparison.
  4. DCounterfeit medicines may contain toxic chemicals.✗ Evidence for why fakes do damage.
  5. EMore time and effort should be put into tackling counterfeit medicines.✓ “This should now be remedied” means: stop neglecting fake medicines and tackle them too. E spells that out.
Tip A short “This should…” at the end is the conclusion. Work out what “this” refers to.

2(Q3 in the original paper) FlawAnswer B
All European countries have seen a decline in the number of people who vote in elections.Context In the 2005 General Election in the UK only 61% of those eligible to vote did so. When this percentage is further broken down to show the number of people who voted for the political party that went on to form the government, the number is even lower. Of those who did vote, only 35% voted for the party that won power.Evidence / example It becomes clear, therefore, that the party voted into government did not have legitimacy, if 'legitimacy' is defined as the government having the support of the majority of the people.Main conclusion

Which one of the following represents a flaw in the argument above?

  1. AThose eligible to vote who do not vote forfeit the right to criticise the composition of the government.✗ An opinion about non-voters. It does not touch the step from votes to support.
  2. BA government may have the support of eligible voters who did not exercise their right to vote.✓ The 39% who stayed at home are counted as not supporting the winner. Some of them may well support it, so it might still have a majority.
  3. CThe responsibility for lack of legitimacy lies with non-voters, not the government.✗ About who is to blame, which is not what the argument concludes.
  4. DThe argument does not give an explanation for the decline in voting.✗ The argument doesn’t need to explain the decline to reach its conclusion.
  5. EThe definition of legitimacy needs to be revised in the light of low voter turnout.✗ A suggestion for the future. The argument uses the definition it gives, so this is not a flaw in it.
Tip “Didn’t vote for it” is not the same as “doesn’t support it”.

3(Q4 in the original paper) Draw a conclusionAnswer E
Only John Lewis, the nationally adored and trusted UK retail group, can get away with the shamelessly sentimental marketing that they do.Reason More than 100,000 people watched and re-watched on YouTube in the first week it was available their most popular 'Always a Woman' advertisement.Evidence / example If anyone else had dreamt it up, they would have been pilloried as sexist and out-of-touch.Reason In it, John Lewis offers a politically incorrect view of modern womanhood: pair-bonding and breeding are significant milestones, they say, not making CEO of a company.Context Surprisingly however, figures show that the retailer has magnificently outperformed every single rival.Evidence / example

Which one of the following can be drawn as a conclusion from the above passage?

  1. AThe British public will accept anything from John Lewis.✗ “Anything” is far too strong. We only know they accept this kind of advert.
  2. BMarketing that plays on our emotions is morally reprehensible.✗ No moral verdict is drawn. The passage says it worked.
  3. CMarriage and motherhood suit women more than business achievement.✗ This is the advert’s view, which the passage calls politically incorrect. The author doesn’t endorse it.
  4. DThe number of hits on YouTube is a good measure of success.✗ Success is shown by the sales figures. The YouTube views are never called a measure of anything.
  5. EIn the right hands an appeal to sentiment can be a successful marketing tool.✓ Sentimental ads + a trusted brand (“only John Lewis can get away with”) + beating every rival = sentiment can work for the right company.
Tip “In the right hands” is the option’s way of saying “only John Lewis can get away with it”.

4(Q5 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer C
In the 1920s and 1930s there were successes for extremist parties in elections across Europe. Several of these parties formed governments and were directly involved in starting the Second World War. In recent elections extremist parties were particularly successful at the expense of the more traditional parties.Evidence / example It is possible that this dangerous situation will get worse.Intermediate conclusion Our schools must teach children about the link between extremist politics and war in order to reduce the likelihood of future wars.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AEvents in history inevitably repeat themselves in the future.✗ “Inevitably” is too strong. The passage only says things could get worse.
  2. BIt is not the job of parents to teach children about politics.✗ Parents are never mentioned. Schools teaching it doesn’t mean parents can’t.
  3. CTeaching about the link between extremist politics and war will cause fewer people to vote for extremist parties.✓ Teaching only cuts the chance of war if it changes how people vote. That missing link is C.
  4. DExtremism is the main reason that countries go to war.✗ “Main reason” is too strong. Extremism only needs to be one cause of war.
  5. EThe recent election success of extremist parties will continue to grow.✗ The passage only says it is “possible”. The lessons would still make sense if it doesn’t grow.
Tip Negation test: “the lessons will NOT change how anyone votes”. Then they can’t reduce war, so C is assumed.

5(Q9 in the original paper) WeakenAnswer C
A study of shooting victims in Philadelphia has found that people who carry guns are far more likely to be shot than those who are unarmed. Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania looked at 677 shootings over two and a half years, taking into account various factors such as the age and socioeconomic status of those involved. The researchers concluded that armed citizens were 4.5 times more likely to get shot and 4.2 times more likely to get killed than those who were unarmed.Evidence / example Therefore, it is significantly safer not to carry a gun in Philadelphia.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the above argument?

  1. ASome of those shot were carrying concealed weapons.✗ Concealed or not, they were still armed. The link is unchanged.
  2. BPeople carrying guns have a sense of empowerment.✗ A feeling of empowerment says nothing about how safe they actually are.
  3. CPeople who are at risk of getting shot are more likely to carry a gun.✓ Reverse cause: being at risk makes people carry a gun, not the gun putting them at risk. Then putting the gun down won’t make them safer.
  4. DPeople carrying guns are likely to overreact in potentially violent situations.✗ Overreacting explains why carrying a gun is dangerous, so it strengthens.
  5. EThe bearing of arms is protected by the Second Amendment of the US Constitution.✗ Whether it is legal has nothing to do with whether it is safe.
Tip Correlation (armed and shot) → cause (the gun). Ask: could it run the other way?

6(Q10 in the original paper) FlawAnswer B
Recurring dreams (those that you keep on having time after time) make good sense when you think of them as messages from your unconscious. Some messages are too important to send just once in case you don't understand them first time around or don't realise their importance.Reason So, a recurring dream must contain a message that is worth repeating over and over.Main conclusion

Which one of the following identifies the flaw in the reasoning in the above argument?

  1. ASupernatural explanations for natural events do not make any sense.✗ Messages from your own unconscious aren’t supernatural, and this is a counter-claim, not the faulty step.
  2. BEven if an explanation makes good sense it is not necessarily true.✓ It starts with “make good sense if you think of them as messages” and ends with “must contain a message”. A neat story is not proof.
  3. CNot everyone remembers the dreams they have had.✗ Remembering dreams has nothing to do with the step from “makes sense” to “must”.
  4. DNot all dreams contain messages.✗ The argument is only about recurring dreams. It never says all dreams carry messages.
  5. ESometimes we may dream that we have had a dream before.✗ A side oddity about dreams. It doesn’t touch the reasoning.
Tip Watch the jump in strength: “makes good sense” → “must”.

Currently 56% of British school children do not study a foreign language at all at GCSE, and seven university language departments have closed in the past seven years.Evidence / example People with only one language feel disempowered, whereas those who speak other languages are comfortable to travel the world. Once you can speak their languages, some of the mystique of foreigners goes away and you feel less intimidated by them. 9(Q16 in the original paper) AssumptionAnswer B
School science lessons were once the place of bangs, flashes, smells, blood and droplets of mercury on the bench. Few, if any, people ever died from these experiences. Now, the activities that once made science fun are rare – banned on health and safety grounds.Evidence / example It has been reported that governments are concerned about the decreasing percentage of students enrolling on science courses at universities.Context If they do wish to reverse this trend, they need to take steps to curtail the pervasive 'health and safety' culture in which we now live.Main conclusion

Which one of the following is an underlying assumption of the above argument?

  1. AIt does not matter if only a few people died as a result of school science lessons.✗ Not needed. The point is about university numbers, not whether a few deaths matter.
  2. BDoing exciting activities in science lessons leads to a higher take-up of science courses at university.✓ Bringing back bangs and flashes only lifts university numbers if fun lessons lead to more science students. Nothing in the passage says so.
  3. CThere is no reason to ban science practicals that are only minimally dangerous.✗ Not needed. The argument is about reversing a trend, not whether bans are justified.
  4. DHealth and safety legislation has had only a negative effect on education.✗ “Only a negative effect” is too strong. Less red tape just needs to help here.
  5. EScience is only fun if it is dangerous.✗ “Only fun if dangerous” is too strong. The argument just needs the banned activities to have been fun.

10(Q17 in the original paper) StrengthenAnswer A
In the UK, house prices have risen at far greater rates than inflation, fuelled by unprecedented levels of borrowing.Evidence / example Because of these high prices, owning their own home has become unaffordable for many people. Those who are able to 'own' their own home must do so burdened by a huge level of debt.Intermediate conclusion Since the recent financial crisis can be attributed in part to those who were unable to meet the repayments on their debts,Reason governments should attempt to tackle the problem at its root and take action to prevent a continued rise in house prices above the level of inflation.Main conclusion

Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the above argument?

  1. AThe gap between the average house price and average salary has continued to widen over the past five years.✓ Prices still racing ahead of pay means homes keep getting less affordable and debts bigger. So the problem is ongoing and action is needed.
  2. BHouse prices have fallen sharply and are no longer increasing at such dramatic rates.✗ If prices are already falling, there is less need to act: this weakens.
  3. CMortgage companies are more reluctant to lend money without substantial deposits.✗ Lenders are already curbing risky borrowing without government help: this weakens.
  4. DThere are few practical interventions that governments could make to lower house prices.✗ If governments can’t do much, the call to act is weaker.
  5. EMany people are dependent on the value of their property increasing more than the value of their loan in order to pay off their mortgage.✗ Stopping price rises would hurt these people: a reason not to act.
Tip Strengthen a “should act” argument by showing the problem is real and still growing.

11(Q21 in the original paper) FlawAnswer C
We need a fair policy for dealing with drivers whose ability is impaired by drugs, such as cannabis, amphetamines and cocaine.Context Setting limits for driving under the influence of drugs may be difficult. Tests would be necessary for the levels in the blood of each of these drugs. But, such limits may not reflect the complex interactions between drugs and their effect on driving: a driver may be severely impaired but under the limit for each individual drug. Also, setting a legal limit for the use of a substance that is illegal seems self-contradictory.Reason So to solve the problem of drug-driving there should be a new offence of driving with any level of illegal drug in the body.Main conclusion This would make it much easier to catch and convict drug-drivers – the ultimate deterrent.Reason

Which one of the following identifies the flaw in the above argument?

  1. ASome countries have legalised the drugs that impair driving ability.✗ Other countries’ laws don’t matter. The policy is for a place where these drugs are illegal.
  2. BDrivers whose ability was not impaired may be convicted.✗ A side effect the author accepts: he says any limit for an illegal drug is self-contradictory, so any level counts.
  3. CSome legally available drugs may impair driving ability.✓ The problem is drivers impaired by drugs; the fix only covers illegal drugs. Drivers impaired by legal drugs slip through, so it doesn’t “solve the problem of drug-driving”.

  5. ESome illegal drugs can improve driving ability.✗ Even if true, the drugs are still illegal, and it doesn’t show the plan misses impaired drivers.

12(Q22 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer A
Adding fluoride to the water supply would not,Main conclusion as some claim,Opposing view improve dental health.Main conclusion If fluoride really did improve dental health, one would expect people living in areas where water is naturally fluoridated to have healthier teeth than people whose water is fluoride free.Reason In fact, research suggests that there is no difference in dental health between the two groups.Evidence / example

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. AIf badgers still lived in this sett, there would be badger droppings at its entrance. Since there are no droppings, there are no badgers here.✓ Same skeleton: if X were true, we would see Y; there is no Y; so X is not true.
  2. BThe only animals in Britain that leave little piles of soil all over the lawn are moles. So those piles of soil on our lawn mean we have moles.✗ Only X leaves Y; we see Y; so X. It starts from a sign that is there.
  3. CThe chicken house needs to be secured against foxes. There are definitely foxes living in this area. If any foxes get into the chicken house, they will kill the chickens.✗ A recommendation backed by reasons (foxes about, foxes kill). No “if X then Y; no Y”.
  4. DThe strange noise we heard last night may have been a hedgehog. I cannot think what else it could have been, and I know for a fact that there are hedgehogs around here.✗ A guess (“may have been”) because nothing else comes to mind. No test that fails.
  5. EIf there are grey squirrels in an area, it is unlikely that there are any red squirrels. The animals we just saw were grey squirrels. So there are probably not any red squirrels in this area.✗ If X, probably not Y; X; so probably not Y. It confirms the “if” part instead of denying the “then” part.

Working: strip it to a skeleton

Original: if X (fluoride helps teeth) we would see Y (better teeth where water is naturally fluoridated). No Y (no difference) so not X.

AIf X (badgers here) → Y (droppings at the entrance). No Y (no droppings) → so not X (no badgers). ✓ Same shape.
BOnly X (moles) makes Y (soil piles). Y is there → so X. Reasons from a sign that is present. ✗
CShould secure the house ← foxes nearby + foxes kill chickens. A recommendation, no failed prediction. ✗
DMaybe X (hedgehog) ← can’t think of anything else + hedgehogs live here. A guess by lack of alternatives. ✗
EIf X (greys) → probably not Y (reds). X is true → probably not Y. Affirms the “if”, not denies the “then”. ✗
Tip Look for all three parts in order: “if X, then Y”, “no Y”, “so no X”.

13(Q23 in the original paper) PrincipleAnswer A
Inhabitants of towns and roads with odd names often complain that property values are lower and that they face embarrassment when describing where they live.Context People have a right to decide as a community what is in the best interests of that communityReason and, therefore, local residents should have the right to rename their town or road if they wish.Main conclusion

Which one of the following illustrates the principle used in the above argument?

  1. AResidents of an area, not central government agencies, should be able to decide where new school buildings are located.✓ Principle: a community should decide what is best for itself. A gives local residents (not outsiders) the decision on new schools.
  2. BTown planning needs to be conducted by outside agencies to avoid neglecting the interests of all groups.✗ The opposite: outsiders decide for the community.
  3. CMayors should be elected only on the basis of their abilities, not how long they have lived in an area.✗ About what voters should look for in a mayor, not who gets to decide.
  4. DPeople who have only recently moved to an area should not expect to be part of the local community.✗ About who belongs to a community, not its right to decide.
  5. ESchool pupils should obey the rules set by the headteacher and staff.✗ People following rules set by those in charge: not a group deciding for itself.

Working: write the principle first

Odd names hurt residents communities have the right to decide what is in their own interests so residents may rename their town or road.
So the principle: the people affected should decide matters that concern their own community.

ALocal residents, not central government, choose where new schools go. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
BOutside agencies plan the town. Takes the decision away from the community. ✗
CMayors chosen on ability. A rule about who to pick, not who decides. ✗
DNewcomers aren’t part of the community. About belonging, not deciding. ✗
EPupils obey staff. Those in charge decide for the group. ✗
Tip Strip out the topic (road names) and keep the rule (the community decides). Then find it somewhere new.

Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  2. BWe must not allow the data protection principle to be abandoned.✓ “We must not allow this principle to be abandoned”. Everything else (threat to privacy, liberty, possible future misuse) is a reason for it.

19(Q35 in the original paper) Main conclusionAnswer B
Paying bonuses of at least £1 million to high-flying stockbrokers and employees of banks cannot be justified.Main conclusion Such massive bonuses encourage these high-flyers to take unacceptable risks with shareholders' assets in the pursuit of profits. Furthermore, the system discourages our brightest young talent from taking jobs in industry, where the rewards are much more modest but where there is a great need for such talent. Which one of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the above argument?

  3. CVery large bonuses encourage City firms to take unjustifiable risks.✗ The first reason, and it is the high-flyers, not the firms, who take the risks.

24(Q46 in the original paper) Parallel reasoningAnswer C
If you want to get a good job then you need to write a good application letter.Reason You didn't get the last job that you applied for,Evidence / example so you can't have written a good application letter.Main conclusion

Which one of the following most closely parallels the reasoning used in the above argument?

  1. ARecording the best time in your heat would guarantee you a place in the final race. You don't have a place in the final, so you can't have recorded the best time in your heat.✗ Here A guarantees B (sufficient), so “no B, so no A” is valid. Different shape.
  2. BTo join the army you need to pass a physical examination. Since your health is very poor, you won't be able to join the army.✗ Need A for B; no A; so no B. Valid, and it denies the condition, not the result.
  3. CYou can't be part of the yachting team without a certificate to show that you can swim. Since you were not allowed on the yachting team, you must not have a certificate to show that you can swim.✓ Same skeleton and same flaw: A is needed for B; no B; so no A. But you can have A and still miss B.
  4. DThe manager will promote only people who have worked well in the previous year. Since you've been working hard you should expect a promotion.✗ Need A for B; have A (sort of); so B. Treats necessary as enough: a different flaw.
  5. EIf you want to get to the meeting on time then you need to leave within the next 20 minutes. Since you are nearly ready to leave, you should get to the meeting on time.✗ Need A for B; nearly have A; so B. Again necessary treated as enough.

Working: necessary vs sufficient

Original: you need A (good letter) to get B (good job) you didn’t get B so you can’t have had A.

The flaw: A is only needed, not enough. A great letter can still lose to a better candidate.

CNeed A (swimming certificate) for B (yachting team) → not on the team (no B) → so no certificate (no A). ✓ Identical shape and flaw.
AA (best time) guarantees B (final) → no B → so no A. Sufficient, not necessary, so this one is sound. ✗
BNeed A (pass physical) for B (army) → no A (poor health) → so no B. Denies A, not B, and it is sound. ✗
DNeed A (worked well) for B (promotion) → have A (working hard) → so B. Necessary treated as enough. ✗
ENeed A (leave within 20 min) for B (on time) → nearly have A → so B. Necessary treated as enough. ✗
Tip Match the three parts in order: “need A for B”, “no B”, “so no A”. The options that start “have A” are a different mistake.

The credit crisis has significantly damaged pensions and pensioners. Millions of people coming up to retirement now, without a final salary pension, are at risk of poverty.Evidence / example It is previous government policy decisions, not just the credit crisis, that are to blame.Intermediate conclusion We have had a pensions crisis building up for the last few years.Evidence / example One should always take responsibility for one's mistakes.Reason Therefore, the Government must act!Main conclusion

Which one of the following best illustrates the principle underlying the argument above?

  1. AIf I had not tried to carry so many plates at once I would not have dropped some when I slipped, so I should replace the broken ones.✓ Principle: if your mistake helped cause harm, you should put it right, even if bad luck played a part too. The slip is the “credit crisis”; carrying too many plates is the mistake.
  2. BMy dog always attacks people who come to the house, so I keep her in the back garden.✗ Preventing future harm, not putting right damage your mistake has already caused.
  3. CThe Government should take action to help people who have lost possessions in the recent floods.✗ Same topic (the Government must act), but the floods are not the Government’s mistake.
  4. DWe should all feel responsible for our own future security so it is right that the Government provides a safety net.✗ About personal responsibility and a safety net. Nobody made a mistake.
  5. EWhile there are some situations for which we cannot plan, if we fail to insure ourselves we only have ourselves to blame.✗ Says who is to blame but stops there. Nothing about acting to put it right.

Working: write the principle first

Bad luck (credit crisis) + our own mistake (past policy) harm (pensioners at risk) take responsibility for your mistakes so act to put it right.
So the principle: if your mistake contributed to the harm, you must take action to repair it.

ABad luck (slipped) + my mistake (too many plates) → broken plates → so I replace them. ✓ Same principle, new setting.
BDangerous dog → keep her away. Stopping future harm, no mistake owned. ✗
CThe Government helps flood victims, but it didn’t cause the floods. ✗
DFeel responsible for your own future → so a safety net is right. No mistake, no repair. ✗
EDidn’t insure → only ourselves to blame. Blame, but no duty to act. ✗
Tip Both halves must match: the mistake and the duty to fix it. C has the action without the mistake; E has the blame without the action.
