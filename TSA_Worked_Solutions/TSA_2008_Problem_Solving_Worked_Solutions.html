1(Q2 in the original paper) Testing the options

A child's bus fare is cheaper than the adult fare1 but is more than half the adult fare2. The total cost of a single journey for an adult and two children is £1.203. Adult fares are all multiples of 10 p4.

What is the adult fare5?

  1. A30 p
  2. B40 p
  3. C50 p
  4. D60 p
  5. E70 p

Reading it properly

  • 1Child < adult.
  • 2Child > half the adult. Strictly more, so exactly half is not allowed.
  • 3adult + child + child = 120p.
  • 4Only the adult fare has to be a round 10p. The child fare can be any amount.
  • 5Five adult fares to test: perfect for trying each one.

Working it out

  1. Write the total as one line, then the child fare drops out of any adult fare:

    adult + 2 × child = 120p
    child = (120 − adult) ÷ 2

  2. Test each option against both rules (cheaper than adult, more than half of adult):

    A: adult 30 child (120 − 30) ÷ 2 = 45: dearer than the adult ✗
    B: adult 40 child 40: the same, not cheaper ✗
    C: adult 50 child 35: between 25 and 50 ✓
    D: adult 60 child 30: exactly half, not more than half ✗
    E: adult 70 child 25: less than half (35) ✗
  3. adult fare = 50 p
C50p: the child fare is then 35p, which is cheaper than 50p and more than 25p.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 30p makes the child fare 45p, which is more than the adult fare.
  • B✗ 40p makes the child fare 40p too: the same as the adult, not cheaper.
  • D✗ 60p makes the child fare 30p, which is exactly half. The rule says more than half.
  • E✗ 70p makes the child fare 25p, which is less than half of 70p (35p).
Tip Child = (120 − adult) ÷ 2. Only 50 gives a child fare (35) strictly between half the adult (25) and the adult (50).
2(Q6 in the original paper) Multiples (LCM)

Steven has a customised motorcycle that he is very proud of and shows regularly in competitions. It is very important to him that his presentation is perfect and he likes to stop in front of the judges with both his tyre valves in the same position1. The front tyre is smaller than the rear to aid its handling. The circumference of the front wheel is 2m and the circumference of the rear wheel is 2.5m2.

Steven starts with both valves in the same position at the bottom of each wheel.

How far must he drive before the valves line up again at the bottom of each wheel3?

  1. A2.0 m
  2. B2.5 m
  3. C4.5 m
  4. D10.0 m
  5. E12.5 m

Reading it properly

  • 1A valve is back at the bottom after every whole turn of its wheel.
  • 2One turn moves the bike one circumference: 2 m for the front, 2.5 m for the rear.
  • 3Both at once: the first distance that is a whole number of turns for both wheels (the lowest common multiple).

Working it out

  1. List where each valve is back at the bottom:

    front: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 … m
    rear: 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5 … m
  3. first match = 10 m front 10 ÷ 2 = 5 turns, rear 10 ÷ 2.5 = 4 turns
    answer = 10.0 m
    Tip Hate decimals? Count in half-metres: 2 m = 4 halves and 2.5 m = 5 halves. LCM of 4 and 5 is 20 halves = 10 m.
D10 m: the front wheel turns 5 times and the rear wheel 4 times.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 2 m is one turn of the front wheel only. The rear valve has gone 0.8 of a turn, so it isn’t at the bottom.
  • B✗ 2.5 m is one turn of the rear wheel only. The front has done 1.25 turns.
  • C✗ 4.5 m just adds the two circumferences (2 + 2.5). That isn’t a whole number of turns for either wheel.
  • E✗ 12.5 m is 5 rear turns, but the front would be on 6.25 turns. (And 10 m comes first anyway.)
Tip Multiples of 2.5: 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10. The first one that is also a multiple of 2 is 10 m.
3(Q7 in the original paper) Codes

I have so many four digit code numbers to remember that I cannot keep them all in my head. The banks say you should not write down your Personal Identification Numbers (PINs) for cash and credit cards and keep them in a wallet with the cards. So I can find out what the numbers are, I have devised a code which should be complicated enough to confuse any thief who steals my wallet. I code all the numbers using 1=A, 2=B up to 9=I, 0=J1. Then I write the coded numbers down backwards2 so 1234 is written DCBA. Finally at the end of each coded number I add an extra letter3 between A and J to say what that particular code is of: C is for credit card; F is for phone card; B is for bicycle lock combination4; E is for my Eastern Bank card and D is for my Denton Building Society card.

My list looks as follows:

ADGEF
CJDBE
BGFAC
GJACD
DFGCB

What is my bicycle lock combination5?

  1. A4673
  2. B5741
  3. C7613
  4. D4356
  5. E3764

Reading it properly

  • 1Letter = position in the alphabet, with J standing for 0.
  • 2Backwards! After decoding, turn the digits round.
  • 3The type letter is the 5th (last) letter, not the first.
  • 4So we want the line that ends in B.
  • 5One line only: the bike lock.

Working it out

  1. Find the bike lock by its last letter:

    A D G E | F = phone card
    C J D B | E = Eastern Bank
    B G F A | C = credit card
    G J A C | D = Denton
    D F G C | B = bicycle lock ✓

  2. Decode D F G C, then undo the backwards writing:

    D F G C 4 6 7 3 reversed 3764
  3. Check Test the method on their own example: 1234 is written DCBA. Decoding DCBA gives 4321, and reversing gives 1234 back. Same method.
E3764: the line ending in B is DFGC, which decodes to 4673, and reversed that is 3764.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 4673 decodes D F G C but forgets to turn it back round.
  • B✗ 5741 is the phone card line (A D G E, ending in F), decoded and reversed.
  • C✗ 7613 takes the line that starts with B (B G F A C) and reads G F A C forwards. The type letter goes at the end, not the start.
  • D✗ 4356 would need the letters D C E F (or F E C D) in the list, and no line has them.
Tip Line ending in B: DFGC. Decode 4673, reverse: 3764.
4(Q8 in the original paper) 3D views

The drawing shows a sculpture as seen from above1.

Which of these could not2 be a view from one of the sides?

  1. Aview A
  2. Bview B
  3. Cview C
  4. Dview D
  5. Eview E

Reading it properly

  • 1From above you only see a small square inside a big one. It could be a block sticking up, a hole going down, or a shallow dip. We don’t know which!
  • 2Four views can be explained by some sculpture. Find the one that can’t.

Working it out

  1. For each option, invent a sculpture that would give it and still look like the top view:

    • B: a small cube sitting in the middle of the top. From above you see its square. ✓
    • D: the same, plus a square tunnel through the side. ✓
    • C: a square shaft down from the top and a square tunnel through the sides. ✓
    • E: the small square is a shallow dip in the top, hidden from the side. ✓

  2. A has a notch in its outline. You can only see sky through the top of a side view if the gap goes all the way through, front to back. That is a slot across the top:

  3. From above, a slot would show as two lines right across the top, not a small closed square. So the one that is not a side view is A.

    Check A side view shows the outline of the whole sculpture. A hole in the middle of the top is hidden behind the front wall, so it can’t make a dent in the outline.
AView A: its notch would need a slot right across the top, which the top view does not show.

Why not the others?

  • B✗ B works: a small block standing on top of the middle shows as the inner square from above.
  • C✗ C works: a square tunnel through the sides (with a shaft down from the top) shows as a square inside the side view.
  • D✗ D works: a block on top and a tunnel through the side.
  • E✗ E works: if the inner square is a shallow dip in the top, the side just looks like a plain block.
Tip A notch in a side outline means a slot right through, which would show from above as a strip across the top. The top view has a small square instead, so A.
5(Q12 in the original paper) Two unknowns

Pierre and Marc are waiters. In one particular month, Pierre worked 30 sessions at the normal rate and 10 sessions at the overtime rate1. Marc worked 20 sessions at the normal rate and 5 sessions at the overtime rate2. At the end of this month, Pierre earned 700 euros. Marc earned 425 euros3.

What is the overtime rate4 per session?

  1. A35 euros
  2. B30 euros
  3. C25 euros
  4. D20 euros
  5. E15 euros

Reading it properly

  • 1Pierre: 30 normal + 10 overtime.
  • 2Marc: 20 normal + 5 overtime. Exactly half of Pierre’s overtime: handy!
  • 3Their pay: €700 and €425.
  • 4We want the overtime rate, not the normal one.

Working it out

  1. Write both months as a line each (N = normal rate, V = overtime rate):

    Pierre: 30N + 10V = 700
    Marc: 20N + 5V = 425

  2. Double Marc, so both have 10 overtime sessions. Then the only difference is 10 normal sessions:

    2 × Marc: 40N + 10V = 850
    850 − 700 = 150 10N = 150 N = €15

  3. Put N = 15 back into Pierre:

    30 × 15 = 450
    700 − 450 = 250 for 10 overtime sessions V = €25
    Check Marc: 20 × 15 + 5 × 25 = 300 + 125 = 425. It fits.
C€25 per overtime session (the normal rate is €15).

Why not the others?

  • A✗ With €35 overtime, Marc’s normal rate would be (425 − 175) ÷ 20 = €12.50, and then Pierre would earn €725, not €700.
  • B✗ With €30 overtime, Marc’s normal rate would be €13.75 and Pierre would earn €712.50.
  • D✗ With €20 overtime, Marc’s normal rate would be €16.25 and Pierre would earn €687.50.
  • E✗ €15 is the normal rate, not the overtime rate.
Tip Double Marc: 40N + 10V = 850. Take away Pierre: 10N = 150, so N = 15. Then 10V = 700 − 450 = 250, so V = 25.
6(Q13 in the original paper) Pie charts

The chart below shows the distribution of results this season for the Arlsea Strikers hockey team. They have played 24 matches1 to date and lost none2.

How many matches have they won3?

  1. A4
  2. B6
  3. C14
  4. D16
  5. E18

Reading it properly

  • 1The whole pie = 24 matches.
  • 2No losses, so every match is a win or a draw. Wins = 24 − draws.
  • 3All wins: home and away.

Working it out

  1. No numbers on the chart, so look for an easy angle. The two draw slices sit side by side, and their outside edges make a perfect right angle:

  2. draws = 90° = ¼ of the pie ¼ × 24 = 6
    wins = 24 − 6 = 18
    Check Same thing from the other side: the wins cover the other three-quarters, ¾ × 24 = 18.
E18 wins: the draws are a quarter of the pie (6 matches), so the other three-quarters are wins.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 4 is about the size of one draw slice (the away draws), not the wins.
  • B✗ 6 is the number of draws.
  • C✗ 14 is roughly the home wins on their own. It leaves out the away wins.
  • D✗ 16 would mean 8 draws, a third of the pie. The draws make a right angle, which is a quarter.
Tip The two draw slices form a right angle: ¼ of 24 = 6 draws, so 24 − 6 = 18 wins.
7(Q14 in the original paper) Nets & cubes

The cut out below was folded together to make a cube1.

Which of the following cubes was formed from this cut-out2?

  1. Acube A
  2. Bcube B
  3. Ccube C
  4. Dcube D
  5. Ecube E

Reading it properly

  • 1In a row of faces, two faces with one face between them end up opposite. You can never see opposite faces together.
  • 2Only one picture is possible. Knock the others out one rule at a time.

Working it out

  1. Name the faces and pair up the opposites (one face between them in a straight line):

  2. Test 1: opposite faces can’t both be seen.

    A: stripe on top and a stripe on the front ✗
    B: stripe on top and a stripe on the right ✗
    C: the thin line on top and the cross ✗
    D: stripe, line, band allowed
    E: stripe, cross, band allowed

  3. Test 2: D or E? Use a corner. In both, the top stripe runs straight into the front-left face. The stripe below the cross does that (it carries on the cross’s bar, and the line face is opposite the cross), so the top face is the bottom stripe. Now look at the corner in the middle of each picture:

    • D: the middle corner joins the line, the bottom stripe and a band. Every band runs into that corner (red rings above), but D’s band goes the other way ✗
    • E: the middle corner joins the cross, the bottom stripe and a band. A band never touches that corner, and E’s doesn’t. The cross’s bar also runs neatly into the stripe ✓
    answer = E
ECube E: cross at the front, the bottom stripe on top and a band on the right, all joined the way the net folds.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ A shows two plain stripes side by side, but the two stripe faces are opposite each other.
  • B✗ B also shows both stripe faces at once (top and right), and they are opposite.
  • C✗ C shows the thin line and the cross together, but they are opposite faces.
  • D✗ D’s faces are allowed, but the band is on the wrong diagonal: it should run into the middle corner where it meets the line and the bottom stripe.
Tip Opposites: stripe/stripe, band/band, cross/line. That kills A, B, C. In D the band misses the corner it shares with the line, so E.
8(Q18 in the original paper) Percentages

Inflation in Bolandia has been a steady 10% per year1 for many years. Evitan, a Bolandian citizen, bought his car one year ago for 500 Bols2. He now wants to trade in his car for an identical model. He can expect to receive 80% of the current new value of his existing car3. The price of new models has increased in line with inflation4.

How many extra5 Bols will he have to pay for his new car?

  1. A50
  2. B55
  3. C60
  4. D100
  5. E110

Reading it properly

  • 1Prices go up 10% a year.
  • 2Paid 500 Bols, one year ago.
  • 3Trap! 80% of what the car costs new today, not 80% of the 500 he paid.
  • 4So a new car now costs 500 + 10%.
  • 5Extra = new price − what he gets for the old car.

Working it out

  1. new price now = 500 + 10% = 500 + 50 = 550
  2. trade-in = 80% of 550 10% = 55, so 80% = 8 × 55 = 440
  3. extra = 550 − 440 = 110
    Tip Shortcut: he gets 80% back, so he pays the other 20% of today’s price. 20% of 550 = 110.
E110 Bols: a new car costs 550 and he gets 440 for the old one.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 50 is only the price rise (550 − 500). It forgets he gets just 80% back for his car.
  • B✗ 55 is 10% of 550, as if he lost only 10% of the value.
  • C✗ 60 is 500 − 440: it compares the trade-in with the old price, forgetting the new car now costs 550.
  • D✗ 100 is 20% of 500: it uses the old price and ignores the 10% inflation.
Tip He pays the 20% he doesn’t get back, of today’s price: 20% of 550 = 110.

10(Q20 in the original paper) Maps & routes

The roads on Bolandia all run either north - south or east - west1 and are laid out on a 5km grid. Ahmed and Wayne are delivery drivers. One morning, Ahmed starts at Abbeytown and drives 5km west then 15km south to reach Barneyville. He then drives 5km east to Carloston2. Wayne started at Barneyville, drove 5km south to Denburgh then 10km east and 15km north to Easterby3. Ahmed then rings Wayne to arrange to meet for lunch.

If one of them stays where he is4, what is the minimum distance5 the other must drive to meet him?

  1. A10 km
  2. B15 km
  3. C25 km
  4. D30 km
  5. E35 km

Reading it properly

  • 1No diagonal shortcuts: road distance = east–west part + north–south part.
  • 2Ahmed finishes at Carloston.
  • 3Wayne finishes at Easterby. Both trips pass through Barneyville, so measure from there.
  • 4Only where they are now matters, not how far they have already driven.
  • 5Shortest road route from Carloston to Easterby.

Working it out

  1. Put Barneyville at the centre and track each driver (E = east, N = north):

    Carloston: Barneyville + 5 E 5 E, 0 N
    Easterby: 5 S, then 10 E, then 15 N 10 E, 10 N
  3. east: 10 − 5 = 5 km
    north: 10 − 0 = 10 km
    shortest = 5 + 10 = 15 km
B15 km: Easterby is 5 km east and 10 km north of Carloston.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 10 km is only the north–south part. It forgets the 5 km east.
  • C✗ 25 km is Ahmed’s whole morning drive (5 + 15 + 5), not the gap between them.
  • D✗ 30 km is Wayne’s whole morning drive (5 + 10 + 15).
  • E✗ 35 km retraces Wayne’s route back to Barneyville and on to Carloston (30 + 5). Far from the shortest.
Tip From Barneyville: Carloston is (5 E, 0) and Easterby is (10 E, 10 N). Gap = 5 + 10 = 15 km.
11(Q24 in the original paper) Calendars

A long-running play in London's West End is performed every evening except Sunday1. In addition, there are matinee performances every Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday2.

What is the maximum3 number of performances that can be given during a calendar month of 31 days4?

  1. A36
  2. B38
  3. C39
  4. D41
  5. E42

Reading it properly

  • 16 evening shows a week (Monday to Saturday).
  • 2Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday have 2 shows; Sunday has none.
  • 3We get to choose which day the month starts on.
  • 431 days = 4 full weeks + 3 extra days, and the 3 extra days come in a row.

Working it out

  1. one week: 6 evenings + 3 matinees = 9 shows
    4 full weeks (28 days): 4 × 9 = 36

  2. Shows per day: Mon 1, Tue 1, Wed 2, Thu 2, Fri 1, Sat 2, Sun 0. Find the best 3 days in a row:

    Tue + Wed + Thu = 1 + 2 + 2 = 5
    Wed + Thu + Fri = 2 + 2 + 1 = 5
    Thu + Fri + Sat = 2 + 1 + 2 = 5
    Fri + Sat + Sun = 3, Sat + Sun + Mon = 3 … best is 5
    Trap! Three 2-show days (Wed, Thu, Sat) would give 6, but they are not next to each other: Friday is in the way.

  3. Start the month on a Wednesday, for example:

    36 + 5 = 41
D41: four full weeks give 36, and the best 3 extra days in a row add 5.

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 36 is just the 4 full weeks. It forgets the 3 extra days.
  • B✗ 38 is what you get with the worst extra days (Sun, Mon, Tue: 0 + 1 + 1). We want the maximum.
  • C✗ 39 counts only an evening show on each extra day (36 + 3), missing the matinees.
  • E✗ 42 gives each of the 3 extra days two shows (Wed, Thu, Sat), but those days aren’t in a row.
Tip 4 × 9 = 36. The best 3 days in a row (e.g. Wed–Fri) add 2 + 2 + 1 = 5. Total 41.
12(Q25 in the original paper) Charts & schedules

Peter is having a large conservatory built at the back of his house. Below is a plan of the project, showing when each part of the conservatory is to be built.

Each blocked square on the plan represents one man for one day1.

How many days labour2 will Peter have to pay for, and what is the maximum number of workers on site at the same time3?

  1. A48 and 3
  2. B48 and 4
  3. C58 and 3
  4. D58 and 4
  5. E58 and 6

Reading it properly

  • 1Count squares, not bars. The brickwork bar is 2 squares tall: 2 men every day.
  • 2Total squares = total man-days.
  • 3The tallest pile of squares on any one day.

Working it out

  1. Count each bar: days long × men tall.

    foundations: 6 days × 1 = 6 (days 1–6)
    brickwork: 9 days × 2 = 18 (days 5–13)
    roof: 16 days × 1 = 16 (days 10–25)
    glass: 10 days × 1 = 10 (days 12–21)
    decorate: 8 days × 1 = 8 (days 20–27)
    total = 6 + 18 + 16 + 10 + 8 = 58
    Tip Count along the grid lines, or read the start and end days and do end − start + 1. For the roof, 25 − 10 + 1 = 16.

  2. Now stack the jobs day by day to find the busiest day:

    days 12–13: brickwork 2 + roof 1 + glass 1 = 4
  3. answer = 58 and 4
D58 man-days, with at most 4 workers on site (days 12 and 13).

Why not the others?

  • A✗ 48 is 10 short (as if a whole job like the glass was missed), and 3 forgets the brickwork has 2 men.
  • B✗ The 4 is right, but 48 is 10 squares short of the true total of 58.
  • C✗ The 58 is right, but 3 counts the brickwork as one man. On days 12–13 there are 2 + 1 + 1 = 4.
  • E✗ 6 adds up every row of the chart (1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1), as if every job ran on the same day. They don’t all overlap.
Tip Squares: 6 + 18 + 16 + 10 + 8 = 58. Busiest: brickwork (2) + roof + glass overlap on days 12–13 = 4.

14(Q30 in the original paper) Area

I am resurfacing my driveway using bricks in the pattern shown below. The visible part of the brick measures 10cm by 20cm1. To achieve a rectangular shape, part of some of the bricks must be cut off. The part which is cut off can be used elsewhere on the drive2. The diagram shows that eight bricks would be required to cover an area 40cm by 40cm3:

What is the minimum4 number of bricks needed to resurface a rectangular drive measuring 550cm by 400cm5?

  1. A1080 bricks
  2. B1100 bricks
  3. C1120 bricks
  4. D1127 bricks
  5. E1128 bricks

Reading it properly

  • 1One brick covers 10 × 20 = 200 cm².
  • 2No waste! Every cut-off half gets used somewhere else. So you can just divide the areas.
  • 3Check the idea: 40 × 40 = 1600 cm², and 1600 ÷ 200 = 8. It works.
